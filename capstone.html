The teaching point in the verdict

The bank is green in the Problemfall but yellow in the Extremfall — a direct illustration of M4's lesson that the two scenarios redefine both numerator and denominator, so neither dominates automatically. Here the Extremfall is the binding constraint: the economic-capital view, with its full risk set (liquidity FLVaR, full participation haircut, macro), bites harder than the regulatory view even though the Extremfall Deckungsmasse counts the whole own-funds stack. Because the Extremfall is yellow and Solo utilisation exceeds 90%, the bank owes a Management Summary and must plan remedial measures (§4.5).

5 · Management Summary (the deliverable)

Management Summary — Raiffeisenbank Mustertal eGen, 31.12. (English)

Result. Problemfall utilisation 76,1% (green); Extremfall utilisation 92,5% (yellow / Beobachtungsstatus). The regulatory/normative view is comfortable; the economic (Extremfall) view is the binding constraint and exceeds the 90% appetite threshold.

Drivers. The Extremfall is dominated by credit unexpected loss (€42,0 m, 58% of risk before buffer), of which the FX add-on and the granularity surcharge (HHI 0,05) contribute €4,0 m; participation risk (€9,0 m) and market risk (€6,5 m) are the next-largest. Liquidity (FLVaR €2,2 m) and macro (€5,0 m) appear only in the Extremfall, explaining the green-to-yellow gap versus the Problemfall.

Measures (to restore ≤90%). (1) Reduce single-name corporate concentration to lower the HHI surcharge; (2) hedge/limit the CHF/JPY FX book to cut the FX exposure add-on and the FW-Eigenmittelrisiko; (3) lengthen funding to shrink the over-1y liquidity gap and the FLVaR; (4) build the Deckungsmasse via retained earnings (raise freies Kapital). Target: Extremfall utilisation ≤ 88% by the next two quarter-ends. (5) Großkredite monitoring remains green; no GvK breaches the sector concentration signal.

6 · Viva — defend the methodology

The Aufsichtsrat does not want the answer; it wants to know that you know why. First, the graded multiple-choice defence. Then six open "defend this" prompts with model answers — speak each aloud before revealing.

Open defence prompts (model answers)

1. "Why do you add the risks with correlation +1? Isn't that needlessly punitive?"

Model answer: Addition (ρ=+1) means we claim no diversification benefit across risk types. It is deliberately conservative: it is what lets us defend a 95% Problemfall as adequate even for larger banks, and it sidesteps the hardest-to-estimate, least-stable parameter in any economic-capital model — the inter-risk correlation matrix, which spikes toward 1 in exactly the stress we care about. We do allow ρ<1 where it is empirically well-grounded and within a single risk type: currencies in FX, rating classes in spread, maturity bands in the FLVaR, and Zinsrisiko↔Credit-Spread inside market risk. (M4, M12)

2. "M = 2,5 years and LGD = 45% are just fixed. Defend hard-coding parameters the CRR lets banks estimate."

Model answer: The functional form (Art. 153/154, the 99,9% calibration, R(PD), the maturity adjustment) is regulatory and untouched. What we fix are inputs, under the Proportionalitätsgedanke: estimating M and full A-IRB LGDs per borrower across ~400 small Primärbanken is infeasible and would add model risk we cannot validate. M = 2,5y sets the maturity adjustment to exactly 1 (its normalisation point), so we neither inflate nor deflate; 45% is the regulatory F-IRB unsecured value, and for Corporate/Retail we replaced it with pooled, backtested estimates (45,5 / 54,4 / 55,8%) that are more sector-representative. The trade-off — precision for comparability and auditability across the sector — is exactly what proportionality licenses. (M5, M15)

3. "Where do 0,71 and 1,33 come from, and why is the op-risk factor 5% and not also a simple ratio?"

Model answer: Both are VaR vertical consistency — rescaling a charge calibrated at 99% to our confidence: 0,71 = Φ⁻¹(0,95)/Φ⁻¹(0,99) and 1,33 = Φ⁻¹(0,999)/Φ⁻¹(0,99), under a normal assumption. Op risk is different because its loss distribution is fat-tailed: we model it as ⅓ normal + ⅔ lognormal, so the 95% factor is 15%·(0,33·1,645/3,090 + 0,67·5,180/21,982) = 5,0% — materially below the ~8% a normal-only rescaling would give. The lognormal weight is what captures the rare-but-large operational loss. (M7, M8)

4. "Why is liquidity risk zero in the Problemfall but €2,2 m in the Extremfall?"

Model answer: Short-term liquidity is managed as a limit-and-monitoring discipline under the Leitfaden Liquidität and the LCR/NSFR — it is not a capital charge, so it does not consume the Problemfall Deckungsmasse. The Extremfall is an economic, near-liquidation view, where the structural funding-cost risk does consume economic capital: the FLVaR is the present-value loss of closing the over-1y funding gaps at a stressed Mischzinssatz (99,9%, 250 days), and it only counts when there is a genuine funding need (negative undiversified FLVaR). (M9)

5. "You flagged the Leitfaden's own FX example as wrong. Explain — carefully."

Model answer: §5.1 states an FX-elasticity of the PiT PD of 1,9, but its worked example computes "2% + 273%·25%·2% = 3,005%", which uses 273% and even then doesn't reconcile (273% gives 3,365%; the printed 3,005% implies ≈2,0). The three figures are mutually inconsistent — a documentation slip left from an earlier parameterisation, confirmed by the version history updating the FX elasticity. We apply the governing parameter 1,9: PD_PiT = 2%·(1+1,9·0,25) = 2,95%. We have flagged the erratum to the methodology owners. (M5)

6. "This bank is green Solo. Could the IPS view change that, and why?"

Model answer: Yes. The same bank shows different utilisation Solo vs KI-Gruppe (§30 BWG) vs consolidated IPS, because consolidation changes both sides: on the risk side we eliminate intra-circle participations (our Sektorbeteiligung at the RLB) and intra-group exposures (e.g. the Liquiditätsreserve) to avoid counting the same euro twice; on the cover side we use consolidated own funds. So our Solo participation risk of €9,0 m largely disappears at IPS level, but so does some cover. We must always pair risk and Deckungsmasse on the same scope — never a Solo risk against a consolidated cover. (M12)
