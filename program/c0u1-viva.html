Viva 0.1 — defend the foundations

This is how the real defense feels: a question, then a follow-up that goes straight at the weakest word in your answer. Answer each out loud or in writing first. Only then reveal the model answer and the probe. If the probe catches you, that topic goes in your error log — go remediate before the unit's spaced re-test.

Rules of engagement

I will not accept "because the guide says so", "it's standard", or a number without its assumption. Vagueness is met with a sharper question, not a pass. Aim to survive all six probes. Rate yourself honestly: a probe you couldn't have answered unprompted is a gap, not a win.

B Your boss
Q1. You told the Aufsichtsrat the Problemfall covers a "1-in-20-year" loss. A board member asks: "So we're safe for 20 years?" Answer them.
Hint: what kind of average is 20, and under what assumptions?
A strong answer

"No. Twenty years is the mean waiting time between breaches if utilisation stayed at 100 %, under the idealisation that years are independent and identically distributed. The waiting time is geometric, whose mode is one year — the single most likely breach year is next year. And we don't run at 100 %; we don't target above 90 % utilisation, so realised breaches are rarer than the headline. It's a calibration statement about the confidence level \\(q=95\\%\\) (\\(1/(1-q)=20\\)), not a guarantee."

B Probe
"You said i.i.d. years. Name the single most realistic violation of that, and which direction it biases the 20-year figure."

Expected: serial correlation — a multi-year recession clusters breaches, so conditional on a bad year the next is likelier; the i.i.d. number understates near-term clustering risk. Acceptable also: a drifting/growing portfolio changes \\(p\\) over time.

A Aufsichtsrat
Q2. Your CVA Problemfall number is the regulatory charge times 0,71. Justify the 0,71 from first principles — and tell me exactly what you assumed.
Hint: a ratio of what, over what base, under which distributional assumption?
A strong answer

"For a zero-mean normal loss, \\(\\mathrm{VaR}_q=\\sigma\\Phi^{-1}(q)\\), so the VaR at two confidence levels relates by the ratio of standard-normal quantiles — \\(\\sigma\\) cancels. The regulatory CVA charge sits at the 99 % level; the Problemfall wants 95 %. So the scalar is \\(\\Phi^{-1}(0{,}95)/\\Phi^{-1}(0{,}99)=1{,}6449/2{,}3263=0{,}707\\approx0{,}71\\). Assumptions: the loss is normal and zero-mean, and the charge corresponds to a 99 % quantile — the first two are ÖRS modelling choices, the charge itself is regulatory."

A Probe
"Suppose the underlying loss has a non-zero mean. Show me what breaks in your one line, and whether 0,71 then over- or under-states the rescaled charge."

Expected: with \\(\\mu\\neq0\\) the ratio is \\((\\mu+\\sigma\\Phi^{-1}(q_2))/(\\mu+\\sigma\\Phi^{-1}(q_1))\\); \\(\\mu\\) no longer cancels. For a loss with positive mean (\\(\\mu>0\\)) this ratio is larger than \\(\\Phi^{-1}(q_2)/\\Phi^{-1}(q_1)\\), so 0,71 understates the rescaled charge. Candidate must reason it through, not just assert.

B Your boss
Q3. The op-risk Problemfall factor is 5 %. The internal auditor says "shouldn't a 95 % number be more than half of the 99,9 % one — so closer to 8 %?" Defend the 5 %.
A strong answer

"The auditor is implicitly assuming a symmetric loss, where the 95 % point is \\(1{,}645/3{,}090=53\\%\\) of the 99,9 % point — that does give \\(15\\%\\times0{,}53\\approx8\\%\\). But operational losses are fat-tailed: a few events dominate. For a lognormal severity the 95 % point is only \\(5{,}180/21{,}982=24\\%\\) of the 99,9 % point. We weight the risk ⅓ symmetric, ⅔ fat-tailed, blending to \\(0{,}33\\cdot0{,}53+0{,}67\\cdot0{,}24=0{,}33\\), so \\(15\\%\\times0{,}33=5\\%\\). The auditor's 8 % is the answer to a thin-tailed world we don't live in."

B Probe
"Which single parameter in that blend would I attack to call your 5 % too low — and what's your defense?"

Expected: the \\(\\sigma=1\\) of the lognormal (sets tail heaviness) and/or the ⅔ weight. A bigger \\(\\sigma\\) or weight makes the fat tail heavier and the factor lower; a critic wanting a higher charge would push \\(\\sigma\\) down / weight toward normal. Defense: the choice is deliberately conservative-and-simple given no internal loss data, is backtested at portfolio level, and is superseded by CRR III SMA anyway. Candidate must name \\(\\sigma\\)/weight, not just "the assumptions".

A Aufsichtsrat
Q4. You add all risks together "at correlation +1". That ignores diversification — surely that's wrong and overstates our capital. Defend the choice or concede it.
A strong answer

"It is conservative by design, and that conservatism is a feature. VaR is not generally sub-additive — for fat-tailed or skewed risks the VaR of a combined book can exceed the sum of the parts, so a model that booked a diversification benefit could understate risk in exactly the tail we care about. Adding at +1 is the comonotonic case, where VaR is provably additive; it is an upper bound that can never be accused of inventing diversification VaR cannot guarantee. The cost is overstated capital when risks are genuinely less than perfectly dependent — a price the guide pays knowingly, and which the sector Sicherungseinrichtungen further justify."

A Probe
"You said VaR isn't always sub-additive. Give me the one-sentence condition under which your +1 addition is not conservative but exactly correct."

Expected: comonotonicity — perfect positive dependence among the risks; then \\(\\mathrm{VaR}_q(L+L')=\\mathrm{VaR}_q(L)+\\mathrm{VaR}_q(L')\\) holds with equality. Anything short of that, the addition over-states.

B Your boss
Q5. Quick one, no notes: you claim EL = PD·LGD·EAD is additive across the book. Is that because defaults are independent? Be precise.
A strong answer

"No — it's linearity of expectation, which needs no independence at all: \\(\\mathbb E[\\sum_i L_i]=\\sum_i\\mathbb E[L_i]\\) always holds. Independence matters for the variance and the tail — the unexpected loss — not for the mean. That's exactly why EL is the easy, dependence-free part the guide parks in the Deckungsmasse, while UL, which depends on the correlation structure, needs the whole Vasicek machinery."

B Probe
"Then where, concretely, does dependence first enter the credit number you report?"

Expected: in the unexpected loss — the high quantile of \\(L=\\sum_i L_i\\), via the single systematic factor / asset correlation \\(\\rho\\) in the ASRF / IRB formula (Course 3). EL is untouched by it.

A Aufsichtsrat
Q6 (synthesis). In one minute: of every number you reproduced today — 0,71, 1,33, 5,180, 21,982, 5 % — which is the most regulatory and which is the most ÖRS-discretionary, and why does that distinction matter to this board?
A strong answer

"Most regulatory: the inputs the scalars act on — the CVA and trading-book own-funds charges, and the 15 % op-risk base — are fixed by the CRR/Basel framework. Most discretionary: the op-risk 5 %, because it layers three ÖRS choices on top — the ⅓/⅔ mixture, the \\(\\sigma=1\\) lognormal, and the 95 % target — each of which I can move. The scalars 0,71/1,33 are in between: regulatory charge, ÖRS rescaling. It matters because the board's challenge should land on the discretionary parameters — that's where judgement, and therefore defensibility, lives; the regulatory inputs we take as given. Knowing which is which is how I tell the board where the real model risk is."

A Probe
"Good. Then name the one parameter from today you would put first on a model-risk register, with the reason."

Acceptable: the op-risk mixture (\\(\\sigma\\)/weights) — most assumptions stacked, least empirical support at primary-bank scale, and soon to be superseded by CRR III SMA. A defensible alternative with reasoning is fine; "I don't know" is not.
