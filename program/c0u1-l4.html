L4 — VaR, confidence levels & reproducing the op-risk 5,0 %

Everything from L1–L3 converges here. We reconstruct the guide's operational-risk Problemfall factor — 5,0 % — from a two-distribution mixture, and then take the first hard look at what VaR guarantees, which is what licenses the guide's "Addition = Korrelation +1".

Lecture 4 of 4≈ 3–4 h Outcome: derive 5,0 %; state & use VaR coherence

1 · The op-risk scaling problem, stated cleanly

Primary text · §5.6 (operationelles Risiko)

"Unter der Annahme, dass das operationelle Risiko zu einem Drittel normalverteilt und zu zwei Drittel logarithmisch normalverteilt ist, wird der operationelle Risikofaktor für den Problemfall auf Basis des Extremfalls auf 95 % skaliert … Das operationelle Risiko wird im Problemfall somit mit 5 % der Betriebserträge … festgesetzt."

Extremfall: op-risk \(=15\,\%\) of Betriebserträge (operating income) at 99,9 %. Problemfall: rescale that to 95 % under a ⅓-normal / ⅔-lognormal mixture assumption.

The building blocks are all in hand:

  • The Extremfall charge is \(15\,\% \times\) Betriebserträge — a regulatory-anchored figure: the Basisindikatoransatz uses 15 %, and the Standardansatz's business-line weights (12–18 %) average ≈15 % too (§5.6).
  • To move from 99,9 % to 95 % we rescale by a quantile ratio — but which distribution's ratio? A single charge has to stand in for many heterogeneous op-loss events: lots of small, near-symmetric losses, plus a few catastrophic, fat-tailed ones.

2 · Derivation — the mixture rescaling

Model the op-loss as a mixture: with weight \(w_N=\tfrac13\) it behaves normally (the bulk of frequent, symmetric losses), and with weight \(w_L=\tfrac23\) lognormally (the heavy-tailed catastrophic part). Rescaling a charge from level \(q_1=99{,}9\,\%\) to \(q_2=95\,\%\) multiplies each component by its own quantile ratio (L2 for the normal, L3 for the lognormal, both with \(\mu=0,\sigma=1\)):

$$ s \;=\; w_N\,\frac{\Phi^{-1}(0{,}95)}{\Phi^{-1}(0{,}999)} \;+\; w_L\,\frac{e^{\Phi^{-1}(0{,}95)}}{e^{\Phi^{-1}(0{,}999)}} \;=\; \tfrac13\cdot\frac{1{,}645}{3{,}090} \;+\; \tfrac23\cdot\frac{5{,}180}{21{,}982}. $$

Evaluate each piece:

$$ \tfrac13\cdot\frac{1{,}645}{3{,}090}=0{,}33\cdot0{,}5324=0{,}17569,\qquad \tfrac23\cdot\frac{5{,}180}{21{,}982}=0{,}67\cdot0{,}23565=0{,}15789, $$ $$ s=0{,}17569+0{,}15789=0{,}33358. $$

Apply to the 15 % Extremfall charge:

$$ \boxed{\ \text{op-risk}_{\text{Problemfall}} = 15\,\% \times 0{,}33358 = 5{,}00\,\%\ \text{of Betriebserträge.}\ } $$

Reproduced to 5,0037 % — the guide's 5,0 %. The deep point: the heavy lognormal component (weight ⅔) shrinks far less aggressively than the normal would (0,236 vs 0,532, L3), so the blended factor stays well above the 0,532 a pure-normal model would give. The fat tail is doing real work in keeping the Problemfall charge from collapsing.

Reg vs ÖRS — line by line
  • regulatory-anchored the 15 % base (BIA / averaged Standardansatz).
  • ÖRS choice the ⅓ / ⅔ mixture weights; the \(\sigma=1\) standardisation of both components; the decision to rescale by quantile ratios at all (i.e. the normal/lognormal-VaR scaling assumption); and the 95 % / 99,9 % scenario levels.
  • What it trades off: a transparent, reproducible, conservative-but-crude proxy for a real op-loss distribution the bank cannot estimate at primary-bank scale. It deliberately avoids an AMA-style internal model (data-hungry) — and CRR III's SMA supersedes the whole BIA logic anyway (flagged for Course 6).
R the op-risk Problemfall factor, end to end 
wN <- 1/3; wL <- 2/3            # mixture weights (ÖRS choice)
qn <- qnorm(c(0.95, 0.999))    # 1.6449 , 3.0902

norm_part <- wN * qn[1]/qn[2]           # 0.17746  (using exact qnorm)
logn_part <- wL * exp(qn[1])/exp(qn[2]) # 0.15715
s <- norm_part + logn_part              # 0.33461
0.15 * s                                # 0.05019 -> 5.0%

# exactly the guide's print, with its rounded inputs 0.33/0.67, 1.645/3.090, 5.180/21.982
0.15 * (0.33*1.645/3.090 + 0.67*5.180/21.982)   # 0.050035 -> 5.0%
⚠ Trap — exact qnorm vs the guide's rounded constants

Using R's exact qnorm and weights \(\tfrac13,\tfrac23\) gives \(5{,}02\,\%\); using the guide's rounded \(1{,}645/3{,}090\), \(5{,}180/21{,}982\), \(0{,}33/0{,}67\) gives \(5{,}00\,\%\). Both round to 5,0 %. In a defense, show you can hit the number their way and note the rounding — don't let a reviewer catch a 0,02 pp "discrepancy" you didn't pre-empt.

3 · Stepping back — VaR as a risk measure, and what it guarantees

You have now used VaR as a quantile three times. Before Course 2 leans on it, fix what VaR does and does not promise. A risk measure \(\rho\) is coherent (Artzner et al.) if it satisfies, for losses \(L,L'\):

AxiomStatementVaR?
Monotonicity\(L\le L'\Rightarrow \rho(L)\le\rho(L')\)yes
Translation inv.\(\rho(L+c)=\rho(L)+c\)yes
Positive homog.\(\rho(\lambda L)=\lambda\rho(L),\ \lambda\ge0\)yes
Sub-additivity\(\rho(L+L')\le\rho(L)+\rho(L')\)not always

VaR can violate sub-additivity: occasionally the VaR of a merged portfolio exceeds the sum of the parts' VaRs (diversification appears to increase risk — a genuine defect, sharpest for fat-tailed or skewed losses). The full counterexample and the Expected-Shortfall fix are Course 2; here just hold the fact.

Why this licenses the guide's "Addition = Korrelation +1" (§5/§7)

The Leitfaden aggregates all material single risks by simple addition, stating this "entspricht einer Korrelation von +1". Two things make that defensible, and you now have the language for both: (i) under comonotonicity (perfect positive dependence) VaR is additive — \(\mathrm{VaR}_q(L+L')=\mathrm{VaR}_q(L)+\mathrm{VaR}_q(L')\) — so addition is the comonotonic case, the most conservative dependence; (ii) because addition assumes no diversification benefit, it is an upper bound that side-steps VaR's sub-additivity defect entirely — you can never be accused of booking a diversification benefit that VaR cannot guarantee. The cost is conservatism (capital is overstated when risks are not in fact comonotonic). That trade — ÖRS choice conservatism over realism — is a central viva theme.

Self-check 4.1 — Recompute the op-risk factor if op-loss were assumed purely normal (no lognormal part). Why does the guide not do this?
Show answer
Pure normal: \(15\,\%\times(1{,}645/3{,}090)=15\,\%\times0{,}532=7{,}99\,\%\approx8\,\%\). That is much larger than 5 % — a symmetric model rescales less aggressively because its tail is thin, so more of the 99,9 % charge "survives" down to 95 %. The guide's ⅔ lognormal weight pulls the factor down to 5 % by recognising that the bulk of op-risk severity is fat-tailed, where the 95 % quantile is a much smaller fraction of the 99,9 % quantile. (This is also the crisp answer to "why 5 % not 8 %?" — the teach-back task.)
Self-check 4.2 — Is adding risks at correlation +1 conservative or anti-conservative? State the one condition under which it is exactly right.
Show answer
Conservative (it overstates risk) whenever the true dependence is less than comonotonic — i.e. almost always. It is exactly right only under comonotonicity (perfect positive dependence), where VaR is genuinely additive. So the guide trades realism for an unimpeachable upper bound.

Recap — the unit in one breath

  • The op-risk Problemfall factor is derived, not assumed: \(15\,\%\times[\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac{1{,}645}{3{,}090}+\tfrac23\cdot\tfrac{5{,}180}{21{,}982}]=5{,}0\,\%\), blending a thin-tailed and a fat-tailed rescaling.
  • VaR is monotone, translation-invariant and homogeneous but not always sub-additive; under comonotonicity it is additive.
  • That is exactly why the guide's +1 correlation addition is a defensible, conservative upper bound — the headline aggregation choice of the whole RTFA, now grounded.
🇦🇹 Zusammenfassung (Deutsch)
  • Der operationelle Risikofaktor im Problemfall ist hergeleitet: \(15\,\%\times[\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac{1{,}645}{3{,}090}+\tfrac23\cdot\tfrac{5{,}180}{21{,}982}]=5{,}0\,\%\) — eine Mischung aus dünn- und fettschwänziger Skalierung.
  • Der VaR ist monoton, translations­invariant und homogen, aber nicht stets sub-additiv; bei Komonotonie ist er additiv.
  • Deshalb ist die Addition mit Korrelation +1 des Leitfadens eine zulässige, konservative obere Schranke — die zentrale Aggregationsentscheidung der RTFA.
