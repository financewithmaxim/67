L2 — Distributions, the quantile function & the standard normal

The confidence level is a quantile; the quantile of the normal is \(\Phi^{-1}\). Today we make both precise and cash them in: the Leitfaden's CVA scalars 0,71 and 1,33 fall straight out.

Lecture 2 of 4≈ 2.5–3.5 h Outcome: \(\mathrm{VaR}_q=\mu+\sigma\Phi^{-1}(q)\); reproduce 0,71 / 1,33

1 · The quantile function — the right inverse of \(F\)

A CDF \(F\) can be flat or jump, so it need not have an ordinary inverse. The object we need is the generalised inverse (quantile function):

$$ F^{-1}(q)\;=\;\inf\{x\in\mathbb R: F(x)\ge q\},\qquad q\in(0,1). $$
read: the smallest loss level whose probability of not being exceeded is at least \(q\).

When \(F\) is continuous and strictly increasing (our normal/lognormal cases), \(F^{-1}\) is the plain inverse and \(F(F^{-1}(q))=q\). The key identity we will use repeatedly:

$$ \mathbb P\big(X\le F^{-1}(q)\big)=q \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathbb P\big(X> F^{-1}(q)\big)=1-q. $$

Define Value-at-Risk at level \(q\) as exactly this quantile of the loss:

$$ \boxed{\;\mathrm{VaR}_q(L)=F_L^{-1}(q)\;} $$

This closes the loop with L1: \(\mathbb P(L>\mathrm{VaR}_q)=1-q\), which is what the "1-in-\(1/(1-q)\)" frequency rested on. (The deeper properties of VaR as a risk measure — monotonicity, translation, homogeneity, and the failure of sub-additivity — are L4 and Course 2.)

2 · The standard normal \(\Phi\) and its inverse

The standard normal has density and CDF

$$ \varphi(z)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-z^2/2},\qquad \Phi(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{z}\varphi(u)\,du. $$

\(\Phi\) is continuous and strictly increasing, so \(\Phi^{-1}\) (the "probit") exists outright. Two facts do all the lifting:

  • Symmetry: \(\varphi\) is even \(\Rightarrow \Phi(-z)=1-\Phi(z)\Rightarrow \Phi^{-1}(1-q)=-\Phi^{-1}(q)\). (This is why a 95 % upside quantile and a 5 % downside quantile are mirror images — it underlies the market-risk "percentile of the confidence interval" in §5.5.)
  • Location–scale: if \(Z\sim N(0,1)\) then \(X=\mu+\sigma Z\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)\), and since \(x\mapsto\mu+\sigma x\) is increasing for \(\sigma>0\), quantiles transform the same way.

3 · Derivation — VaR of a normal loss, and the scaling identity

Let \(L\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)\). Using location–scale on the quantile:

$$ \mathrm{VaR}_q(L)=F_L^{-1}(q)=\mu+\sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(q). $$
derivation: \(F_L(x)=\Phi\!\big(\tfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\big)\); set \(=q\) and solve \(\Rightarrow x=\mu+\sigma\Phi^{-1}(q)\).

Now the crucial corollary. For a zero-mean normal loss (\(\mu=0\)), the VaR at two confidence levels relates by a pure quantile ratio, with \(\sigma\) cancelling:

$$ \frac{\mathrm{VaR}_{q_2}(L)}{\mathrm{VaR}_{q_1}(L)}=\frac{\sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(q_2)}{\sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(q_1)}=\frac{\Phi^{-1}(q_2)}{\Phi^{-1}(q_1)}. $$
this is the engine behind every "Skalierung des Eigenmittelerfordernisses unter Annahme des Normalverteilungsansatzes" in the guide.
What is regulatory, what is ÖRS, in this one identity

regulatory The CVA own-funds charge itself (CRR Title VI; the guide's PosNr 7516000 at 8 % EM-weight) and the trading-book own-funds charge. ÖRS choice (i) the assumption that the underlying loss is normal and zero-mean so VaR scales linearly with \(\Phi^{-1}\); (ii) the reading that the regulatory charge corresponds to a 99 % quantile (the scaling denominator); (iii) the target levels 95 % / 99,9 % for the two scenarios. The number you defend is regulatory at its base but ÖRS in its rescaling — say exactly that.

4 · Worked example — reproducing the CVA scalars (§5.2)

Primary text · §5.2 (CVA Risiko)

"CVA Risiko im Problemfall = CVA Eigenmittelerfordernis · 0,71 … Der Faktor 0,71 ergibt sich durch das unterschiedliche Konfidenzintervall, d.h. 1,6449 (95,0 %) dividiert durch 2,3263 (99 %)."
"CVA Risiko im Extremfall = CVA Eigenmittelerfordernis · 1,33 … 3,0902 (99,9 %) dividiert durch 2,3263 (99 %)."

Apply the scaling identity with denominator \(q_1=99\,\%\):

$$ \text{Problemfall: } \frac{\Phi^{-1}(0{,}95)}{\Phi^{-1}(0{,}99)}=\frac{1{,}6449}{2{,}3263}=0{,}7070\approx \mathbf{0{,}71}. $$ $$ \text{Extremfall: } \frac{\Phi^{-1}(0{,}999)}{\Phi^{-1}(0{,}99)}=\frac{3{,}0902}{2{,}3263}=1{,}3284\approx \mathbf{1{,}33}. $$

Both reproduced. The same two scalars reappear verbatim for the trading-book price risk (§5.5: "Preisrisiko HB · 0,71" / "· 1,33") — identical logic, identical assumption. Find them once, defend them everywhere.

⚠ Trap — the scaling silently assumes \(\mu=0\)

If the loss has a non-zero mean, \(\mathrm{VaR}_{q_2}/\mathrm{VaR}_{q_1}=(\mu+\sigma\Phi^{-1}(q_2))/(\mu+\sigma\Phi^{-1}(q_1))\neq\Phi^{-1}(q_2)/\Phi^{-1}(q_1)\). For a one-year market/CVA charge the zero-mean (or mean-folded-into-the-charge) assumption is defensible; for anything with material drift it is not. The viva will ask "what if the mean isn't zero?" — have this ready.

R the quantiles and the scalars 
# the three governing quantiles
qnorm(c(0.95, 0.99, 0.999))        # 1.644854 2.326348 3.090232

# CVA / trading-book scalars = quantile ratios with 99% base
prob <- qnorm(0.95 ) / qnorm(0.99) # 0.7070  -> 0.71
extr <- qnorm(0.999) / qnorm(0.99) # 1.3284  -> 1.33
round(c(Problemfall = prob, Extremfall = extr), 4)

# symmetry check: Phi^{-1}(1-q) = -Phi^{-1}(q)
all.equal(qnorm(0.05), -qnorm(0.95))   # TRUE

5 · In practice — the data path

Input → source
  • CVA Eigenmittelerfordernis: aufsichtsrechtliche Meldung, Gesamtbetrag der Risikoposition für Anpassung der Kreditbewertung, PosNr 7516000, times the 8 % EM-weight (§5.2, fn 24). You read the charge; you multiply by 0,71 / 1,33.
  • Trading-book price risk: own-funds charge for position/FX/commodity risk minus the FX own-funds charge (§5.5) — the subtraction avoids double-counting FX, which is captured separately in the offene Devisenposition.
  • The scalars themselves are constants — no data, but they are derived constants you must be able to regenerate, which is the point of this lecture.

Self-check 2.2 — Why is the denominator 99 % and not, say, 99,9 %?
Show answer
Because the regulatory charge being rescaled is taken to correspond to a 99 % quantile (an ÖRS reading of the regulatory CVA methodology). The denominator must be the confidence level of the quantity you start from; the numerator is the level you want. Change the base and every scalar changes.

Recap

  • VaR is a quantile: \(\mathrm{VaR}_q(L)=F_L^{-1}(q)\); for \(L\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)\), \(\mathrm{VaR}_q=\mu+\sigma\Phi^{-1}(q)\).
  • For a zero-mean normal, VaR ratios are pure quantile ratios — \(\sigma\) cancels. This is the entire content of the guide's CVA / trading-book scalings.
  • Reproduced: \(0{,}71=1{,}6449/2{,}3263\) and \(1{,}33=3{,}0902/2{,}3263\). Regulatory at the base (the charge), ÖRS in the normality + zero-mean + 99 %-base assumptions.
