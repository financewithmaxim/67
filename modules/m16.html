Module 16 — Basel IV / CRR III & CRD VI: what changes and what it means for the sector and this guide

The Leitfaden Früherkennung (09/2024) scales regulatory own-funds charges — for op risk, CVA, and the trading book — and uses IRB inputs for credit risk. "Basel IV" (in the EU: CRR III + CRD VI) rewrites several of those very charges. This module explains what changes, why, and which numbers in the guide will have to move in the next update wave.

1 · Motivation — why "Basel IV" exists

After the 2008 crisis, supervisors discovered that two banks with identical portfolios could report very different risk-weighted assets (RWAs) — purely because their internal models (IRB for credit, IMA for market risk, AMA for op risk) calibrated differently. This unwarranted RWA variability corroded the credibility of capital ratios: a 14% CET1 ratio meant one thing at a conservative bank and another at an aggressive one. The Basel Committee's December 2017 package — informally "Basel IV", formally the finalisation of Basel III — set out to restore comparability and trust in capital ratios by floored, more granular, and less model-dependent measurement.

▶ One sentence to remember

Basel IV does not (mainly) raise required capital by changing buffer percentages — it changes how RWAs are measured, putting floors under model outputs so that internal models can no longer drift arbitrarily far below the standardised result. The headline instrument is the Output Floor.

2 · Concept & logic — the framing and the headline

2.1 What "Basel IV" is, in EU terms

"Basel IV" is not a new accord — it is the 2017 finalisation of Basel III. In the EU it is transposed by two instruments you must name correctly:

  • CRR III — Capital Requirements Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2024/1623 verify. Directly applicable (a Regulation), it carries the measurement rules: revised SA/IRB credit risk, FRTB, SA-CCR, revised CVA, the new op-risk SMA, and the output floor.
  • CRD VI — Capital Requirements Directive verify. A Directive (needs national transposition into BWG), it carries supervisory/governance items: buffers, fit-and-proper, ESG/third-country-branch rules, supervisory powers.

Core motivation, restated: reduce unwarranted RWA variability from internal models and restore the comparability and credibility of capital ratios.

2.2 The Output Floor — the headline change

The single most consequential rule. Aggregate RWAs computed under internal models must be at least 72,5% verify rate of the RWAs that the same exposures would attract under the standardised approaches (SA):

$$ RWA_{\text{floored}} \;=\; \max\!\Big(\, RWA_{\text{internal models}},\;\; 0{,}725\times RWA_{\text{standardised}} \,\Big) $$
\(RWA_{\text{internal models}}\) = total RWA the bank gets from all its model-based approaches (IRB credit, IMA market, etc.); \(RWA_{\text{standardised}}\) = the RWA the same book would attract under the standardised approaches. The floor is aggregate (one number for the whole bank), not loan-by-loan. The factor phases in, rising annually to 72,5% around 2030 verify timeline.
What the floor actually limits

It caps the benefit a bank may extract from internal models relative to the standardised result. If your models produce RWAs below 72,5% of the SA number, the difference is added back: your effective RWA becomes the floor. A model can still save you up to 27,5% of SA RWAs — no more.

Why it matters even though most Raiffeisenbanken are SA banks. A local Raiffeisenbank that already uses the standardised approach is, in isolation, not bound by an output floor — for an SA bank the "internal" and "standardised" RWAs coincide, so the floor is non-binding at solo level. But the floor bites at the level of the IRB user in the group — RBI (Raiffeisen Bank International), and through consolidation it tightens the sector's consolidated capital. Because the Früherkennung produces both unconsolidated and R-IPS-consolidated views (Module on aggregation/consolidation), a floor that lifts RBI's RWAs feeds straight into the consolidated Auslastung the sector monitors. verify timeline

3 · Structure — the components of CRR III

CRR III rewrites measurement across risk types. Here is the structure, each item tagged where a date/rate is involved and linked to the Leitfaden module it touches.

3.1 Revised credit-risk SA and IRB

  • More granular standardised risk weights: real-estate exposures split into LTV (loan-to-value) buckets; finer treatment of unrated corporates and retail. verify weights
  • IRB input floors: regulatory minimum PD and LGD inputs so model inputs cannot be set implausibly low. verify floors
  • Scope limits: A-IRB (advanced IRB) no longer permitted for large corporates and financial institutions — those move to F-IRB (foundation IRB) or to SA. Equity exposures move off IRB. verify scope
  • Removal of the 1,06 IRB scaling factor.
🟣 Connect to Module 5 (the IRB formula)

The Vasicek/ASRF formula you derived in M5 — \(K=\big[LGD\cdot PD_{\text{cond}}(q)-LGD\cdot PD\big]\cdot\text{(maturity adj.)}\) — stays. What changes is two-fold: (1) its inputs get floors (PD and LGD can no longer be driven below regulatory minima), and (2) the output floor caps the whole IRB benefit at the portfolio level. So the formula the Leitfaden leans on is unchanged in shape but constrained at both ends — input floors below, output floor above.

3.2 FRTB — Fundamental Review of the Trading Book

A wholly new market-risk framework replacing the current own-funds market-risk charge. It comprises a revised standardised approach (a sensitivities-based method, SBM) and an internal models approach with P&L attribution tests and explicit treatment of non-modellable risk factors (NMRF).

⚠ FRTB sits under the Leitfaden's trading-book scalars (M6)

The Leitfaden's Preisrisiko Handelsbuch charge = own-funds market-risk charge (minus FX, to avoid double count), then scaled ×0,71 (Problemfall) / ×1,33 (Extremfall). FRTB changes the charge those scalars multiply. The scaling logic (quantile rescaling, M4) survives; the base number under it is replaced. In the EU, FRTB own-funds reporting/application was deferred (Commission delegated deferral) — so the timing of when this base actually moves is itself uncertain. verify deferral

3.3 SA-CCR — standardised counterparty credit risk

The standardised approach for counterparty credit risk replaces the older Current Exposure Method (CEM) / standardised method for computing the exposure of derivatives. SA-CCR feeds downstream calculations — notably CVA and large exposures (Großkredite) — so it is upstream of several Leitfaden inputs even though it has no scalar of its own.

3.4 Revised CVA — BA-CVA and SA-CVA

The current CVA (credit valuation adjustment) framework is replaced by two new approaches: BA-CVA (basic) and SA-CVA (standardised, sensitivities-based), the latter aligned with FRTB's sensitivities machinery.

⚠ This supersedes the base of the Leitfaden's CVA charge (M7)

The Leitfaden takes the regulatory CVA Eigenmittelerfordernis (own-funds requirement, PosNr 7516000, EM-weight 8%) and scales it ×0,71 / ×1,33. CRR III revises the very own-funds charge being scaled: BA-CVA/SA-CVA produce a different — and more risk-sensitive — CVA number than today's framework. The Leitfaden's "8% · CVA-Position × 0,71/1,33" therefore sits on a charge CRR III replaces. Flag explicitly: the scalars stay, the base is superseded.

3.5 New op-risk SMA — Standardised Measurement Approach

CRR III replaces all three current op-risk approaches — BIA (Basisindikatoransatz), TSA (standardised), and AMA (advanced) — with a single Standardised Measurement Approach (SMA):

$$ \text{Op-risk capital} \;=\; \underbrace{BIC}_{\text{from Business Indicator}} \times \underbrace{ILM}_{\text{Internal Loss Multiplier}} $$
BI (Business Indicator) = a P&L-based size proxy (interest, services, financial components); BIC (Business Indicator Component) = the BI passed through a progressive marginal-coefficient schedule (buckets); ILM = a multiplier built from the bank's own historical operational losses. In the EU, ILM is set to 1 (a national/Union discretion), so the charge is essentially BIC-based and loss history does not (yet) scale it. verify ILM discretion
⚠ This supersedes the Leitfaden's income-based op-risk (M8)

The Leitfaden anchors op risk on 15% of Betriebserträge (Extremfall, ≈ Basisindikatoransatz) and derives 5,0% for the Problemfall by rescaling 15% from 99,9% to 95% under a ⅓-normal/⅔-lognormal assumption. CRR III's SMA replaces the BIA-style 15% basis with the BIC schedule. The 15%/5% machinery is therefore built on a base CRR III supersedes — the Leitfaden's op-risk basis will need to be re-anchored on BIC. verify

The bottom line for the ÖRS

The ÖRS will need to update the Früherkennung's op-risk and CVA bases — the two clearest supersessions — and watch the FRTB base and the output floor's consolidated effect. The Leitfaden's version history shows it updates regularly (recall the FX-elasticity revision flagged in M5); CRR III is the next update wave. The scaling architecture (quantile rescaling to 95% / 99,9%) is robust to all of this — it is the underlying regulatory charges that move.

4.1 Worked example — the Output Floor, numerically

Worked example — an IRB bank hitting the floor

An IRB bank computes, for the same book, two RWA totals:

  • RWA under the standardised approaches: \(RWA_{\text{SA}} = \) €1.000m
  • RWA under its internal models: \(RWA_{\text{IRB}} = \) €650m (models save 35% vs SA)

Step 1 — compute the floor at the fully phased-in 72,5%:

$$ 0{,}725 \times 1.000\text{m} \;=\; 725\text{m}. $$

Step 2 — apply the floor (take the max of model RWA and the floor):

$$ RWA_{\text{floored}} = \max(650,\; 725) = \textbf{725m}. $$

Step 3 — read the bite. The floor is binding (725 > 650). RWAs rise from €650m to €725m, a

$$ \frac{725-650}{650} = \frac{75}{650} = \textbf{+11{,}5\%} \text{ RWA increase.} $$

For a fixed capital base, an +11,5% RWA increase lowers the bank's capital ratio by the same proportion — the floor "claws back" part of the model benefit. A bank whose models were less aggressive (say \(RWA_{\text{IRB}}=\)€800m > €725m) would be unaffected: the floor only bites when models drop below 72,5% of SA.

4.2 Implementation timeline

EU application of most of CRR III from 1 January 2025 verify against EUR-Lex, with two notable carve-outs: the FRTB own-funds requirement / reporting was deferred by the Commission verify deferral, and the output-floor factor phases in, rising annually to 72,5% around 2030 verify phase-in. CRD VI requires national transposition (into the BWG) on its own schedule. Confirm all of this against EUR-Lex, EBA, and FMA before relying on any specific date.

5 · Structure recap — the Leitfaden's place in the new world

Keep the layering straight. The Leitfaden does three things to regulatory numbers, and CRR III touches the inputs to all three:

  • It computes credit-risk UL via the IRB formula → CRR III floors the inputs and caps the output.
  • It scales own-funds charges (CVA, trading book) by 0,71 / 1,33 → CRR III replaces the charges being scaled (revised CVA, FRTB).
  • It anchors op risk on income (15% / 5%) → CRR III replaces the basis with BIC.

6 · Nuances & traps

⚠ The output floor is on RWAs, not on capital directly

The floor sets \(RWA_{\text{floored}}=\max(RWA_{\text{IM}},\,0{,}725\,RWA_{\text{SA}})\). It does not directly add a capital requirement; it raises the RWA denominator, which lowers the capital ratio for a fixed numerator. Confusing "a floor on RWAs" with "a floor on capital" leads to wrong impact arithmetic.

⚠ SA banks feel the floor mainly via consolidation at RBI

A standardised-approach Raiffeisenbank is not bound by the output floor solo (its IM and SA RWAs coincide). The floor's real bite is at the IRB user — RBI — and it reaches the sector through R-IPS consolidation. Don't conclude "we're SA, so the floor is irrelevant to us": the consolidated Früherkennung view inherits RBI's floored RWAs.

⚠ CRR III revises the very bases the Leitfaden scales

The 0,71 / 1,33 trading-book and CVA scalars, and the 5% / 15% op-risk factors, will ride on new numbers: FRTB replaces the market-risk charge, BA-CVA/SA-CVA replace the CVA charge, and the SMA's BIC replaces the income basis. The Leitfaden's scaling logic is untouched; its inputs are superseded. Treating the old base as permanent is the core error this module guards against.

⚠ ILM = 1 in the EU — op risk is BIC-based, not loss-scaled (for now)

The SMA's Internal Loss Multiplier is set to 1 under EU discretion, so historical operational losses do not currently scale the charge. Assuming the EU applies a loss-sensitive ILM today overstates how loss-driven the EU op-risk charge is. verify discretion

⚠ Every date and rate here must be verified

72,5%, 1 January 2025, the ~2030 phase-in, the FRTB deferral, and the regulation number 2024/1623 are stated as adopted/expected. They post-date the cutoff and are subject to Commission deferrals, level-2 measures, and national transposition.
