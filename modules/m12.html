Module 12 — Risikoaggregation (§7): addition as correlation +1, and the IPS consolidation adjustments

You have measured every single risk in Modules 5–11. Now you must turn the list into one Gesamtrisiko (total risk) and set it against one Deckungsmasse (risk-cover mass) — on the right scope. This module derives why the Leitfaden simply adds the single risks (it is a deliberate correlation-of-+1 assumption, the most conservative choice), then builds the heart of §7: the IPS consolidation adjustments that stop the Raiffeisen sector from counting the same euro of risk twice across Primärbank, Landeszentrale and RBI.

By the end of this module you can…

  • Derive why pure addition of VaRs equals an assumed correlation of \(\rho=+1\), and why that is the most conservative aggregation.
  • Name the only places the Leitfaden allows \(\rho<1\) (intra-market-risk), and explain why this conservatism underwrites the simple 95%.
  • Apply the IPS consolidation adjustments: consolidated own funds on the cover side, and elimination of intra-circle Beteiligungsrisiken and Intra-Group credit exposures on the risk side.
  • Explain why the same bank shows a different Auslastung (utilisation) under Solo, KI-Gruppe (§30 BWG) and IPS, and never pair a Solo risk with a consolidated cover.
  • Connect the IPS risk-side eliminations to the CRR's CET1 cross-holding deductions (Module 2) as two faces of the same anti-double-counting principle.

1 · Motivation — why aggregation is its own problem

Each Module 5–11 produced a number: credit-risk UL, market-risk VaR, operational risk, Beteiligungsrisiko, Länderrisiko, the 5% Puffer, and so on. Senior management and the Aufsichtsrat do not want eleven numbers; they want one Gesamtrisikobetrag and one traffic light. Two non-trivial questions stand between you and that single number:

  • How do the risks combine? Risks are not perfectly aligned in reality — a credit downturn and an FX shock need not peak in the same instant — so naïvely adding them overstates the joint loss. The honest aggregation needs a dependence structure (a correlation matrix, or a copula). The Leitfaden makes a deliberate, defensible shortcut here (§2–§3).
  • On what scope? A single Raiffeisenbank can be looked at Solo, inside a §30 BWG KI-Gruppe (prudential group), or as one cell of the R-IPS. Inside the sector, banks own and lend to each other. Add the solo risks of all members and you count the same underlying exposure several times. The consolidated IPS view must strip those intra-sector links out — that is §7's real content (§5).
▶ The one sentence to hold onto

Aggregation makes the total bigger (addition = no diversification benefit), while consolidation makes the total smaller (intra-group eliminations remove double counts). They pull in opposite directions and operate on different axes — dependence vs scope. Keeping them apart is half of this module.

2 · Concept & logic — addition is a modelling choice about dependence

The Leitfaden's aggregation rule is blunt: Gesamtrisiko = the sum of all material single risks ÖRS choice. No inter-risk-type diversification benefit is granted. This is not laziness and it is not an accidentally missing diversification benefit — it is an explicit assumption that all risk types are perfectly positively correlated (\(\rho=+1\)), which is the most conservative possible aggregation. We derive that equivalence in §3.

The few exceptions are tightly bounded. The only correlations below 1 the Leitfaden ever uses live inside a single risk type, never between types:

🟣 The rule, stated precisely

ÖRS choice Across risk types: \(\rho=+1\) (pure addition). Within market and funding-liquidity sub-risks only: a correlation matrix with \(\rho<1\) is used (each of these still produces a single risk-type number, which then enters the cross-type addition at \(\rho=+1\)). Everything else — Beteiligungsrisiko, operational risk, Länderrisiko, the 5% Puffer — is added with no diversification at all. Beteiligungsrisiko explicitly has no internal diversification (M11): single risks summed.

3 · Derivation — why addition is correlation +1

Take two risk types with stand-alone VaRs \(\mathrm{VaR}_A\) and \(\mathrm{VaR}_B\). Under the variance–covariance (parametric) view, where each risk is a centred normal loss and the portfolio loss is their sum, the combined VaR obeys the same algebra as the standard deviation of a sum of two correlated variables:

$$ \mathrm{VaR}_P \;=\; \sqrt{\,\mathrm{VaR}_A^{2} \;+\; \mathrm{VaR}_B^{2} \;+\; 2\,\rho\,\mathrm{VaR}_A\,\mathrm{VaR}_B\,} $$
where \(\rho\in[-1,1]\) is the correlation between the two loss sources; \(\mathrm{VaR}_A,\mathrm{VaR}_B\ge 0\). This is exact when losses are jointly elliptical (e.g. multivariate normal) and the same quantile scaling applies to each — the regime in which VaR is sub-additive and the matrix form is valid.

Now set \(\rho=1\). The radicand becomes a perfect square:

$$ \mathrm{VaR}_P\big|_{\rho=1} \;=\; \sqrt{\,\mathrm{VaR}_A^{2}+\mathrm{VaR}_B^{2}+2\,\mathrm{VaR}_A\,\mathrm{VaR}_B\,} \;=\; \sqrt{\,(\mathrm{VaR}_A+\mathrm{VaR}_B)^{2}\,} \;=\; \mathrm{VaR}_A+\mathrm{VaR}_B. $$
At \(\rho=1\) the square root collapses exactly to the arithmetic sum. So "add the single risks" and "assume the risk types are perfectly positively correlated" are the same statement. By induction the same holds for \(n\) risks: \(\sum_i \mathrm{VaR}_i\) is the \(\rho_{ij}=1\;\forall i,j\) corner of the quadratic form \(\sqrt{\mathbf v^{\top}\mathbf C\,\mathbf v}\) with \(\mathbf v\) the vector of VaRs and \(\mathbf C\) the all-ones matrix.

For any \(\rho<1\), the cross term shrinks and \(\mathrm{VaR}_P<\mathrm{VaR}_A+\mathrm{VaR}_B\). That shortfall is the diversification benefit — the \(\sqrt{\text{sum of squares}}\)-style shrinkage. At the opposite extreme \(\rho=0\) you get \(\mathrm{VaR}_P=\sqrt{\mathrm{VaR}_A^2+\mathrm{VaR}_B^2}\) (independent risks), strictly below the sum. Therefore:

Addition is the upper bound of all sub-additive aggregations

For elliptical losses, \(\mathrm{VaR}_P(\rho)\) is monotone increasing in \(\rho\), so \(\mathrm{VaR}_A+\mathrm{VaR}_B\) is the maximum over all \(\rho\in[-1,1]\). Pure addition is the single most conservative aggregation — it grants the bank zero diversification credit and assumes every risk type peaks together. Choosing it is choosing prudence over realism, on purpose.

🟣 Why this conservatism is what makes the simple 95% defensible

ÖRS choice The Problemfall runs at a relatively low confidence (95%) for tractability and comparability across hundreds of banks (Module 4). A low quantile alone could look soft. But layering the most conservative aggregation (\(\rho=+1\), no diversification) on top of the 95% systematically pushes the aggregate risk up, offsetting the mildness of the quantile. The conservatism of the aggregation is precisely the cushion that lets the ÖRS defend a simple 95% normative view to the FMA/OeNB and the Aufsichtsrat: "we take no diversification credit anywhere across risk types, so our total is an upper bound." Cross-link Module 4 — vertical/horizontal VaR consistency plus +1 aggregation together make the simplification supervisorily safe.

3.1 The Masterskala presentation

So that risks are comparable across Forderungsklassen (exposure classes) and across institutions, the Leitfaden presents risk on a Masterskala (master scale) — the same 10-step rating-grade scale that carries the midpoint PDs in Module 5 (0,5 → … → 5,x). Mapping every Forderungsklasse's risk onto one ordinal scale is what lets the Aufsichtsrat read a single comparable picture; it is a presentation/comparability device ÖRS choice, not a change to the underlying VaR. The aggregation arithmetic above (addition at \(\rho=+1\)) is what produces the totals shown on that scale.

4 · Worked example — naïve sum vs consolidated IPS view

Worked example — a stylised mini-IPS (clean, illustrative numbers)

Three sector members along the Sektorbeteiligungen chain of Module 10:

  • Primärbank P holds a €5m participation in the RLB (Beteiligung at the Landeszentrale), and has a €3m intra-group deposit at the RLB (part of its Liquiditätsreserve).
  • RLB (Landeszentrale) holds a €20m participation in RBI (the Zentralinstitut, ZI).

Suppose each member, computed Solo, carries the following risk on these positions (illustrative risk amounts, after applying the relevant risk factors of M9/M11):

ItemHeld bySolo riskRisk type
€5m participation in RLBP€1,5mBeteiligungsrisiko
€3m deposit at RLB (Liquiditätsreserve)P€0,2mIntra-Group credit risk
€20m participation in RBIRLB€6,0mBeteiligungsrisiko
All other (non-intra-group) riskP + RLB + RBI€40,0mcredit/market/op/…

Naïve sum of solo risks (just add everything — which is what \(\rho=+1\) aggregation does within each entity, then summed across entities):

$$ R_{\text{naïve}} = 1{,}5 + 0{,}2 + 6{,}0 + 40{,}0 = \textbf{€47,7m}. $$

But the €5m participation, the €3m deposit and the €20m participation are intra-circle: the capital backing P's stake in the RLB is the same capital the RLB already holds, which in turn is the same capital RBI holds. Counting P's Beteiligungsrisiko and the RLB's own risk and RBI's own risk triple-counts one underlying euro chain (Primärbank → Landeszentrale → RBI). The €3m deposit is an asset of P and a funding liability of the RLB inside the same consolidated circle — it nets to zero on consolidation, so its credit risk must be eliminated.

Consolidated IPS view — eliminate the intra-circle participations and the intra-group credit exposure:

$$ R_{\text{IPS}} = \underbrace{47{,}7}_{\text{naïve}} \;-\; \underbrace{1{,}5}_{\text{P→RLB part.}} \;-\; \underbrace{0{,}2}_{\text{P deposit at RLB}} \;-\; \underbrace{6{,}0}_{\text{RLB→RBI part.}} \;=\; \textbf{€40,0m}. $$

Result: the consolidated risk (€40,0m) is lower than the naïve sum (€47,7m). The difference (€7,7m) is exactly the double-counted intra-sector exposure. Pair this consolidated risk with the consolidated Deckungsmasse (consolidated own funds, with the participation deductions / stille-Reserven adjustments of §5.1) and you get the IPS Auslastung. Pair P's solo risk with the consolidated cover and you would get a meaningless, flattering ratio — the rookie error of Module 1.

Direction of the effect — commit it to memory

Cross-type aggregation at \(\rho=+1\) only ever raises the total (no diversification). Intra-group consolidation eliminations only ever lower the consolidated total versus the naïve sum of solos. Different banks, different views, different Auslastung — for one and the same underlying bank.

5 · In practice — the three views and the §7 consolidation adjustments

5.1 Why Solo, KI-Gruppe (§30 BWG) and IPS give different Auslastung

Auslastung = Risiko / Deckungsmasse. Moving from a Solo view to a consolidated view changes both the numerator and the denominator:

  • Numerator (risk): consolidation eliminates intra-group participations and intra-group exposures (they net out within the circle), so the consolidated risk is below the sum of solo risks.
  • Denominator (Deckungsmasse): consolidation replaces the sum of solo own funds with consolidated available own funds, and applies the deduction / stille-Reserven adjustments for non-consolidated participations so the cover is not overstated either.

Because both sides change — and not proportionally — the ratio differs across views for the same bank. There is no contradiction in a bank being yellow Solo and green IPS, or vice versa; they answer different questions on different scopes.

⚖️ The consistency rule and the §30 BWG bridge

The Leitfaden's iron rule (first met in Module 1): risk and Deckungsmasse must be on the SAME scope — Solo-with-Solo, KI-Gruppe-with-KI-Gruppe, IPS-with-IPS. The KI-Gruppe is the prudential consolidation group under §30 BWG regulatory (the Austrian transposition defining which entities are consolidated for supervisory own-funds purposes). The ÖRS regards an explicit Überleitungsrechnung (reconciliation) from the Früherkennung's consolidated figures to the §30 BWG KI-Gruppe as supervisorily desirable — it lets the FMA/OeNB tie the sector's economic view back to the legal prudential group ÖRS choice.

5.2 The IPS consolidation adjustments (the heart of §7)

For a consolidated R-IPS view you must avoid overstating risk because of intra-sector links. Two sides:

(1) Deckungsmasse side. Use consolidated available own funds (anrechenbare Eigenmittel) for the circle, and apply the deduction / stille-Reserven adjustments for non-consolidated participations so the cover is not overstated. (This dovetails with Module 13's Deckungsmassen mechanics: freier Überschuss, freies Kapital, stille Reserven, excess/shortfall — here on a consolidated basis.)

(2) Risk side — two eliminations:

  • Exclude Beteiligungsrisiken within the consolidation circle — participations in the Zentralinstitut and in Spezialgesellschaften (special-purpose sector companies). If you don't, the same capital is counted as risk at the Primärbank and at the RLB and at RBI. The Sektorbeteiligungen chain of Module 10 (Primärbank → Landeszentrale → RBI) is precisely the double-count mechanism: each layer's participation in the next is backed by the next layer's own capital, which already bears its own risk.
  • Exclude intra-group credit exposures — the "Intra-Group-Geschäfte" to the ZI and to other members of the circle, e.g. the Liquiditätsreserve a Primärbank holds at its Landeszentrale. Inside the consolidated circle that deposit is an intercompany claim that nets to zero; leaving its credit risk in would double-count it against the same underlying assets.
🟣 IFRS-group units — risk side and cover side

Some units sit in the IFRS consolidation group but not in the §30 BWG KI-Gruppe (or vice versa). The Leitfaden treats them consistently on both sides ÖRS choice:

  • In RISK: via look-through if the unit is also in the §30 BWG KI-Gruppe (its risks enter line by line); otherwise — units only in the IFRS group — as a Beteiligungsrisiko (the stake, not the look-through).
  • In DECKUNGSMASSE: "both-group" units (in both §30 BWG and IFRS) contribute via freier Jahres-/Konzernüberschuss (free annual/group surplus) and freies Kapital (free capital); IFRS-only units contribute only via freier Überschuss.
  • Stille Reserven (hidden reserves) are taken on the §30 BWG KI-Gruppe basis, because hidden reserves exist at the single-institution level only (they have no meaning in the IFRS consolidated accounts).

5.3 Tie to Module 2 — the CRR already does the cover-side analogue

⚖️ Cross-holding deductions (CRR) vs IPS risk-side elimination

The CRR regulatory already deducts significant and non-significant holdings in financial-sector entities from CET1 (Articles 36, 43–48 — Module 2) so that capital is not double-counted up a holding chain: if Bank A owns Bank B's shares, A cannot count B's capital as A's own loss-absorbing capacity. That is a cover-side deduction. The IPS risk-side elimination of intra-circle Beteiligungsrisiken and intra-group exposures is the risk-side analogue of the very same anti-double-counting principle: the CRR stops the sector double-counting capital; the Früherkennung's §7 stops the sector double-counting the risk that capital backs. Both prevent the sector from counting the same euro twice — one on the denominator, one on the numerator of the Auslastung.

6 · Nuances & traps

⚠ Addition = correlation +1 is deliberate, not an accident

Do not describe the absence of cross-type diversification as a "missing benefit". It is the chosen \(\rho=+1\) corner — the most conservative aggregation, picked on purpose to keep the simple 95% defensible. An examiner asking "why no diversification credit?" wants exactly this: it is prudence, not an omission. The only \(\rho<1\) used is within market risk (Zins ↔ Spread), within FX (currencies), within spread (rating classes) and within the FLVaR (bands) — never across risk types.

⚠ Intra-group eliminations REDUCE risk — mind the sign

Aggregation (\(\rho=+1\)) adds; consolidation eliminations subtract. Consolidated IPS risk is lower than the naïve sum of solo risks, because the intra-circle participations and intra-group exposures are removed. If your consolidated number comes out higher than the sum of solos, you have aggregated where you should have eliminated.

⚠ Never pair a Solo risk with a consolidated Deckungsmasse

This is the consistency rule made concrete. A Solo risk (which still contains the intra-group participation and the Liquiditätsreserve exposure) set against a consolidated cover (which has netted those out and consolidated own funds) gives a flattering, meaningless ratio. Solo-with-Solo, KI-Gruppe-with-KI-Gruppe, IPS-with-IPS — always.

⚠ Stille Reserven exist only at single-institution level

Hidden reserves (book value vs Verkehrswert on securities and participations) are a single-institution phenomenon — they vanish in IFRS consolidated accounts. So in the consolidated views, stille Reserven are always taken on the §30 BWG KI-Gruppe (solo-existence) basis, never re-derived from the IFRS consolidated balance sheet. Mixing the two double-counts or invents reserves.

Recap

  • Aggregation = addition of all material single risks = an assumed correlation of \(\rho=+1\) across risk types. From \(\mathrm{VaR}_P=\sqrt{\mathrm{VaR}_A^2+\mathrm{VaR}_B^2+2\rho\,\mathrm{VaR}_A\mathrm{VaR}_B}\), setting \(\rho=1\) collapses the root to \(\mathrm{VaR}_A+\mathrm{VaR}_B\) — the most conservative aggregation (zero diversification), which is what makes the simple 95% defensible.
  • The only \(\rho<1\) used is within market risk (Zins ↔ Spread), within FX (currencies), within spread (rating classes), and within the FLVaR (bands) — never across risk types. Totals are presented on a Masterskala for comparability across Forderungsklassen.
  • IPS consolidation (§7): (1) Deckungsmasse on consolidated own funds, with deduction/stille-Reserven adjustments for non-consolidated participations; (2) risk side excludes intra-circle Beteiligungsrisiken (ZI/Spezialgesellschaften) and intra-group credit exposures (Intra-Group-Geschäfte, e.g. the Liquiditätsreserve). This prevents the Primärbank → Landeszentrale → RBI chain double-counting.
  • Solo vs KI-Gruppe (§30 BWG) vs IPS give different Auslastung because consolidation changes both numerator and denominator. Keep risk and cover on the SAME scope; an Überleitungsrechnung to the §30 BWG KI-Gruppe is supervisorily desirable. IFRS-only units: risk as Beteiligungsrisiko, cover via freier Überschuss only; both-group units: look-through risk, cover via freier Überschuss + freies Kapital; stille Reserven on §30 BWG basis.
  • The CRR's CET1 cross-holding deductions (M2) are the cover-side analogue; the IPS risk-side elimination is the risk-side analogue — both stop the sector counting the same euro twice.
🇦🇹 Zusammenfassung (Deutsch)
  • Die Risikoaggregation erfolgt durch Addition aller wesentlichen Einzelrisiken — das entspricht einer angenommenen Korrelation von +1 über die Risikoarten. Aus \(\mathrm{VaR}_P=\sqrt{\mathrm{VaR}_A^2+\mathrm{VaR}_B^2+2\rho\,\mathrm{VaR}_A\mathrm{VaR}_B}\) wird bei \(\rho=1\) genau \(\mathrm{VaR}_A+\mathrm{VaR}_B\): die konservativste Aggregation (kein Diversifikationseffekt), die das einfache 95% rechtfertigt.
  • Korrelationen \(<1\) nur innerhalb des Marktrisikos (Zins ↔ Credit-Spread), innerhalb der Devisen, innerhalb der Spread-Ratingklassen und innerhalb des FLVaR (Laufzeitbänder). Darstellung auf einer Masterskala.
  • IPS-Konsolidierung (§7): Deckungsmasse auf konsolidierte Eigenmittel (mit Abzügen/stille-Reserven-Anpassung für nicht konsolidierte Beteiligungen); auf der Risikoseite werden Beteiligungsrisiken innerhalb des Konsolidierungskreises (ZI/Spezialgesellschaften) und Intra-Group-Geschäfte (z. B. die Liquiditätsreserve) eliminiert — sonst Doppelzählung entlang Primärbank → Landeszentrale → RBI.
  • Solo vs. KI-Gruppe (§30 BWG) vs. IPS ergeben unterschiedliche Auslastungen, weil Zähler und Nenner sich ändern. Risiko und Deckungsmasse stets auf gleicher Ebene; eine Überleitungsrechnung auf die §30-BWG-KI-Gruppe ist aufsichtlich erwünscht. Stille Reserven auf §30-BWG-Basis (nur auf Einzelinstitutsebene vorhanden).
  • Die CRR-Abzüge für Überkreuzbeteiligungen vom CET1 (M2) sind das kapitalseitige Gegenstück; die IPS-Eliminierung ist das risikoseitige Gegenstück desselben Prinzips gegen Doppelzählung.
