Module 8 — Operational risk (§5.6): deriving the 5,0% Problemfall factor from a ⅓-normal / ⅔-lognormal tail

Operational risk is the one place in the RTFA where the Problemfall rescaling does not reduce to a simple ratio of normal quantiles. The Extremfall charge is a 15% factor anchored to the regulatory Basisindikatoransatz; the Problemfall factor is 5,0% — and that 5,0% only falls out if you model op-risk loss as a mixture of a normal and a fat-tailed lognormal component. This module derives that 5,0% step by step, shows why a normal-only rescaling would wrongly give ~8%, and places IKT/ICT and AML risk where they belong: inside operational risk.

By the end of this module you can…

  • Define operationelles Risiko precisely and explain why Betriebserträge (operating income) — not assets — is its exposure proxy.
  • Anchor the 15% Extremfall factor to the CRR Basisindikatoransatz / Standardansatz.
  • Derive the 5,0% Problemfall factor from the ⅓-normal / ⅔-lognormal assumption, including the lognormal-quantile EXKURS.
  • Show that this is just M4 vertical VaR consistency applied to a non-normal distribution — and contrast it with the purely normal CVA scalar 0,71 (M7).
  • Locate IKT/ICT risk and AML/CFT inside op risk, and flag that CRR III's SMA supersedes the BIA/TSA approach.

1 · Motivation — why op risk needs its own tail

Credit and market risk have clean parametric stories: a latent normal factor (M5), a parametric VaR (M6). Operational risk does not. Its loss distribution is dominated by rare, very large events — a fraud, a failed IT migration, a money-laundering penalty, a legal judgment — sitting in a long right tail, while everyday small losses (processing errors, minor outages) cluster near zero. A normal distribution cannot represent that: it has thin, symmetric tails. So when the Leitfaden rescales the regulatory op-risk charge from the 99,9% Extremfall to the 95% Problemfall, it cannot just use the normal-quantile ratio it uses for CVA. It must respect the fat right tail — and that is the entire reason the Problemfall factor is 5,0% and not ~8%.

▶ The one idea of this module

The same machine you met in M4 — vertical VaR consistency, \(\text{VaR}_{q_2}=\text{VaR}_{q_1}\cdot \frac{\text{quantile}(q_2)}{\text{quantile}(q_1)}\) — is applied here to a distribution that is partly lognormal. Because the lognormal's tail grows much faster than the normal's, the 95%-to-99,9% quantile ratio is far smaller for the lognormal component, which drags the rescaled factor down from ~8% (normal-only) to 5,0% (the mixture). The fat tail makes the 95% loss a much smaller share of the 99,9% loss.

2 · Concept & logic — what op risk is, and its indicator

Operationelles Risiko (operational risk) = the risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, people and systems, or from external events. By regulatory definition (CRR Art. 4(1)(52)) it includes Rechtsrisiko (legal risk) — losses from litigation, defective documentation, regulatory penalties. The Leitfaden treats two further families explicitly inside op risk:

  • IKT-/ICT-Risiko (information & communication technology risk) — per the FMA IKT-Leitfaden and now DORA. Sub-types: Verfügbarkeit/Kontinuität (availability/continuity), Sicherheit (security), Veränderung (change management), Datenintegrität (data integrity), and Auslagerung (outsourcing/third-party).
  • Geldwäsche-/Terrorismusfinanzierungsrisiko (AML/CFT — money laundering & terrorist financing) — the financial-crime exposure that materialises as op-risk loss (fines, remediation, legal). It is not a separate RTFA risk type; it lives here.
The indicator is income, not assets

Op risk has no "exposure" you can read off the balance sheet. The regulatory proxy is Betriebserträge (operating income / gross income): the more business an institution does, the larger its op-risk surface — more transactions to misprocess, more systems to fail, more staff and counterparties, more legal and AML touchpoints. So the exposure measure is a flow (income), not a stock (assets). Sourced from FINREP/VERA reporting.

3 · Derivation — the 15% anchor and the 5,0% factor

3.1 The Extremfall: 15% of Betriebserträge

The Extremfall (99,9%) charge equals 15% of the three-year average Betriebserträge. This is anchored directly in the CRR:

$$ \text{OpRisk}_{\text{Extremfall}} \;=\; 15\% \cdot \overline{\text{Betriebserträge}}_{\,3\text{yr}} $$
Basisindikatoransatz (BIA), CRR Art. 315–316: own-funds requirement = 15% of the 3-year average positive gross income (the \(\alpha\)-factor). The Standardansatz (TSA) instead applies business-line betas of 12–18% (retail/asset-mgmt 12%, commercial banking 15%, payment/trading 18%) that average to roughly 15%. The Leitfaden uses the single 15% factor. regulatory-anchored
⚖️ Why 15% is regulatory, not an ÖRS invention

The 15% \(\alpha\) of the Basisindikatoransatz is a fixed CRR coefficient — calibrated by the Basel Committee to a 99,9% / one-year op-risk capital standard, the same confidence as the IRB credit calibration (M4/M5). So the Extremfall = 15%·Betriebserträge is regulatory; what the Leitfaden chooses is to use the BIA-style simplification rather than a loss-distribution model — judged adequate (§5.6) "aufgrund der relativen Gewichte der Geschäftsfelder der RBG" (given the relative weights of the RBG's business lines, no single high-beta line dominates).

3.2 The Problemfall: rescale 15% from 99,9% to 95% — but the distribution is not normal

To get the Problemfall (95%) factor we rescale the 99,9% charge by the ratio of the 95% to 99,9% quantiles — exactly the vertical VaR consistency of M4. But op-risk loss is not normal. The Leitfaden assumes it is a probability-weighted mixture: ⅓ normally distributed and ⅔ lognormally distributed ÖRS modelling assumption. The lognormal ⅔ captures the fat right tail (big, rare losses); the normal ⅓ the everyday operational noise. The rescaling factor is the weighted average of each component's own 95%/99,9% quantile ratio:

$$ f_{\text{Problemfall}} \;=\; 15\% \cdot \Big( \underbrace{0{,}33 \cdot \tfrac{1{,}645}{3{,}090}}_{\text{normal }\tfrac13} \;+\; \underbrace{0{,}67 \cdot \tfrac{5{,}180}{21{,}982}}_{\text{lognormal }\tfrac23} \Big) \;=\; 5{,}0\% $$
\(1{,}645,\,3{,}090\) = the standard-normal 95% and 99,9% quantiles; \(5{,}180,\,21{,}982\) = the corresponding lognormal quantile factors (derived in the EXKURS). Each fraction is that component's 95%/99,9% VaR ratio; the weights ⅓ and ⅔ are the mixture probabilities. ÖRS choice
🟣 EXKURS — the lognormal quantile factor

If \(Y\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)\) then \(X=\exp(Y)\) is lognormally distributed. Because \(\exp(\cdot)\) is strictly increasing, the \(p\)-quantile of \(X\) is the exponential of the \(p\)-quantile of \(Y\):

$$ Q_X(p) \;=\; \exp\!\big(\mu + u(p)\,\sigma\big), \qquad u(p)=\Phi^{-1}(p)\ \text{(standard-normal quantile).} $$

Normalise with \(\mu=0,\ \sigma=1\) to read off the pure multiplicative tail factor. The standard-normal quantiles are \(u(0{,}95)=1{,}645\) and \(u(0{,}999)=3{,}090\), so:

$$ Q_X(0{,}95)=e^{1{,}645}=5{,}180, \qquad Q_X(0{,}999)=e^{3{,}090}=21{,}982. $$

The lognormal stretches the tail: moving from 95% to 99,9% multiplies the quantile by \(21{,}982/5{,}180\approx 4{,}24\), versus only \(3{,}090/1{,}645\approx 1{,}88\) for the normal. That asymmetry is the fat tail, and it is what pulls the Problemfall factor below the normal-only value.

3.3 The arithmetic, made explicit

Compute each component's 95%/99,9% quantile ratio, weight, and sum:

$$ \text{normal component: } \frac{1{,}645}{3{,}090}=0{,}532, \qquad \text{lognormal component: } \frac{5{,}180}{21{,}982}=0{,}236. $$ $$ 0{,}33\cdot 0{,}532 \;+\; 0{,}67\cdot 0{,}236 \;=\; 0{,}1756 + 0{,}1581 \;=\; 0{,}3337 \;\approx\; \tfrac13. $$ $$ f_{\text{Problemfall}} = 15\% \cdot 0{,}333 \;=\; 5{,}0\%. $$
The mixture rescales the 99,9% charge to ≈ ⅓ of itself, giving 5,0%. The normal piece contributes 0,176, the lognormal piece 0,158 — even though the lognormal carries the larger weight (⅔), its tiny ratio (0,236) means it pulls the average down.
This is M4, applied to a non-normal distribution

In M7 (CVA) the Problemfall scalar was \(\Phi^{-1}(0{,}95)/\Phi^{-1}(0{,}99)=1{,}645/2{,}326=0{,}71\) — a pure normal ratio, because the CVA charge is treated as a normal VaR. Here the same vertical-consistency idea is applied to a mixture with a lognormal tail, so the scalar is the weighted ratio \(0{,}333\), not a single normal ratio. Same engine, different distribution. The ⅓/⅔ split and the lognormality are the ÖRS modelling assumptions; the engine (vertical VaR consistency) is the regulatory-style logic from M4.

4 · Worked example

Worked example — €20m Betriebserträge, both scenarios, and the trap factor

Inputs: 3-year average Betriebserträge = €20m (from FINREP/VERA).

Extremfall (99,9%): \(\;15\% \cdot 20\text{m} = \textbf{€3{,}0m}.\)

Problemfall (95%): \(\;5\% \cdot 20\text{m} = \textbf{€1{,}0m}.\)   (Using the derived factor \(f_{\text{Problemfall}}=5{,}0\%\).)

Why it is NOT 8%. Suppose — wrongly — you rescaled with the normal-only ratio, as if op-risk loss were Gaussian like CVA:

$$ 15\% \cdot \frac{1{,}645}{3{,}090} = 15\% \cdot 0{,}532 = 8{,}0\% \;\Rightarrow\; 8\%\cdot 20\text{m} = \text{€1{,}6m}. $$

That overstates the Problemfall charge by 60% (€1,6m vs €1,0m). The ⅔ lognormal weight, whose 95%/99,9% ratio is only 0,236, drags the blended factor from 8,0% down to 5,0%. The fat tail means the 95% loss is a much smaller fraction of the 99,9% loss than a normal world would predict — so the Problemfall charge is correspondingly lighter.

5 · In practice (the Leitfaden & the data)

  • Data source: Betriebserträge is taken from FINREP/VERA as the three-year average operating income; the same series that feeds the BIA reporting.
  • The simplification's justification: the single 15%/5% factor approach is judged adequate "aufgrund der relativen Gewichte der Geschäftsfelder der RBG" — because the Raiffeisen Bankengruppe's business mix is dominated by lower-beta retail/commercial banking, so a business-line-weighted TSA would land near the BIA's 15% anyway.
  • IKT/ICT risk is monitored along its sub-types — Verfügbarkeit/Kontinuität, Sicherheit, Veränderung (Change), Datenintegrität, Auslagerung (Outsourcing) — but capitalised within this op-risk charge, not separately.
  • AML/CFT (Geldwäsche/Terrorismusfinanzierung) is likewise treated as an op-risk driver: its losses (fines, remediation, legal) fall under the same 15%/5% factor, not under a standalone risk type.
⚖️ CRR III flag — the SMA supersedes BIA/TSA/AMA

Under CRR III (EU application from 1 Jan 2025) the Basisindikatoransatz, the Standardansatz and the Advanced Measurement Approach are all replaced by a single Standardised Measurement Approach (SMA):

$$ \text{Op-risk capital (SMA)} = \underbrace{\text{BIC}}_{\text{Business Indicator Component}} \times \underbrace{\text{ILM}}_{\text{Internal Loss Multiplier}}. $$

The BIC is a marginal-coefficient function of a Business Indicator (interest, services and financial components — a refined income measure), and the ILM scales it by the bank's own loss history (10-year average losses). So SMA adds loss-history sensitivity that the flat 15% BIA lacks: a bank with a clean loss record can have ILM < 1; a loss-heavy one > 1. The Leitfaden's 15%/5% (BIA-style) approach therefore post-dates the CRR III change and is superseded. verify against CRR III / SMA

6 · Nuances & traps

⚠ Trap — 5% is NOT a normal-only rescaling

If you compute \(15\%\cdot\Phi^{-1}(0{,}95)/\Phi^{-1}(0{,}999)=15\%\cdot0{,}532\) you get 8,0%, not 5,0%. The 5,0% only appears because ⅔ of the distribution is lognormal, with a much smaller 95%/99,9% ratio (0,236). Reaching for the CVA-style single normal ratio is the classic error here — op risk is the one charge where the rescaling is not purely normal.

⚠ Trap — the op-risk indicator is income, not assets

Op-risk exposure scales with Betriebserträge (a flow), not with RWA or total assets (a stock). A bank can shrink its balance sheet and still carry the same op-risk surface if its income — transaction volume, staff, systems — is unchanged. Sizing op risk off assets is wrong.

⚠ Trap — AML and IKT live HERE, not as separate risk types

In the RTFA there is no standalone "AML risk" or "IT risk" line. Both Geldwäsche/Terrorismusfinanzierung and IKT/ICT risk are components of operationelles Risiko (op risk includes Rechtsrisiko too). Listing them as separate aggregated risks double-counts and breaks the §5.6 structure.

Recap

  • Operationelles Risiko = loss from inadequate/failed internal processes, people, systems or external events; includes Rechtsrisiko, and houses IKT/ICT and AML/CFT risk. Indicator = Betriebserträge (income, not assets).
  • Extremfall = 15% of 3-yr avg Betriebserträge, anchored to the CRR Basisindikatoransatz (Art. 315–316; TSA betas 12–18% ≈ 15%). regulatory
  • Problemfall = 5,0%, derived by rescaling 15% from 99,9% to 95% under a ⅓-normal / ⅔-lognormal mixture: \(15\%\cdot(0{,}33\cdot\tfrac{1{,}645}{3{,}090}+0{,}67\cdot\tfrac{5{,}180}{21{,}982})=15\%\cdot0{,}333=5{,}0\%\). ÖRS choice
  • EXKURS: lognormal \(p\)-quantile = \(\exp(\mu+u(p)\sigma)\); \(e^{1{,}645}=5{,}180\), \(e^{3{,}090}=21{,}982\). This is M4 vertical VaR consistency on a non-normal distribution — contrast CVA's pure-normal 0,71 (M7).
  • A normal-only rescaling would give 8,0%; the lognormal ⅔ pulls it to 5,0% — the fat tail matters. CRR III replaces BIA/TSA/AMA with the SMA = BIC × ILM (loss-history sensitive). verify against CRR III
