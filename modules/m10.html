Module 10 — Beteiligungsrisiko (§5.4): the participation-risk framework

The Raiffeisenbankengruppe (RBG) is an institution built on mutual participations — Primärbanken hold the Landeszentrale, Landeszentralen hold RBI. So Beteiligungsrisiko (participation / equity-investment risk) is not a footnote: it is structural. Because there is no market-priced, CRR-style formula for it, almost every number in this module is an ÖRS expert estimate expressed as a band — and your job is to know exactly which numbers are forced and which are modelled.

By the end of this module you can…

  • Name and define the six sub-risks of Beteiligungsrisiko in German and English, and place them on the value-ladder (Verkehrswert → Buchwert → stille Reserve).
  • Compute participation risk as Verkehrswert · Risikofaktor, building the Verkehrswert from on-balance, weighted off-balance and stille Reserven.
  • Read and apply the rating→Risikofaktor band tables for Bank/Industrie vs Immobilien, above and below the €10m threshold.
  • Explain the "3× rule", the Ausübungswahrscheinlichkeiten (exercise probabilities), and why bands beat point values.
  • Separate the regulatory anchors from the heavy ÖRS expert estimation, and spot the IPS double-count that Module 12 removes.

1 · Motivation — why participation risk needs its own method

A loan has a contractual cash flow, a PD and an LGD; the IRB formula (Module 5) prices its unexpected loss. An equity participation (Beteiligung) has none of that machinery: no promised repayment, no default event, no LGD. Its loss distribution is the distribution of the equity value itself. For a listed name you could take an equity VaR off market data — but the RBG's participations are overwhelmingly unlisted, mutual, strategic holdings (a Primärbank's share in its Landeszentrale, an Industriebeteiligung, a property-holding subsidiary). There is no liquid price and no CRR formula. So the Leitfaden does the only honest thing: it builds a haircut model calibrated by expert judgement, and it is transparent that the numbers are estimates.

▶ Why this matters more here than anywhere else

The sector's capital architecture is a chain of participations. A value fall in a Landeszentrale propagates up the holding chain and across the R-IPS. Getting Beteiligungsrisiko approximately right — and being able to defend the haircuts to the Aufsichtsrat — is a core competence for a Sector Risk Controller. The flip side: because Sektorbeteiligungen are internal to the group, the solo numbers double-count risk that the consolidated (IPS) view in Module 12 must eliminate.

2 · Concept — the six sub-risks and the value ladder

Beteiligungsrisiko decomposes into six sub-risks. Define each precisely — the German term carries the legal meaning:

Sub-risk (German)EnglishWhat goes wrong
DividendenausfallrisikoDividend-default riskThe participation pays a smaller dividend than budgeted (or none). Hits the income side, not the carrying value — so it is treated apart from the other five.
Teilwertabschreibungsrisiko (TWA)Write-down riskThe fair value drops below book value (Buchwert) far enough to force a Wertberichtigung / Teilwertabschreibung in the accounts — a realised P&L hit while still holding the stake.
VeräußerungsverlustrisikoDisposal-loss riskOn sale, the proceeds fall short of the carrying value — a realised loss crystallised by exit.
Risiko aus NachschusspflichtenTop-up (capital-call) riskA statutory or contractual obligation to inject further capital (e.g. cooperative Nachschusspflicht, Genossenschaft) — cash out the door beyond the original stake.
Risiko aus strategischer (moralischer) SanierungsverantwortungVoluntary cooperative bail-out responsibilityA voluntary but quasi-binding obligation to recapitalise a troubled member to protect the group/brand. Applies only to strategic participations (the sector commitment, not a contract).
Reduktion stiller ReservenHidden-reserve reductionThe cushion between fair value and book value (the stille Reserve) shrinks. No P&L hit yet, but the Deckungsmasse (risk-bearing capacity) loses cover.
The value ladder — how a value fall climbs the five

Picture a participation at \(t=0\) with three stacked quantities: Verkehrswert (fair value, top) > Buchwert (book value, middle); the gap between them is the stille Reserve (hidden reserve). Now let value fall over \([t_0,t_1]\):

  1. First it eats the stille Reserve — Verkehrswert drops toward Buchwert. That is Reduktion stiller Reserven: cover lost, no accounting loss yet.
  2. Then it breaches book value — Verkehrswert < Buchwert forces a Wertberichtigung. That is Teilwertabschreibungsrisiko: a realised write-down.
  3. Then it forces an action — a sale below carrying value (Veräußerungsverlustrisiko) and/or a cash injection (Nachschuss / Sanierungsverantwortung).

So the five "value" sub-risks are not independent stories — they are stages of one move down the ladder. Dividend risk is the odd one out: it lives on the income statement, not the ladder.

Except Dividendenausfallrisiko, all five value sub-risks are measured the same statistical way: the loss not exceeded with 95% probability (Problemfall) and with 99,9% probability (Extremfall) ÖRS choice — the same VaR confidence levels as every other risk type (Module 4 vertical consistency). The difference is that here the loss distribution is not derived from a model; the percentile is encoded directly in the haircut bands.

3 · Derivation — Risiko = Verkehrswert · Risikofaktor

The whole framework collapses to one product:

$$ \text{Beteiligungsrisiko} \;=\; \text{Verkehrswert} \,\cdot\, \text{Risikofaktor (Haircut)} $$
Verkehrswert = fair value of the participation = Buchwert (on-balance carrying value + weighted off-balance) + stille Reserven.   Risikofaktor = the percentile loss (95% or 99,9%) expressed as a fraction of fair value — read from the rating→segment band tables in §5.

Build the Verkehrswert from its components:

$$ \text{Verkehrswert} \;=\; \underbrace{B_{\text{on}}}_{\text{on-balance book}} \;+\; \underbrace{\sum_j w_j \cdot E_j}_{\text{weighted off-balance}} \;+\; \underbrace{R_{\text{still}}}_{\text{stille Reserven}} $$
\(B_{\text{on}}\) = on-balance carrying value of the equity-financing instruments; \(E_j\) = nominal of off-balance exposure \(j\); \(w_j\) = its Ausübungswahrscheinlichkeit (exercise probability, §5.5); \(R_{\text{still}}\) = hidden reserves (fair value − book) on the holding.

Which financing instruments count? The Verkehrswert aggregates every form of equity and equity-near funding the bank has put into the participation:

  • Grund-/Stammkapital — ordinary share / nominal capital.
  • Partizipationskapital — participation capital (Austrian quasi-equity).
  • Eigenkapitalnahes Mezzanin — Sozietäre Genussrechte — equity-near mezzanine / participating profit-rights certificates.
  • Off-balance Eventualverpflichtungen — contingent liabilities: Haftungen (guarantees) and Garantien, brought on via exercise probabilities (the off-balance term above).
🟣 Why bands, not point values

ÖRS choice Every Risikofaktor is an expert estimate given as a bandwidth (e.g. 10–15%), never a single number. Two deliberate reasons: (1) it avoids Scheingenauigkeit (false precision) — pretending you can pin an unlisted strategic stake's tail loss to one decimal is dishonest; (2) the band implicitly absorbs concentration and cyclicality that a single point could not — the assessor picks within the band according to how concentrated / cyclical the specific holding is. The model is backtested annually against actual company valuations and rating migrations, and recalibrated. There is essentially no CRR formula being approximated here — this is genuine internal modelling under proportionality.

4 · Worked example — one industrial participation, both scenarios

Worked example — an Industriebeteiligung, rating 2,5

Step 1 — build the Verkehrswert.

  • On-balance Buchwert \(B_{\text{on}}=\)€12,0m (Grund-/Stammkapital + a Sozietäres Genussrecht).
  • Off-balance: a Haftung (guarantee) of nominal €15,0m. The participation is rated 2,5, so its Ausübungswahrscheinlichkeit (§5.5) is 20% → weighted off-balance \(=0{,}20\cdot 15{,}0=\)€3,0m.
  • Stille Reserve \(R_{\text{still}}=\)€5,0m (fair value above book).
$$ \text{Verkehrswert}=12{,}0+3{,}0+5{,}0=\textbf{€20,0m}. $$

Step 2 — pick the Risikofaktor. Segment = Industrie, book value > €10m ⇒ use the general/bank band table (§5.3). Rating 2,5 ⇒ Problemfall 10–15%, Extremfall 30–45%. There is no special concentration and the holding is mid-cyclical, so take the mid-band: 12,5% and 37,5%.

$$ \text{Risiko}_{\text{Problemfall}}=20{,}0\cdot 12{,}5\%=\textbf{€2,5m}, \qquad \text{Risiko}_{\text{Extremfall}}=20{,}0\cdot 37{,}5\%=\textbf{€7,5m}. $$

Step 3 — sanity-check with the 3× rule. \(7{,}5 / 2{,}5 = 3{,}0\): the Extremfall is exactly 3× the Problemfall, as the rule of thumb predicts and the bands roughly obey (10–15% → 30–45%).  Band-choice reasoning: had this been a single dominant, highly cyclical stake you would slide toward the top of the band (15% / 45%); a granular, defensive holding pulls toward the bottom (10% / 30%). The band is the concentration/cyclicality adjustment.

5.4 The "3× rule"

Extremfall ≈ 3 × Problemfall

As a rule of thumb the Extremfall write-down risk is about three times the Problemfall. Verify against the bands: rating 2,0/2,5 reads 10–15% → 30–45% (×3 exactly at both ends); 3,0 reads 15–20% → 45–60% (×3); 3,5 reads 20–25% → 60–75% (×3). The relation breaks down only where Extremfall saturates at 100% (4,5 and 5,x) — there the ratio compresses below 3 because the haircut cannot exceed total loss. So 3× is a rule of thumb the bands roughly obey by construction, not a derived law: it encodes the jump from a 1-in-20-year (95%) to a 1-in-1000-year (99,9%) equity loss for an unlisted stake.

5.5 Off-balance Eventualverpflichtungen — Ausübungswahrscheinlichkeiten

Contingent liabilities (Haftungen / Garantien given for the participation) are off-balance, so they are pulled into the Verkehrswert via Ausübungswahrscheinlichkeiten (exercise probabilities) — directly analogous to the regulatory Credit Conversion Factor (CCF) for credit risk, but rating-driven here ÖRS choice:

(Note: the §4 example used 20% because that stake was rated 2,0-band-equivalent; a 2,5 stake's own holding uses 30%. In the example the guarantee was on a 2,0-rated obligor → 20%.)

5.6 Segments — Bank vs Industrie vs Immobilien

  • Bankbeteiligungen: participations in Bundesgruppe & R-IPS members and their subsidiaries, including bank-near service companies (bank-nahe Dienstleister). Sektorbeteiligungen = all bank participations within the RBG, whether inside or outside Austria. A Primärbank's Sektorbeteiligung is typically its stake in the Landeszentrale; the Landeszentrale's is its stake in RBI. These use the master band table.
  • Industriebeteiligungen: operating-company holdings. > €10m → master band table; ≤ €10m → flat factors. Most cyclical.
  • Immobilienbeteiligungen: property-holding companies. Lower bands (§5.2) reflecting low cyclicality and indexed rents.

5.7 Eigengenutzte Immobilien (own-used property)

Own-used real estate (eigengenutzte Immobilien) — on the bank's own balance sheet and inside subsidiaries — carries a flat haircut of 5% Problemfall / 15% Extremfall ÖRS choice (again roughly 3×). The corresponding stille Reserven (Schätzwert − Buchwert, i.e. appraisal value minus book) are credited to the Deckungsmasse, capped at that difference: \( R_{\text{still}}^{\max}=\text{Schätzwert}-\text{Buchwert}\). So own-used property is both a small risk charge and a source of cover.

5.8 Aggregation and the rating source

No diversification. Total Beteiligungsrisiko is the plain sum of the single-participation risks — correlation is set to +1, consistent with the rest of the RTFA (Module 4). There is no diversification credit across participations. Under proportionality, the Beteiligungsrating may default to the existing Kreditrating of the company where no dedicated participation rating exists; the expert haircut mapping is backtested annually against realised company valuations and rating migrations.

6 · Nuances & traps

⚠ Bands are not point values — resist Scheingenauigkeit

10–15% is a band, not "12,5%". The assessor must justify the chosen point inside the band by the holding's concentration and cyclicality. Quoting a single mid-band number to senior management without that reasoning misrepresents an expert estimate as a measurement. The band is the honesty mechanism — use it.

⚠ The €10m line is a single-assessment threshold, not a risk-free line

Below €10m you do not escape the charge — you switch from individual band assessment to flat per-rating-class factors (which are themselves conservative). The threshold controls effort, not existence. And it is per single participation (book value), not a portfolio aggregate.

⚠ Sektorbeteiligungen create the intra-IPS double-count (→ Module 12)

A Primärbank books participation risk on its Landeszentrale stake; the Landeszentrale books its own risk; the underlying assets are counted again as that bank's own risk. On a solo basis this is correct. On a consolidated R-IPS basis it double-counts: Module 12 (Aggregation & consolidation, Ch.7) explicitly excludes participation risks within the consolidation circle (and intra-group credit exposures). Never report a solo Beteiligungsrisiko as if it were the group's.

⚠ Banking-book equities: Beteiligungsrisiko OR Marktrisiko — not both

Equities held in the Bankbuch (banking book) may fall under Beteiligungsrisiko (strategic holdings) or under Preisrisiko Bankbuch / Marktrisiko (e.g. indirect or fund holdings, priced via a market-vola VaR or an index like the ATX — Module 6). The classification must be unambiguous per holding to avoid either a gap or a double count. Cross-link: M6 · Market risk.

⚓ What little here is regulatory

regulatory The concept of holding capital against equity investments, the analogy to the CCF (CRR Part Three) and the use of Säule-2 confidence levels are regulatory anchors. ÖRS choice But essentially every number in this module — all the haircut bands, the exercise probabilities, the €10m threshold, the 5%/15% own-property factors, the 3× rule, the no-diversification stance — is ÖRS expert estimation under proportionality. That is the headline you defend to the Aufsichtsrat: a transparent, backtested expert model, not a market measurement.

Recap

  • Six sub-risks: Dividendenausfall, Teilwertabschreibung (TWA), Veräußerungsverlust, Nachschusspflicht, strategische/moralische Sanierungsverantwortung (strategic holdings only), Reduktion stiller Reserven. Five sit on one value ladder (Verkehrswert → Buchwert → stille Reserve); dividend risk is income-side. All but dividend measured at 95% / 99,9%.
  • Risiko = Verkehrswert · Risikofaktor; Verkehrswert = Buchwert (on-balance + weighted off-balance via Ausübungswahrscheinlichkeiten) + stille Reserven; instruments = Grund-/Stammkapital, Partizipationskapital, Sozietäre Genussrechte, Eventualverpflichtungen.
  • Haircuts are expert-estimated bands (anti-Scheingenauigkeit; bands absorb concentration/cyclicality). Bank/Industrie>€10m share one table; Immobilien>€10m lower; below €10m flat factors. RBI A2(1,0) → 10%/30%.
  • 3× rule: Extremfall ≈ 3× Problemfall, obeyed by the bands except where Extremfall saturates at 100%.
  • No diversification (sum, corr = +1); rating may default to Kreditrating; backtested annually. Traps: the €10m line is effort not existence; Sektorbeteiligungen double-count solo→IPS (Module 12 removes it); banking-book equities are Beteiligungs- OR Marktrisiko (Module 6).
🇦🇹 Zusammenfassung (Deutsch)
  • Das Beteiligungsrisiko zerfällt in sechs Teilrisiken (Dividendenausfall-, Teilwertabschreibungs-, Veräußerungsverlust-, Nachschuss-, strategische/moralische Sanierungs- und Reduktion-stiller-Reserven-Risiko). Bis auf das Dividendenausfallrisiko ist jedes der mit 95% (Problemfall) bzw. 99,9% (Extremfall) nicht überschrittene Verlust.
  • Risiko = Verkehrswert · Risikofaktor. Der Verkehrswert = Buchwert (on-balance + gewichtete off-balance über Ausübungswahrscheinlichkeiten) + stille Reserven; einbezogen werden Grund-/Stammkapital, Partizipationskapital, sozietäre Genussrechte und Eventualverpflichtungen.
  • Die Risikofaktoren sind Experteneinschätzungen in Bandbreiten (gegen Scheingenauigkeit; sie erfassen implizit Konzentration und Zyklizität). Bank/Industrie>€10m teilen eine Tabelle, Immobilien>€10m sind niedriger, unter €10m gelten Pauschalfaktoren.
  • Faustregel: Extremfall ≈ 3× Problemfall (außer bei Sättigung auf 100%). Keine Diversifikation — das Gesamtrisiko ist die Summe der Einzelrisiken. Sektorbeteiligungen erzeugen die IPS-Doppelzählung, die Modul 12 eliminiert.
