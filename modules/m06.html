Module 6 — Market risk (§5.5): Zinsrisiko Bankbuch, Währungsrisiko, Preisrisiko & Credit-Spread VaR

Market risk in the Leitfaden is not one number but four sub-risks — Zinsrisiko Bankbuch (interest-rate risk in the banking book / IRRBB), Währungsrisiko (FX / open foreign-currency position), Preisrisiko (price risk, trading and banking book), and Spreadrisiko (credit-spread VaR) — each measured on a 250-day banking-book holding period, except the trading book, which is charged off own funds. This module derives the parametric-VaR machinery they share, the +200 bp shift that rescues the log-volatility method under negative rates, and the undiversified-vs-diversified split that is one of the few places the guide lets correlation fall below +1.

By the end of this module you can…

  • State the general market-risk frame: confidence-defined Problem-/Extremfall, the 250-day banking-book holding period, and the trading-book "own-funds charge minus the FX charge" double-count fix.
  • Derive the IRRBB parametric VaR as Barwert − simulierter Barwert per Laufzeitband and currency, and explain the +200 bp parallel shift as a computational device for the stetige (log) method under negative rates.
  • Compute an FX VaR per currency and aggregate it both undiversified (sign-neutral sum) and diversified (correlation matrix), and write the two-asset formula.
  • Scale a trading-book price charge by ×0,71 / ×1,33 and explain it is the same normal-quantile-ratio scaling as CVA.
  • Build the Credit-Spread VaR (PV2 − PV1) by Forderungsklasse × Ratingklasse and explain why it does not double-count with credit risk.

1 · Motivation — why market risk is four risks, not one

A Raiffeisen banking book is dominated by loans and deposits, not by a trading desk. Yet it still carries market risk: its assets and liabilities reprice at different speeds (interest-rate gap), it holds foreign-currency positions, it owns securities whose prices and credit spreads move, and — for the few Landesbanken with a desk — it runs a small Handelsbuch. The Leitfaden refuses to fold these into a single "market VaR". Each has a different driver, a different data source, and a different regulatory anchor, so each is modelled separately and then added into the Risikotragfähigkeitskalkül (correlation +1 across risk types, Module 4/12) — with one carefully-scoped exception inside market risk itself.

▶ The one place correlation < 1 is allowed

Across risk types the guide aggregates by addition (no diversification). But within market risk it permits sub-correlations: between currencies in FX risk, between rating classes in spread risk, and — per §7 — between Zinsrisiko and Credit-Spread risk. Recognising where diversification is and is not allowed is half of defending this methodology to the Aufsichtsrat (supervisory board). Cross-link: Module 4 (aggregation) and Module 12 (correlation governance).

2 · Concept & logic — the shared frame

Every banking-book market sub-risk is a parametric (variance-covariance) VaR: take a position, shock the relevant market rate by a volatility scaled to a confidence level, revalue, and call the present-value loss the risk. Two scenario settings drive the confidence:

  • Problemfall — confidence 95% ÖRS choice (the sector's Risikoappetit; see Module 4).
  • Extremfall — confidence 99,9% regulatory-anchored (IRB-consistent economic capital).

The FX sub-risk is stated over the full 95% … 99,9% percentile range for completeness. The shared mechanics:

Three structural choices that recur in every sub-risk
  • Holding period = 250 Tage (banking book) ÖRS choice. Volatilities estimated from a 3-year market-rate history are scaled to 250 trading days by the square-root-of-time rule (Module 4). The trading book has no holding period — it is charged off the regulatory own-funds requirement instead.
  • Relative (multiplicative) volatility. The shock is applied as a factor on the market level (rate · Vola), not an additive bp move — which is why negative rates become a problem (next section).
  • Diversified vs undiversified. The figure set against the Deckungsmasse is the diversified VaR (correlation matrix); the undiversified sum is reported alongside as a prudent upper bound.
⚖️ Trading book: own-funds charge MINUS the FX charge

The trading-book (Handelsbuch) market risk is taken as the regulatory own-funds requirement for position risk + FX risk + commodity risk, minus the FX own-funds charge. Why subtract the FX charge? Because the open foreign-currency position is already captured in full — across both books — by the separate Währungsrisiko sub-risk below. Leaving the FX charge inside the trading-book number would double-count the same open position. So: trading-book price risk = (position + FX + commodity charge) − (FX charge). The position-risk charge itself covers general + specific + option risk. regulatory charge, ÖRS de-duplication rule.

3 · Derivation

3.1 IRRBB — the present-value-difference VaR

Zinsrisiko Bankbuch (interest-rate risk in the banking book) is built on the supervisory IRRBB / ITS gap report: net interest-rate gaps \(g_{b,c}\) per Laufzeitband (maturity band) \(b\) and currency \(c\). The risk per band is the difference between the present value at today's rate and the present value at a shocked rate:

$$ \text{Risiko}_{b,c}\;=\;\underbrace{BW_{b,c}}_{\text{Barwert}}\;-\;\underbrace{BW^{\text{sim}}_{b,c}}_{\text{simulierter Barwert}} $$ $$ BW_{b,c}=\frac{g_{b,c}}{\big(1+r_{b,c}\big)^{t_b}},\qquad BW^{\text{sim}}_{b,c}=\frac{g_{b,c}}{\big(1+r_{b,c}\cdot(1+\sigma_{b,c}\,z_q)\big)^{t_b}} $$
\(g_{b,c}\) = net interest-rate gap in band \(b\), currency \(c\) (from the IRRBB/ITS gap report); \(r_{b,c}\) = spot market rate matched to band \(b\); \(t_b\) = the band's representative maturity in years; \(BW\) = Barwert (present value), discounting the gap at the spot rate; \(BW^{\text{sim}}\) = simulierter Barwert, discounting at the rate scaled by its volatility; \(\sigma_{b,c}\) = relative volatility of the maturity-matched market rate (3-year history); \(z_q=\Phi^{-1}(q)\) = the standard-normal quantile for the confidence \(q\) (1,6449 at 95%; 3,0902 at 99,9%), scaling the volatility from one standard deviation to the confidence level. The shock is multiplicative: rate · Vola.

Bands and currencies are then combined through a parametric VaR with a correlation matrix (not a simple sum) so that offsetting gaps and imperfectly-correlated rates net out. Gaps in "Sonstige Währungen" (other currencies, individually immaterial) are priced on the EUR curve as a simplification. Optionality enters via delta-equivalent off-balance gaps — i.e. an option is represented by its delta-weighted notional placed in the appropriate band.

3.2 The +200 bp parallel shift — why negative rates break the log method

The volatility \(\sigma_{b,c}\) is computed by the stetige (continuous / log-return) method: from a 3-year history of the rate \(r_t\), take log returns \(\ln(r_t/r_{t-1})\) and use their standard deviation. This is the natural choice because rate moves are multiplicative — but it is undefined when any rate level is \(\le 0\): \(\ln\) of a non-positive number does not exist, and \(\ln(r_t/r_{t-1})\) breaks the moment either \(r_t\) or \(r_{t-1}\) is negative.

$$ \sigma=\operatorname{sd}\!\Big[\ln\tfrac{r_t}{r_{t-1}}\Big]\quad\text{requires}\quad r_t>0\ \forall t. $$
For EUR (and other currencies) the 3-year history contains negative rates (the post-2014 ECB era), so \(\ln(r_t)\) is undefined and the log/relative-volatility method cannot run.

The Leitfaden's fix: for any currency whose history contains negative rates, apply a +200 bp parallel shift across the whole curve before computing volatility, so that every point becomes positive and the stetige method is defined again:

$$ \tilde r_{t}\;=\;r_{t}+200\text{ bp},\qquad \sigma=\operatorname{sd}\!\Big[\ln\tfrac{\tilde r_t}{\tilde r_{t-1}}\Big]. $$
\(\tilde r_t\) = the shifted rate; +200 bp = +2,00 percentage points added uniformly to every tenor. The shift is applied only to make the log computation possible; the resulting relative volatility is then used in the VaR.
🟣 The +200 bp shift is a modelling workaround, not an economic scenario

ÖRS modelling workaround The +200 bp parallel shift exists for one reason only: to lift all rate levels above zero so the log-return (stetige) volatility is mathematically defined. It is not a +200 bp stress scenario and must not be read as one. What it trades off: it distorts the absolute level of the curve (a rate near 0 looks like 2,0%, so its relative volatility is understated relative to its true tiny base), but it preserves the relative-volatility method the whole framework relies on — a deliberate accuracy-for-tractability swap. Note the +200 bp number coincides with the EBA/GL/2018/02 standard parallel shock and the Supervisory Outlier Test (SOT) shift, but here it serves the volatility computation, not the outlier test. (The genuine outlier/SOT shocks live in the IRRBB outlier reporting, Module 19.)

3.3 FX risk — per-currency VaR and the two-asset diversification formula

For each open currency position the risk is a one-line parametric VaR:

$$ \text{VaR}_c\;=\;|P_c|\cdot \sigma_c^{(250)}\cdot z_q,\qquad \sigma_c^{(250)}=\sigma_c^{\text{(3y)}}\cdot\!\sqrt{\tfrac{250}{T_{\text{base}}}} $$
\(P_c\) = the open position in currency \(c\) (in EUR); \(\sigma_c^{(250)}\) = FX volatility scaled to the 250-day banking-book holding period from the 3-year history; \(z_q=\Phi^{-1}(q)\) = percentile for the confidence (95% … 99,9%). The position enters in absolute value — direction is handled at aggregation.

Undiversified aggregation is a sign-neutral sum — add the per-currency VaRs with no correlations, the most prudent reading:

$$ \text{VaR}_{\text{undiv}}=\sum_c \text{VaR}_c. $$

Diversified aggregation — the figure actually set against the Deckungsmasse — applies the FX correlation matrix. For two currencies the closed form is:

$$ \text{VaR}_{P}\;=\;\sqrt{\,\text{VaR}_A^{2}+\text{VaR}_B^{2}+2\,\rho_{AB}\,\text{VaR}_A\,\text{VaR}_B\,} $$
\(\rho_{AB}\) = correlation between the two FX rates. Generalised to \(n\) currencies, with VaR vector \(\mathbf{v}=(\text{VaR}_1,\dots,\text{VaR}_n)^\top\) and correlation matrix \(\mathbf{C}\): \(\;\text{VaR}_P=\sqrt{\mathbf{v}^\top \mathbf{C}\,\mathbf{v}}\). When all \(\rho=1\), this collapses back to the undiversified sum.
Within-risk-type diversification is the exception, not the rule

This \(\rho<1\) benefit is allowed because it is inside a single risk type (FX). The same logic recurs in spread risk (between rating classes) and IRRBB (between bands/currencies). Across risk types the guide still adds with \(\rho=+1\). Cross-link: Module 4 / Module 12.

3.4 Credit-Spread VaR — the PV2 − PV1 structure

Spreadrisiko measures the loss from credit-spread moves on securities — applied to the "nur Wertpapiere" (securities-only) and "nur Covered Bonds" books, sliced by Forderungsklasse × Ratingklasse (exposure class × rating class). The risk factor is the relative bond volatility per rating class (Markit Overall index, 3-year history) scaled to the 250-day holding period.

$$ \text{Risiko}=PV_2-PV_1,\qquad PV_2=MV\cdot\frac{\big(1+s+CS_{\text{cur}}\big)^{D}}{\big(1+s+CS_{\text{str}}\big)^{D}} $$
\(PV_1\) = current present value (market value \(MV\)); \(PV_2\) = stressed present value; \(s\) = swap rate; \(CS_{\text{cur}}\) = current credit spread of the rating class; \(CS_{\text{str}}=CS_{\text{cur}}\cdot(1+\sigma_{\text{CS}}z_q)\) = stressed credit spread (relative spread volatility, scaled to confidence); \(D=\) Dlfzt, the capital-weighted average maturity per rating class (years). Intuition: widen the spread by its scaled volatility, discount over the average life, and the PV falls — that fall is the VaR.

Diversification across rating classes uses a correlation matrix on the PV-change vector:

$$ \text{CS-VaR}_{\text{div}}=\sqrt{(PV_2-PV_1)^\top\,\mathbf{Corr}\,(PV_2-PV_1)} $$
\((PV_2-PV_1)\) = vector of PV changes per rating class; \(\mathbf{Corr}\) = correlation matrix between rating classes. Within market risk, the correlation between Zinsrisiko and Credit-Spread risk is also considered (per §7).

4 · Worked example

Worked example — FX VaR: one position, then a diversified pair

Setup. An open long position of CHF 10.000.000 ≈ €10,0 m (assume parity for clarity). Its 250-day-scaled FX volatility is \(\sigma_{\text{CHF}}^{(250)}=6{,}0\%\). Quantiles: \(\Phi^{-1}(0{,}95)=1{,}6449\) regulatory standard-normal, \(\Phi^{-1}(0{,}999)=3{,}0902\).

Step 1 — single-currency VaR.

$$ \text{VaR}^{95\%}_{\text{CHF}}=10{,}0\text{ m}\cdot0{,}06\cdot1{,}6449=\textbf{€0,987 m} $$ $$ \text{VaR}^{99{,}9\%}_{\text{CHF}}=10{,}0\text{ m}\cdot0{,}06\cdot3{,}0902=\textbf{€1,854 m} $$

The Extremfall VaR is \(3{,}0902/1{,}6449=1{,}879\times\) the Problemfall VaR — pure vertical quantile scaling (Module 4), nothing else changed.

Step 2 — add a second currency and diversify. Add an open USD 8,0 m ≈ €8,0 m position with \(\sigma_{\text{USD}}^{(250)}=7{,}0\%\). At 95%:

$$ \text{VaR}^{95\%}_{\text{USD}}=8{,}0\text{ m}\cdot0{,}07\cdot1{,}6449=\textbf{€0,921 m}. $$

Undiversified (sign-neutral sum):

$$ \text{VaR}_{\text{undiv}}=0{,}987+0{,}921=\textbf{€1,908 m}. $$

Diversified with correlation \(\rho_{\text{CHF,USD}}=0{,}45\):

$$ \text{VaR}_P=\sqrt{0{,}987^2+0{,}921^2+2\cdot0{,}45\cdot0{,}987\cdot0{,}921}=\sqrt{0{,}974+0{,}848+0{,}818}=\sqrt{2{,}640}=\textbf{€1,625 m}. $$

Diversification benefit = \(1{,}908-1{,}625=\)€0,283 m (≈15%). Only the diversified €1,625 m is set against the Deckungsmasse; the undiversified €1,908 m is reported alongside as the prudent bound. Had \(\rho=1\), the square-root formula would return exactly €1,908 m — the diversified and undiversified figures coincide only at perfect correlation.

5.2 Preisrisiko Handelsbuch — the ×0,71 / ×1,33 scaling

The trading book is minor even for the Landesbanken, so the guide does not run a bespoke VaR on it. It takes the regulatory position-risk own-funds charge (general + specific + option risk), nets out the FX charge (§2, to avoid double-counting the open Devisenposition), and rescales the result — calibrated at the regulatory 99% — to the scenario confidence:

$$ \text{factor}_{\text{Problemfall}}=\frac{\Phi^{-1}(0{,}95)}{\Phi^{-1}(0{,}99)}=\frac{1{,}6449}{2{,}3263}=\mathbf{0{,}71},\qquad \text{factor}_{\text{Extremfall}}=\frac{\Phi^{-1}(0{,}999)}{\Phi^{-1}(0{,}99)}=\frac{3{,}0902}{2{,}3263}=\mathbf{1{,}33}. $$
These are the identical normal-quantile-ratio scalars as the CVA charge (Module 7): ÖRS scaling choice on a regulatory own-funds charge. They are pure vertical VaR consistency (Module 4) — converting a 99%-calibrated charge to 95% / 99,9%.

5.3 Preisrisiko Bankbuch — where equities land

Equities held in the banking book are treated like Beteiligungsrisiko (participation risk, Module 10) if booked there. Indirect or fund holdings get a VaR on their specific market volatility, or — simplified — via an index proxy (e.g. the ATX), scaled to 95% / 99,9%. Where an equity lands therefore depends on its booking, which is exactly the trap below.

5.4 Spreadrisiko — no double-count with credit risk

Spread risk and credit risk look adjacent but capture different things, so there is no double-count:

  • Credit risk (Module 5) captures default and migration driven by the borrower's rating. Spread risk captures the market repricing of credit: a spread can widen with the rating unchanged (a market re-pricing of risk), which credit risk does not see.
  • To keep the two clean, the credit-risk module sets M = 1 year for "nur Wertpapiere" (instead of the default M = 2,5y) — its downgrade/spread risk is moved here, into Credit-Spread VaR, rather than being charged twice. Cross-link: Module 5, §5.4.
  • Within market risk, the Zinsrisiko ↔ Credit-Spread correlation IS considered (per §7) — the bond PV moves with both the risk-free curve and the spread, and the guide recognises they are related.

6 · Nuances & traps

⚠ Trap — 250-day banking book vs NO holding period in the trading book

The banking-book sub-risks (IRRBB, FX, banking-book price, spread) all scale a volatility to a 250-day holding period. The trading book has no holding period at all — it is taken from the regulatory own-funds charge and merely rescaled by quantile ratio (0,71 / 1,33). Mixing the two — e.g. trying to "scale the trading-book charge to 250 days" — is a category error: one is VaR-based, the other own-funds-based.

⚠ Trap — the +200 bp shift is a computational device, not a stress scenario

The +200 bp parallel shift exists only to lift negative rates above zero so the stetige (log) volatility is computable. It is not a +200 bp economic shock and produces no risk number by itself. If an examiner says "the guide applies a 200 bp rate stress", correct them: it shifts the curve to enable the log method, then computes relative volatility on the shifted series. (The number coincides with the EBA/GL/2018/02 / SOT parallel shock, but the purpose here is different.)

⚠ Trap — undiversified (sum) vs diversified (matrix)

Two different numbers, two different roles. The undiversified figure is the sign-neutral sum of per-position VaRs (\(\rho=1\) implicitly) — the prudent bound. The diversified figure uses the correlation matrix (\(\sqrt{\mathbf{v}^\top\mathbf{C}\mathbf{v}}\)) and is the one set against the Deckungsmasse. Reporting the undiversified number as the RTFA charge over-states risk; reporting the diversified one as the "worst case" under-states it.

⚠ Trap — equities live in either Beteiligungsrisiko or Marktrisiko

The same equity can be a participation (Beteiligungsrisiko, Module 10) or a banking-book price risk (Marktrisiko) depending purely on where it is booked. Never assume; check the booking. A holding moved between books moves between risk modules — and double-counting or a gap is the failure mode.

Recap

  • Market risk = four sub-risks: Zinsrisiko Bankbuch (IRRBB), Währungsrisiko (open Devisenposition), Preisrisiko (Handels- & Bankbuch), Spreadrisiko (Credit-Spread VaR). Banking-book holding period 250 Tage; trading book = own-funds charge for position/FX/commodity risk minus the FX charge.
  • IRRBB: \(\text{Risiko}=BW-BW^{\text{sim}}\) per Laufzeitband & currency; Barwert discounts the gap at the spot rate, simulierter Barwert at rate·Vola; relative 3-yr volatility scaled to confidence; bands/currencies combined via a correlation matrix; "Sonstige Währungen" on the EUR curve; options via delta-equivalent gaps.
  • +200 bp shift: a pure computational device — negative rates make \(\ln(r_t)\) undefined, so add 200 bp to make all levels positive and enable the stetige (log) method. Distorts the level, preserves the relative-vol method. Coincides with EBA/GL/2018/02 / SOT but is not a stress scenario. ÖRS workaround.
  • FX: \(\text{VaR}_c=|P_c|\,\sigma_c\,z_q\); undiversified = sign-neutral sum; diversified \(=\sqrt{\mathbf{v}^\top\mathbf{C}\mathbf{v}}\) (two-asset: \(\sqrt{\text{VaR}_A^2+\text{VaR}_B^2+2\rho_{AB}\text{VaR}_A\text{VaR}_B}\)) — one of the few places \(\rho<1\) is allowed.
  • Trading book: own-funds charge − FX charge, ×0,71 (Problemfall) / ×1,33 (Extremfall) — same quantile-ratio scaling as CVA. Spread VaR: \(PV_2-PV_1\) by Forderungs- × Ratingklasse; no double-count with credit risk (spread moves with rating unchanged; "nur Wertpapiere" uses M = 1y in credit risk because its downgrade risk sits here).
🇦🇹 Zusammenfassung (Deutsch)
  • Das Marktrisiko umfasst vier Teilrisiken: Zinsrisiko Bankbuch (IRRBB), Währungsrisiko (offene Devisenposition), Preisrisiko (Handels- und Bankbuch) und Spreadrisiko (Credit-Spread-VaR). Haltedauer im Bankbuch 250 Tage; das Handelsbuch wird über das Eigenmittelerfordernis (Positions-/Fremdwährungs-/Warenrisiko) abzüglich des FX-Erfordernisses abgebildet.
  • IRRBB: Risiko = Barwert − simulierter Barwert je Laufzeitband und Währung; relative Vola (3-Jahres-Historie, auf das Konfidenzniveau skaliert); Aggregation über eine Korrelationsmatrix.
  • Der +200-bp-Parallelshift ist ein reines Rechenhilfsmittel: bei negativen Zinsen ist \(\ln(r_t)\) nicht definiert, daher wird die Kurve um 200 bp verschoben, damit die stetige Methode rechenbar wird — kein Stressszenario. ÖRS-Festlegung.
  • FX: undiversifiziert = vorzeichenneutrale Summe; diversifiziert über die Korrelationsmatrix \(\sqrt{\mathbf{v}^\top\mathbf{C}\mathbf{v}}\) — die diversifizierte Zahl wird der Deckungsmasse gegenübergestellt.
  • Handelsbuch: Eigenmittelerfordernis × 0,71 (Problemfall) / × 1,33 (Extremfall). Spreadrisiko: \(PV_2-PV_1\) je Forderungs- × Ratingklasse, keine Doppelzählung mit dem Kreditrisiko (deshalb M = 1 Jahr für "nur Wertpapiere").
