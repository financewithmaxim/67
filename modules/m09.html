Module 9 — Liquidity risk: the Funding-Liquidity-VaR (FLVaR), the Mischzinssatz, and the LCR/NSFR link

Liquidity risk splits cleanly into two things that demand two different disciplines: short-term Zahlungsunfähigkeit (running out of cash), which is a limit-based matter, and structural Liquiditätspreisrisiko (your own future refinancing gets dearer), which is a capital matter. This module builds the second one — the Funding-Liquidity-VaR (FLVaR) — from its discounting skeleton, derives the Mischzinssatz (mixed funding rate) that prices the gaps, shows the simplified factor approach for small banks, and positions the whole thing against the Pillar-1 ratios LCR and NSFR.

By the end of this module you can…

  • State the two components of Liquiditätsrisiko and explain why only one of them consumes economic capital.
  • Explain the Problemfall / Extremfall asymmetry: limit-monitored liquidity vs the FLVaR — and why it is built that way.
  • Derive the FLVaR as a present-value loss of closing the open Liquiditätsgaps, with the >5y netting, the sign convention, and the diversified CDS-correlation version.
  • Construct the Mischzinssatz and its stressed counterpart from the actual retail/wholesale funding mix (SPI, CDS, IRS).
  • Apply the simplified factor approach by hand, and explain how the FLVaR complements — but does not replace — LCR and NSFR.

1 · Motivation — why liquidity needs two instruments, not one

Imagine the bank survives the worst credit year intact: capital is fine. It can still fail for an entirely different reason — it cannot meet a payment on the day it is due (Zahlungsunfähigkeit, insolvency of payment), or it can only roll its long-dated funding gap at a punishing spread (Liquiditätspreisrisiko, liquidity-price risk). These are not the same hazard and they cannot be managed with one number.

Short-term insolvency-of-payment is a flow problem on a horizon of days: do tomorrow's outflows have cash behind them? You manage it with limits and survival-period ratios, not by reserving capital — capital does not pay a counterparty on Friday. The structural funding-cost risk is a value problem on a horizon of years: if refinancing your long gap becomes more expensive, the present value of that future cost is a loss today. That one is economic capital. The Leitfaden therefore wires liquidity into the Risikotragfähigkeit (risk-bearing capacity) asymmetrically, and the whole module is about that asymmetry and the model that prices the second half.

▶ The two faces of Liquiditätsrisiko

(i) Kurzfristige Zahlungsunfähigkeit — short-term insolvency of payment: not enough liquid means to settle due obligations. (ii) Strukturelles Liquiditäts-/Liquiditätspreisrisiko — higher own refinancing costs arising from the Liquiditätsgap (the structural funding gap). A third notion, market-liquidity risk (you cannot sell an asset without a price concession), is not a separate liquidity charge here — it is already captured through holding periods in market risk and through haircuts in the Liquiditätspuffer (liquidity buffer). So this module is about (i) and (ii) only.

2 · Concept & logic — the Problemfall / Extremfall split

The single most important fact in §5.7 is which liquidity risk enters the RTFA, and how:

Problemfall (95%)Extremfall (99,9%)
What is capturedShort-term liquidity / payment abilityStructural liquidity-price risk (funding-cost)
HowMeasured per the Leitfaden Liquidität; limited & monitored via Kennziffern (key ratios)Quantified as economic capital via the FLVaR (Funding-Liquidity-VaR)
Deckungsmassen charge?NO — no capital is reserved ÖRS choiceYES — the FLVaR consumes Deckungsmasse, but only when there is a funding need
The asymmetry, and why it is correct

The Problemfall does not levy a liquidity capital charge. Short-term payment ability is a discipline, not a loss distribution: you keep a survival period and a buffer, and you steer it with limits and ratios per the Leitfaden Liquidität. Holding capital against "running out of cash next week" would be category-confusion — capital absorbs value losses, it does not generate liquidity on demand. The Extremfall, by contrast, asks an economic question with a genuine value answer: in a near-Liquidationsfall, what is the present-value hit from having to refinance my open long-term gaps at stressed spreads? That is a loss you can put a 99,9% number on, so that is what the FLVaR charges. Only the structural funding-cost risk consumes economic capital.

⚖️ Where the regulatory liquidity standards sit

The Pillar-1 liquidity ratios — LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio: HQLA vs 30-day stressed net outflows, ≥100%) and NSFR (Net Stable Funding Ratio: available vs required stable funding over 1 year, ≥100%) — are regulatory minimum constraints under CRR Part Six. They govern the Problemfall-style short-term and structural adequacy. The FLVaR is the economic structural complement: it does not replicate a ratio; it prices the funding-cost risk of the residual long gap in value terms. See §3.5 for the precise division of labour.

3 · Derivation — building the FLVaR

3.1 The object being modelled: the open liquidity gaps

The FLVaR is a simplified variance–covariance (parametric) approach that measures the present-value loss of closing the open gaps. The basis is the set of open non-cumulative liquidity gaps of the Normalfall (the normal-case liquidity run-off) in maturity bands > 1 year, with bands > 5 years netted (Saldiert).

🟣 Why net everything beyond 5 years? (Saldierung)

Modelling assumptions that bind capital on cash flows 6, 10, 20 years out are poorly validatable — the longer the horizon, the weaker the empirical basis for the run-off profile. So the Leitfaden caps granularity: the longest band is "≥ 5 years" and everything past it is netted into one bucket (Saldierung), exactly as a Liquiditätsablaufbilanz (liquidity run-off balance sheet) collapses its long tail. This is a deliberate ÖRS simplification: it trades long-horizon precision for a defensible, auditable capital number. Bands at or below 1 year are excluded entirely (they belong to the short-term, limit-managed world).

Confidence is 99,9% regulatory-anchored and the holding period is 250 days ÖRS choice (analogy to IRRBB) — the same calibration as the interest-rate risk in the banking book (Module 6), so the liquidity number is VaR-consistent with the rest of the Extremfall.

3.2 The discounting skeleton — PV1, PV2 and the per-band FLVaR

Each open gap is a future cash flow that, to be closed, must be refinanced. Discount it twice: once at today's mixed funding rate, once at the stressed mixed funding rate. The difference in present value is the loss.

$$ PV1 \;=\; \sum_i lgap_i\cdot DF_i\big(\text{Mischzinssatz}\big), \qquad PV2 \;=\; \sum_i lgap_i\cdot DF_i\big(\text{gestresster Mischzinssatz}\big) $$ $$ \boxed{\;\text{Risiko (FLVaR)} \;=\; PV2 \;-\; PV1\;} $$
\(lgap_i\) = open non-cumulative liquidity gap in maturity band \(i\) (\(i\) ranges over bands > 1y, the >5y bands netted into one); \(DF_i(\cdot)\) = the discount factor for band \(i\), i.e. \(DF_i=(1+r)^{-t_i}\) with \(t_i\) the band's mid-maturity in years and \(r\) the applicable rate; Mischzinssatz = the bank's mixed funding rate per band (derived in §3.4); gestresster Mischzinssatz = the same rate after a 99,9% adverse move over the 250-day holding period. The per-band difference \((lgap_i\,DF_i^{stress}-lgap_i\,DF_i^{base})\) is the FLVaR contribution of band \(i\); summing the bands gives the undiversified FLVaR.

3.3 The sign convention — only a funding NEED is a risk

This is the trap that catches everyone. A liquidity gap can point either way:

  • A negative undiversified FLVaR means the bank has a structural Refinanzierungsbedarf (funding need): it is short of stable funding in the long bands and must refinance — when spreads widen, that hurts. This is the risk.
  • A positive undiversified FLVaR means structural over-funding: the bank has more long-term funding than long assets. Cheaper-or-dearer refinancing is not a loss to be capitalised here.
The FLVaR only counts as risk when there is a funding need

Concretely: compute the undiversified FLVaR; if it is negative (a funding need), it flows into the Extremfall Deckungsmassen charge; if it is positive (over-funding), it does not. You never let an over-funded position generate a capital benefit — that would be booking a windfall on cheaper future funding. Liquidity-price risk is asymmetric by design: you capitalise the cost of having to borrow, not the gain from having lent.

3.4 The Mischzinssatz — pricing the gaps at the actual funding mix

The discount rate is not a single market rate: it is the rate at which this bank actually refinances, which is a blend of cheap, sticky retail deposits and pricier wholesale/interbank money. The blend is the Mischzinssatz, and its weights come from the bank's own funding structure.

First the weight. The Primäreinlagenanteil (primary-deposit share) is the share of stable customer funding in total long-term refinancing:

$$ \text{Primäreinlagenanteil} \;=\; \frac{\sum \text{Primäreinlagen}_{>1\text{y}}}{\sum \text{Gesamtrefinanzierung}_{>1\text{y}}} $$
Primäreinlagen (primary deposits) = customer deposits / savings / sight deposits plus the bank's own retail and non-retail issues (own issues are marginal and counted at the primary level); Interbank-Refinanzierung = total refinancing − Primäreinlagen; Gesamtrefinanzierung = total operative funding products only — equity and the liquidity reserve are excluded (they are not "operative" funding you must roll). Everything is taken over the >1y bands, consistent with the FLVaR basis.

Now the rate. The base Mischzinssatz blends a wholesale leg and a retail leg by that share:

$$ \text{Mischzinssatz} \;=\; \underbrace{(IRS + CDS)}_{\text{wholesale leg}}\cdot\big(1-\text{Primäreinlagenanteil}\big) \;+\; \underbrace{SPI}_{\text{retail leg}}\cdot\,\text{Primäreinlagenanteil} $$
IRS = the swap rate (Interest-Rate-Swap) for the band — the risk-free-ish term base; CDS = the Austrian sovereign CDS curve per band (the wholesale credit/funding spread the sector pays above swaps); SPI = the OeNB sub-1y savings indicator (Sparindikator) — the rate retail savers are actually paid. Reading: wholesale funding costs swap + CDS spread; retail funding costs the savings rate; the bank pays a weighted average by how much of each it uses.

The stressed Mischzinssatz pushes both legs into a 99,9% adverse move over the holding period — wholesale and retail funding both get dearer in a stress:

$$ \text{Mischzinssatz}_{\text{gestresst}} \;=\; IRS \;+\; \big(CDS + \text{CDS-Vola}(99{,}9\%;\,\text{Haltedauer})\big)\cdot\big(1-\text{Primäreinlagenanteil}\big) \;+\; \big(SPI + \text{SPI-rel.Vola}(99{,}9\%;\,\text{Haltedauer})\big)\cdot\text{Primäreinlagenanteil} $$
CDS-Vola(99,9%; Haltedauer) = the 99,9%-percentile adverse move of the sovereign CDS spread over the 250-day holding period; SPI-rel.Vola(99,9%; Haltedauer) = the corresponding relative-volatility shock on the savings indicator. The IRS leg is treated as the common base; the spread components (CDS, SPI) carry the stress. Economic story: in a funding stress, both wholesale money (CDS spread blows out) and retail money (you must pay up to keep deposits) become more expensive at once — so the rate you refinance the >1y gaps at jumps, and the PV of that gap-closing cost rises. The FLVaR is exactly that PV hit, \(PV2-PV1\).

3.5 The diversified FLVaR — the CDS correlation matrix

The undiversified FLVaR sums the band contributions as if every band's spread moved in lock-step. In reality, spreads across maturities co-move imperfectly, so the Leitfaden allows a diversified figure via a CDS correlation matrix across the maturity bands:

$$ \text{FLVaR}_{\text{div}} \;=\; \sqrt{\,(PV2-PV1)^{\mathsf T}\cdot CDS\text{-}Corr\cdot (PV2-PV1)\,} $$
\((PV2-PV1)\) = the column vector of per-band FLVaR contributions; \(CDS\text{-}Corr\) = the symmetric correlation matrix of CDS-spread moves between maturity bands (diagonal 1). When all correlations are +1 this collapses to the absolute-summed undiversified figure; with correlations < 1 it is smaller. Like every diversified figure in the Leitfaden, it enters the Extremfall only when there is a funding need (i.e. when the undiversified FLVaR is negative). Cross-risk aggregation is still addition (correlation +1, Module 4); this correlation lives strictly within the liquidity risk, across its bands.

3.6 The simplified factor approach (small institutions)

For small banks the full Mischzinssatz machinery is disproportionate, so the Leitfaden offers a closed-form factor approach — the liquidity analogue of the IRRBB 200bp parallel shift. It assumes full primary-deposit funding (Primäreinlagenanteil = 1), a savings indicator of 1%, and a 100bp volatility scaled to 99,9% over 1 year:

$$ \text{Liquiditätsrisiko} \;=\; \Big(\sum_i \big(Gap_i\cdot \text{Laufzeitband}_{i,\text{Jahre}}\big)\cdot \text{Preisabweichung}\Big)\cdot 75\%\cdot \text{Abzinsungsfaktor} $$
\(Gap_i\) = liquidity gap in band \(i\); \(\text{Laufzeitband}_{i,\text{Jahre}}\) = the band's maturity in years (a duration-like weight); Preisabweichung = the price/rate deviation = indicator × vola = \(0{,}01\) here; the 75% is a fixed haircut ÖRS simplification; Abzinsungsfaktor = the discount factor closing the gaps to present value. The \(Gap_i\cdot\text{Laufzeitband}\) product is a first-order (duration) proxy for the PV sensitivity that PV2−PV1 computes exactly in the full approach.

This whole approach is marked ÖRS simplification: it replaces the bank-specific funding mix, the CDS/SPI curves and the correlation matrix with three fixed assumptions, in exchange for a one-line calculation a small bank can run without a curve library.

4 · Worked example

Worked example A — the simplified factor approach (the Leitfaden's own numbers)

A small bank reports these >1y gaps (in € m), with the band maturities in years:

BandGap (€ m)Laufzeitband (yrs)Gap · Band
1–2y+501,5+75
2–3y+602+120
3–4y−203−60

Σ+85

Step 1 — weighted gap sum. \( \;50\cdot1{,}5 + 60\cdot2 - 20\cdot3 - 10\cdot5 \;=\; 75 + 120 - 60 - 50 \;=\; \mathbf{85}.\)

Step 2 — apply the fixed factors. Preisabweichung = indicator × vola = \(0{,}01\cdot1 = 0{,}01\); haircut \(0{,}75\):

$$ 85 \cdot 0{,}01 \cdot 1 \cdot 0{,}75 \;=\; \mathbf{0{,}6375}\ \ (\text{€ m, in band-sum units}). $$

Step 3 — discount. Multiply by the Abzinsungsfaktor to bring it to present value: Liquiditätsrisiko = 0,6375 · DF. With, say, a representative \(DF=0{,}95\), the charge is \(\approx\) €0,606 m. The discounting is the last step precisely because the gaps are future flows. ÖRS simplification: full primary-deposit funding, 1% indicator, 100bp vola @99,9%/1y, 75% haircut.

Worked example B — a 2-band full FLVaR (sign & diversification)

Two >1y bands, with PV1/PV2 already computed from the base and stressed Mischzinssatz (€ m):

BandPV1PV2PV2 − PV1 (per-band FLVaR)
2–3y100,098,8−1,2
≥5y60,058,5−1,5

Undiversified FLVaR = (−1,2) + (−1,5) = −2,7 € m. The sum is negative ⇒ a Refinanzierungsbedarf (funding need) ⇒ it counts as risk and flows into the Extremfall. (Had the sum been positive — over-funding — it would not be charged.)

Diversified FLVaR. Let the per-band vector be \(d=(-1{,}2,\,-1{,}5)^{\mathsf T}\) and the CDS correlation between the two bands be \(\rho=0{,}6\):

$$ CDS\text{-}Corr=\begin{pmatrix}1&0{,}6\\0{,}6&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \text{FLVaR}_{\text{div}}=\sqrt{d^{\mathsf T}\,CDS\text{-}Corr\,d}. $$ $$ d^{\mathsf T}CDS\text{-}Corr\,d = (-1{,}2)^2 + (-1{,}5)^2 + 2\cdot0{,}6\cdot(-1{,}2)(-1{,}5) = 1{,}44 + 2{,}25 + 2{,}16 = 5{,}85. $$ $$ \text{FLVaR}_{\text{div}} = \sqrt{5{,}85} = \mathbf{2{,}42\ \text{€ m}}. $$

The diversified figure 2,42 is below the undiversified magnitude 2,7 because the bands' spreads do not move in perfect lock-step (\(\rho=0{,}6<1\)). It enters the Extremfall only because the position is a funding need.

5 · In practice (the Leitfaden & the data)

The inputs map to data you already touch as a controller:

  • The gaps come from the Normalfall Liquiditätsablaufbilanz (run-off balance sheet), non-cumulative, bands >1y, >5y netted — the same source feeding the Leitfaden Liquidität Kennziffern.
  • Primäreinlagenanteil is a balance-sheet ratio: stable customer funding + own issues over total operative >1y refinancing, excluding equity and the liquidity reserve.
  • The curves: IRS (swap), the Austrian sovereign CDS curve, and the OeNB SPI savings indicator, plus their 99,9%/250-day volatilities for the stress leg.
  • The result enters the Extremfall Deckungsmassen charge only when the undiversified FLVaR is negative (funding need).

5.1 FLVaR vs LCR vs NSFR — the division of labour

MeasureHorizon & natureQuestion answeredStatus
LCR30 days, stress, ratioDo HQLA cover 30-day stressed net outflows? (≥100%)regulatory Pillar 1
NSFR1 year, structural, ratioIs stable funding ≥ required stable funding? (≥100%)regulatory Pillar 1
FLVaR250d / 99,9%, value (PV)What is the PV loss of refinancing the open >1y gaps at stressed spreads?ÖRS economic, in the Extremfall

LCR and NSFR are adequacy ratios — pass/fail constraints that sit alongside the RTFA. The FLVaR is the economic structural complement: it does not duplicate a ratio; it puts a 99,9% present-value price on the structural funding-cost risk that the ratios constrain but do not value. The short-term, payment-ability side (the LCR's home turf) is the Problemfall's limit-monitored domain per the Leitfaden Liquidität — and carries no capital charge.

6 · Nuances & traps

⚠ Trap — the Problemfall has NO liquidity capital charge

Short-term liquidity is limit-managed and monitored via Kennziffern per the Leitfaden Liquidität; it does not consume Deckungsmasse. Only the Extremfall's structural funding-cost risk (the FLVaR) does. Capitalising "running out of cash next week" is a category error — capital does not produce liquidity on demand.

⚠ Trap — only a NEGATIVE undiversified FLVaR is a risk

A negative undiversified FLVaR = a funding need (Refinanzierungsbedarf) ⇒ it counts. A positive figure = over-funding ⇒ it does not generate a capital benefit. You capitalise the cost of having to borrow, never a windfall from cheaper future funding. Check the sign before you let anything flow into the Extremfall.

⚠ Trap — bands > 5 years are netted (Saldierung)

Everything beyond 5 years is collapsed into a single netted bucket because capital-binding run-off assumptions that far out are not validatable. Do not model 10y and 20y bands separately — the longest band is "≥5y", like a Liquiditätsablaufbilanz. And remember bands ≤1y are excluded entirely.

⚠ Trap — the FLVaR is a funding-COST risk, not a payment-default risk

The FLVaR does not measure the chance you cannot pay (that is the short-term, limit side). It measures the present-value hit from refinancing your >1y gaps at a more expensive (stressed) Mischzinssatz — a price risk. And that rate blends retail (SPI) and wholesale (CDS) by the bank's actual funding mix (the Primäreinlagenanteil): a deposit-heavy bank's stressed rate is dominated by the SPI shock, a wholesale-funded bank's by the CDS shock.

Recap

  • Liquiditätsrisiko = (i) short-term Zahlungsunfähigkeit (payment ability) and (ii) structural Liquiditätspreisrisiko (own refinancing cost from the gap). Market-liquidity risk sits in market-risk holding periods + Liquiditätspuffer haircuts.
  • Asymmetry: Problemfall measures short-term liquidity per the Leitfaden Liquidität, limit-monitored, no Deckungsmassen charge; Extremfall charges the structural funding-cost risk via the FLVaR.
  • FLVaR = \(PV2-PV1\) over open non-cumulative >1y gaps (>5y netted), 99,9% / 250d; \(PV1=\sum lgap_i\,DF_i(\text{Mischzinssatz})\), \(PV2\) at the stressed rate. Negative undiversified ⇒ funding need ⇒ it counts; positive ⇒ over-funding ⇒ no charge.
  • Mischzinssatz = \((IRS+CDS)(1-\text{Primäreinlagenanteil})+SPI\cdot\text{Primäreinlagenanteil}\); stressed adds CDS-Vola and SPI-Vola at 99,9%. Diversified FLVaR = \(\sqrt{(PV2-PV1)^{\mathsf T}\,CDS\text{-}Corr\,(PV2-PV1)}\), used only on a funding need.
  • Simplified factor approach (small banks): \((\sum Gap_i\cdot\text{Band}_i\cdot\text{Preisabweichung})\cdot75\%\cdot DF\); the Leitfaden example \((50\cdot1{,}5+60\cdot2-20\cdot3-10\cdot5)=85\Rightarrow 85\cdot0{,}01\cdot0{,}75=0{,}6375\) (×DF).
  • LCR (≥100%, 30d) and NSFR (≥100%, 1y) are the regulatory ratios; the FLVaR is the economic structural complement, not a regulatory ratio.
🇦🇹 Zusammenfassung (Deutsch)
  • Das Liquiditätsrisiko besteht aus (i) kurzfristiger Zahlungsunfähigkeit und (ii) strukturellem Liquiditätspreisrisiko (höhere eigene Refinanzierungskosten aus dem Liquiditätsgap). Das Marktliquiditätsrisiko steckt in den Haltedauern des Marktrisikos und den Abschlägen im Liquiditätspuffer.
  • Asymmetrie: Im Problemfall wird die kurzfristige Liquidität gemäß Leitfaden Liquidität über Kennziffern limitiert und überwacht — keine Deckungsmassenbelastung. Im Extremfall wird das strukturelle Liquiditätspreisrisiko über den FLVaR kapitalisiert.
  • FLVaR = \(PV2-PV1\) auf den offenen, nicht-kumulierten Gaps >1 Jahr (>5 Jahre saldiert), 99,9% / 250 Tage. Nur ein negativer undiversifizierter FLVaR (Refinanzierungsbedarf) zählt als Risiko; ein positiver (Überdeckung) wird nicht angerechnet.
  • Mischzinssatz = \((IRS+CDS)\cdot(1-\text{Primäreinlagenanteil})+SPI\cdot\text{Primäreinlagenanteil}\); gestresst um CDS-Vola und SPI-Vola (99,9%). Diversifiziert über die CDS-Korrelationsmatrix.
  • Der vereinfachte Faktoransatz für kleine Institute: \((\sum Gap\cdot\text{Laufzeitband}\cdot\text{Preisabweichung})\cdot75\%\cdot\text{Abzinsungsfaktor}\). LCR und NSFR (≥100%) sind die aufsichtlichen Kennzahlen; der FLVaR ist die ökonomische strukturelle Ergänzung.
