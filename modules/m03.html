Module 3 — The two lenses: normative (Pillar 1) vs economic (Pillar 2 / ICAAP)

The ECB's ICAAP asks every bank to look at its capital through two lenses at once — a normative view (can we keep meeting all regulatory demands, even after a bad year?) and an economic view (is there enough economic substance against our own picture of risk?). This module builds those two lenses, anchors them in ICAAP/SREP/P2R/P2G and the capital stack, and shows they are the conceptual root of the Leitfaden's Problemfall and Extremfall — an analogy that is illuminating but not an identity.

By the end of this module you can…

  • State the two ICAAP perspectives the ECB requires — normative vs economic — and what question each answers.
  • Distinguish ICAAP (the bank's process) from SREP (the supervisor's review), and name their legal anchors.
  • Place Pillar 1, P2R, the combined buffer requirement, and P2G in the correct stacking order and say what breaching each one triggers.
  • Explain the MDA trigger and why it maps to the Problemfall yellow/red mechanics of Module 4.
  • Say precisely why economic ≠ regulatory capital — confidence, risk types, correlations — and why the Leitfaden's economic view is conservative, not a diversified internal model.
  • Map normative ↔ Problemfall and economic ↔ Extremfall, and articulate where the analogy breaks.
  • Use proportionality to justify why ~400 small banks run a simplified ICAAP rather than a full internal economic-capital model.

1 · Motivation — why one capital number is never enough

Ask "is this bank adequately capitalised?" and you immediately hit a fork. Adequate against what? Two answers are both legitimate and both necessary:

  • Against the rulebook. The supervisor sets binding minima and buffers. A bank that drifts below them faces automatic restrictions and supervisory measures — independently of whether its own models say it is "really" safe. Capital adequacy here means staying continuously above every regulatory demand, including over a planning horizon and under stress.
  • Against reality as the bank sees it. Pillar 1 charges deliberately omit or undercapture some risks (participations, macro, concentration, IRRBB). The bank's own economic model — its own risk types, its own confidence level, its own correlations — may demand more (or differently shaped) capital than the rulebook. Capital adequacy here means enough economic substance to survive a near-fatal year.
▶ The ECB's answer: run both views, always

The ECB's ICAAP Guide (2018) makes this explicit: an institution must assess capital adequacy under two perspectives in parallel — the normative perspective (a multi-year ability to meet all capital-related legal/regulatory/supervisory requirements and demands, under a baseline and adverse scenarios) and the economic perspective (the institution's own internal view of economic value, covering all material risks, typically at a higher confidence). Neither dominates; they inform each other. This dual-perspective requirement is the conceptual root of everything in Module 4.

The Leitfaden inherits exactly this shape. Its Problemfall is the "stay above the regulatory minimum after a bad year" lens; its Extremfall is the "enough economic substance for our own risk" lens. Holding the ECB's two perspectives in your head first makes the two scenarios feel inevitable rather than arbitrary — and lets you defend them to the Aufsichtsrat in the supervisor's own vocabulary.

2 · Concept & logic — ICAAP, SREP, and the two perspectives

2.1 ICAAP vs SREP — the bank's process vs the supervisor's review

These two acronyms are constantly confused. They are opposite ends of the same conversation.

ICAAPSREP
Whose processThe bank's own internal process.The supervisor's annual review of the bank.
Full nameInternal Capital Adequacy Assessment ProcessSupervisory Review and Evaluation Process
Legal anchorCRD Art. 73 → §39a BWGCRD Art. 97 (run by ECB/SSM or FMA/OeNB)
What it producesThe bank's own assessment that capital covers all material risks (both perspectives).A score of business model, governance, capital risk & liquidity risk → Pillar-2 add-ons (P2R + P2G).
Where in the LeitfadenThe whole RTFA is the sector's simplified ICAAP.Sets the P2R the Problemfall must add to its minimum.

So the flow is: the bank runs its ICAAP (CRD Art. 73 / §39a BWG); the supervisor reviews it through the SREP (CRD Art. 97), scoring four blocks — business model viability, internal governance, risks to capital, risks to liquidity & funding; and the SREP outcome is, among other things, a Pillar-2 Requirement (P2R) and a Pillar-2 Guidance (P2G) bolted onto the bank's Pillar-1 minimum. The ICAAP feeds the SREP; the SREP feeds back a capital add-on the ICAAP must then respect.

⚠ ICAAP ≠ SREP

One is yours, one is theirs. The ICAAP is the bank's self-assessment; the SREP is the supervisor's verdict on it (and on much else). Saying "we did our SREP this quarter" is wrong — you do your ICAAP; the supervisor does the SREP to you. In the sector, the ÖRS Leitfaden is the standardised ICAAP machinery; the FMA/OeNB (or ECB for significant institutions like RBI) run the SREP.

2.2 The two ICAAP perspectives

Normative perspectiveEconomic perspective
QuestionCan we meet all regulatory/supervisory capital demands — Pillar 1 + P2R + buffers (+ P2G as a soft target) — continuously, under baseline and adverse scenarios?Do we have enough economic capital to cover all material risks, on our own internal value basis?
HorizonMulti-year capital planning (typically ≥ 3 years), baseline + adverse.Point-in-time economic value; usually a 1-year risk horizon.

ConfidenceInherited from the regulatory framework (Pillar 1 ≈ 99,9%, but the test is "ratio ≥ requirement").The bank's own choice — often higher (here 99,9%).

The key mapping of this module

Normative perspective ↔ Leitfaden Problemfall; economic perspective ↔ Leitfaden Extremfall. The normative view asks "are we above the regulatory bar (incl. Säule-2 add-on) after a plausibly bad year?" — exactly the Problemfall. The economic view asks "is there enough substance against our own, fuller, higher-confidence risk picture?" — exactly the Extremfall. Hold this mapping; Module 4 turns it into thresholds and traffic lights.

⚠ The mapping is an analogy, not an identity

The Problemfall is not the ECB's full normative perspective. The ECB's normative view is a genuine multi-year capital-planning exercise (project CET1/T1/Total ratios out 3+ years under baseline and adverse paths, checking they never dip below Pillar 1 + P2R + buffers). The Leitfaden's Problemfall is a one-year, 95% unexpected-loss view set against a one-year Deckungsmasse. It captures the spirit of the normative view (stay above the regulatory minimum incl. the Säule-2 add-on after a stress) but compresses the multi-year planning into a single-year quantile. Likewise the Extremfall is at 99,9% but, as we'll see in §6, it is deliberately conservative (correlation +1) rather than a diversified internal model. Treat both mappings as conceptual bridges, and flag the gap when challenged.

3 · Structure — the capital stack and the Pillar-2 add-ons

3.1 P2R vs P2G — binding vs guidance

The SREP delivers two Pillar-2 numbers, and the difference between them is the single most examined point in this area.

P2R — Pillar 2 RequirementP2G — Pillar 2 Guidance
Binding?Yes — must be met like Pillar 1.No — a soft target / supervisory expectation.
Position in stackBelow the buffers (sits directly on top of Pillar 1).On top of everything (above the combined buffer requirement).
What a breach triggersCounts toward the MDA-relevant stack and supervisory measures; a breach is a hard problem (distribution-restriction-adjacent, capital plan, possible licence consequences).Dialogue with the supervisor; no automatic distribution restriction and it does not feed the MDA.
AddressesRisks underestimated by Pillar 1 (the "normal" Pillar-2 risks).Capital needed to withstand stress (a stress-buffer expectation).
⚖️ Why "binding" matters: P2R sits in the MDA stack, P2G does not

P2R is regulatory and binding: it is added to the Pillar-1 own-funds requirement and the combined buffer requirement stacks on top of that. So a P2R shortfall is treated like an own-funds shortfall. P2G is also regulatory in origin but explicitly non-binding: it sits above the buffers and a breach prompts a supervisory dialogue, not an automatic Maximum Distributable Amount restriction. Conflating the two — treating P2G as hard, or P2R as soft — is a classic error in front of senior management.

3.2 The stacking order and the MDA trigger

Capital demands stack from the hard floor upward. Read bottom-to-top: each layer must be filled before the next is considered "available."

   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  P2G   — Pillar 2 Guidance (non-binding)       │   ← breach ⇒ dialogue only
   ├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │  Combined Buffer Requirement (CBR)             │
   │     CCB + CCyB + SyRB + (O/G-)SII              │   ← breach of CBR ⇒ MDA trigger
   ├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤   ═══════ MDA line ═══════
   │  P2R   — Pillar 2 Requirement (binding)        │
   ├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │  Pillar 1 minimum:  8% total / 4,5% CET1       │   ← hard floor (CRR Art. 92)
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
Pillar 1 regulatory — CRR Art. 92: 8% total own funds, of which 4,5% CET1, 6% T1.
P2R regulatory — SREP-set, binding, sits directly above Pillar 1, below buffers.
CBR regulatory — capital conservation buffer (Kapitalerhaltungspuffer, 2,5% CET1) + countercyclical (antizyklischer) buffer + systemic-risk buffer (SyRB) + the higher of the O-SII/G-SII buffers, all CET1, stacked on top of Pillar 1 + P2R.
P2G regulatory — SREP-set, non-binding, on top of the CBR.
MDA line — the Maximum Distributable Amount mechanism (CRD Art. 141) bites the moment you dip into the combined buffer requirement — i.e. you have met Pillar 1 + P2R but eaten into CCB/CCyB/SyRB/SII. Dividends, AT1 coupons and bonuses are then automatically capped.
Why the stacking order maps to the Problemfall traffic light

This is the bridge to Module 4. The Problemfall measures utilisation against a Deckungsmasse defined by where you sit in the stack:

  • Yellow (>90%) is read against cover including the buffers — CRR + P2R + Puffer. Yellow fires when you start eating into the combined buffer requirement, i.e. you cross the MDA line. Yellow ≈ "MDA territory."
  • Red (>95%) is read against cover excluding the buffers — CRR + P2R only. Red fires only when you would breach the hard minimum (Pillar 1 + binding P2R) even after the buffers are spent. Red ≈ "binding-minimum breach."

So the yellow/red denominator switch in the Problemfall is just the capital stack read top-down: yellow strips nothing but warns at the buffer line (MDA), red strips the buffers and tests the binding floor. The exact wording the Problemfall checks — "regulatorische Mindesterfordernisse inklusive Säule 2 Anforderung, falls von der Aufsicht vorgegeben" (regulatory minimum requirements including the Pillar-2 requirement, where set by the supervisor) — is precisely Pillar 1 + P2R. That phrase is why P2R, and only P2R (not P2G), enters the Problemfall denominator.

4 · Worked example — a SREP P2R moving the Problemfall light

Worked example — how a 2% P2R changes the Problemfall but not the Extremfall

Take a small Raiffeisenbank near its limits, with RWAs = €1.000 m. Last year it had no Pillar-2 add-on. This year's SREP imposes a P2R of 2% regulatory (SREP-set). Trace the effect on each lens.

Regulatory demands (CET1-equivalent, on €1.000 m RWA), before vs after the SREP:

LayerRate€ before P2R€ after P2R
Pillar 1 (total, CRR Art. 92)8,0%80,080,0
P2R (SREP)2,0%0,020,0
Combined buffer requirement (CCB+CCyB+SyRB)3,0%30,030,0
Binding stack (P1+P2R)10,0%80,0100,0
Stack incl. buffers (P1+P2R+Puffer)13,0%110,0130,0

Now suppose the bank holds available own funds of €150 m and its Problemfall Risiko = €17 m (the 95% unexpected loss). The Problemfall Deckungsmasse is essentially the free capital — own funds above the regulatory requirement — plus earnings and reserves. Holding earnings/reserves aside to isolate the P2R effect, the "freies Kapital" portion is own funds minus the requirement:

Before P2RAfter P2R of 2%
Available own funds150,0150,0
− Required incl. buffers (yellow basis)110,0130,0
= Freies Kapital (yellow denominator)40,020,0
Problemfall Risiko17,017,0
Auslastung (Risiko / freies Kapital)42,5%85,0%
Traffic light (yellow at >90%)grüngrün (but close)

The point: the SREP-imposed P2R did not touch the bank's risk at all — the €17 m unexpected loss is unchanged. It moved the Problemfall denominator: by raising the regulatory requirement from €110 m to €130 m, it shrank free capital from €40 m to €20 m and doubled utilisation from 42,5% to 85,0%. A slightly larger risk, or a 2,5% P2R, would tip this bank from green into yellow purely through the supervisor's add-on. This is exactly the "regulatorische Mindesterfordernisse inklusive Säule 2 Anforderung, falls von der Aufsicht vorgegeben" clause biting.

And the Extremfall? Unmoved by the P2R. The economic lens is governed by the bank's own 99,9% number and its own coverage potential (anrechenbare Eigenmittel + reserves − Neubewertungsreserve). The SREP P2R is a regulatory-demand concept; it lives in the normative/Problemfall denominator, not in the economic/Extremfall one. So the two lenses move independently — precisely the value of running both.

5 · In practice — economic vs regulatory capital, and proportionality

5.1 Why economic capital differs from regulatory capital

The economic (Extremfall) view is not "the regulatory number with a bigger quantile." The Leitfaden says the Extremfall "ähnelt der regulatorischen Solvabilitätsbetrachtung, hat aber eine umfassendere und sensitivere Risikobetrachtung" — it resembles the regulatory solvency view but has a broader and more sensitive risk treatment. Three knobs are turned:

KnobRegulatory (Pillar 1)Economic (Extremfall)
Confidence99,9% for credit (IRB); other charges at mixed quantiles.The bank's own — here a uniform 99,9% across all material risks ÖRS choice.
Risk typesCredit, market, op, CVA — omits participation, macro, IRRBB; undercaptures concentration.Adds Beteiligungsrisiko, makroökonomisches Risiko, Konzentrationsrisiko (Granularitätsaufschlag), Zinsrisiko Bankbuch and more — a fuller catalogue.
CorrelationsImplicit in the regulatory formulas.The bank's own — here deliberately correlation = +1 (simple addition) across risk types ÖRS choice.
🟣 The Leitfaden's economic view is conservative, not a diversified internal model

A "real" economic-capital model is usually generous to the bank: it claims diversification benefit (correlations < 1) across risk types, often producing less capital than the sum of standalone risks. The Leitfaden does the opposite: it aggregates with correlation = +1, i.e. it simply adds the material single risks (Module 12). That is the most conservative possible aggregation — it gives away no diversification benefit. So the Extremfall is "economic" in the sense of fuller risk scope and higher confidence and own-chosen parameters, but it is not a diversified internal model. This is a deliberate proportionality trade: simplicity and defensibility over theoretical fidelity. The only place correlation < 1 enters is within a risk type (e.g. between currencies, between rating classes, and Zins ↔ Credit-Spread inside market risk).

5.2 Risikoappetit — expressed through the Deckungsmasse choice

Risikoappetit (risk appetite) is normally a statement of how much risk a bank is willing to run. The Leitfaden expresses it indirectly but precisely: through the choice of which Deckungsmasse composition counts in each scenario. The Problemfall counts only earnings + free capital above the regulatory floor + reserves; the Extremfall counts essentially the whole own-funds stack but excludes next year's hoped-for profit and the Neubewertungsreserve. By defining what may serve as cover in each lens, the sector encodes how cautious it wants to be. The appetite also shows up in the thresholds: the ">90% on a single-institution basis is not targeted" rule (Module 4) is an explicit appetite statement — the sector wants members to plan remedial action well before utilisation reaches 100%, regardless of the traffic-light colour.

5.3 Proportionality — the licence for a simplified ICAAP

CRD Art. 73 / §39a BWG require every institution to run an ICAAP covering all material risks. For a globally significant bank that means a full internal economic-capital model with bespoke correlations and risk engines. For the Raiffeisen sector that would mean asking ~400 small Primärbanken each to build and validate such a model — operationally infeasible and statistically unsound (a single small bank has too little data to estimate its own correlations or tail).

⚖️ Proportionality (Proportionalitätsgedanke) is the enabling clause

CRD/EBA proportionality lets the depth of the ICAAP scale with the size and complexity of the institution. The ÖRS uses this to run one standardised, simplified ICAAP — the Leitfaden — across all members, with pooled data (e.g. the LGD data pool), fixed parameters (M = 2,5 years, the 5% op-risk factor, the 5% "sonstige Risiken" buffer), and conservative aggregation (correlation +1) standing in for bank-specific internal models. This is the licence for every simplification later in the course. Whenever you see a fixed ÖRS number replacing a bank-specific estimate, the justification traces back here: proportionality permits a sector-standard simplification, and the conservatism (e.g. correlation +1) buys back the modelling error the simplification introduces.

6 · Nuances & traps

⚠ Trap 1 — P2R is binding, P2G is not

P2R must be met like Pillar 1 and sits below the buffers; a P2R breach is treated like an own-funds shortfall and is MDA-relevant once you eat into the combined buffer. P2G sits on top of the buffers and is non-binding; a P2G breach triggers a supervisory dialogue, not an automatic distribution restriction. Only P2R enters the Problemfall denominator ("inklusive Säule 2 Anforderung") — never P2G.

⚠ Trap 2 — ICAAP ≠ SREP

ICAAP is the bank's process (CRD Art. 73 / §39a BWG); SREP is the supervisor's review (CRD Art. 97) that scores the bank and sets P2R/P2G. The ICAAP feeds the SREP; the SREP feeds back the add-on. The Leitfaden is the sector's ICAAP — it is not a SREP.

⚠ Trap 3 — normative ≈ Problemfall is an analogy, not an identity

The ECB's normative perspective is a multi-year capital-planning exercise (project regulatory ratios out 3+ years, baseline + adverse). The Leitfaden's Problemfall is a one-year, 95% unexpected-loss view. It captures the spirit (stay above the regulatory minimum incl. P2R after a stress) but is not the same instrument. Say "analogous to," not "equal to."

⚠ Trap 4 — the economic view here is conservative, not diversified

"Economic capital" normally connotes a diversified internal model that reduces capital via correlations < 1. The Leitfaden's Extremfall does the opposite: it adds material single risks with correlation +1. It is economic in scope and confidence, not in claiming diversification. Do not describe the Extremfall as a "diversified internal model" — it is a deliberately conservative, simplified one.

Recap

  • The ECB's ICAAP requires two perspectives: normative (multi-year, meet all regulatory demands — P1 + P2R + buffers — under baseline & adverse) and economic (own internal view of all material risks, often higher confidence).
  • Normative ↔ Problemfall; economic ↔ Extremfall — the key mapping, but an analogy: the Problemfall is one-year/95%, not the ECB's full multi-year planning, and the Extremfall is conservative (corr +1), not a diversified internal model.
  • ICAAP is the bank's process (CRD Art. 73 / §39a BWG); SREP is the supervisor's review (CRD Art. 97) that scores the bank and sets P2R (binding) and P2G (guidance).
  • Stack: Pillar 1 (8%/4,5% CET1) → P2R → combined buffer requirement (CCB+CCyB+SyRB+SII) → P2G. The MDA triggers on breaching the combined buffer; that is why Problemfall yellow (incl. Puffer) ≈ MDA line and red (excl. Puffer) ≈ binding-minimum breach.
  • Only P2R enters the Problemfall denominator — the "inklusive Säule 2 Anforderung, falls von der Aufsicht vorgegeben" clause; a SREP P2R moves the Problemfall light without touching the economic Extremfall view.
  • Economic ≠ regulatory capital because the bank uses its own confidence (99,9%), its own risk types (participation, macro, concentration, IRRBB), and its own correlations (+1, conservative).
  • Proportionality lets the ÖRS run one simplified, standardised ICAAP for ~400 small banks — the licence for every later simplification.
🇦🇹 Zusammenfassung (Deutsch)
  • Der ICAAP der EZB verlangt zwei Perspektiven: die normative (mehrjährig: Erfüllung aller regulatorischen Anforderungen — Säule 1 + P2R + Puffer — unter Basis- und Stressszenario) und die ökonomische (interne Sicht aller materiellen Risiken, oft höhere Konfidenz).
  • Normativ ↔ Problemfall, ökonomisch ↔ Extremfall — die zentrale Zuordnung, aber eine Analogie: der Problemfall ist 1-jährig/95%, nicht die volle Mehrjahresplanung; der Extremfall ist konservativ (Korrelation +1), kein diversifiziertes internes Modell.
  • ICAAP = Prozess der Bank (CRD Art. 73 / §39a BWG); SREP = aufsichtliche Bewertung (CRD Art. 97) → setzt P2R (verbindlich) und P2G (Empfehlung).
  • Stapelung: Säule 1 → P2R → kombinierte Pufferanforderung (KEP+antizykl.+SyRB+SII) → P2G. Der MDA-Mechanismus greift beim Anbruch der Pufferanforderung — daher Problemfall gelb (inkl. Puffer) ≈ MDA-Linie, rot (exkl. Puffer) ≈ Bruch des harten Mindesterfordernisses.
  • Ökonomisches ≠ regulatorisches Kapital wegen eigener Konfidenz (99,9%), eigener Risikoarten (Beteiligung, Makro, Konzentration) und eigener Korrelationen (+1, konservativ). Die Proportionalität erlaubt der ÖRS einen vereinfachten Standard-ICAAP für ~400 Banken.
