Module 2 — Own funds (Eigenmittel): CET1 / AT1 / T2, deductions and the buffer stack

Module 1 said "capital absorbs the surprise." This module makes capital precise: what legally counts as Eigenmittel (own funds), in what quality tiers, after which deductions, with which buffers stacked on top — and how every one of those line items reappears, by position number, in the Leitfaden's Deckungsmassen (cover-fund) lists.

By the end of this module you can…

  • Build the own-funds hierarchy — CET1 → AT1 → T2 → Gesamtkapital — and say which tier absorbs loss going concern vs gone concern.
  • State the three Pillar-1 minimum ratios (4,5% / 6% / 8%) and derive why each tier has its own floor.
  • List the major CET1 deductions (CRR Art. 36/33/34) and connect them to the Leitfaden's freies-Kapital formulae.
  • Stack the buffers (CCB, CCyB, SyRB, G/O-SII) on top of the 8% and explain the MDA / Ausschüttungssperre logic that drives the yellow-vs-red traffic-light split (cross-link M4).
  • Read the leverage ratio as a non-risk-based backstop, and map each concept to a §8.1 Deckungsmasse position number.
  • Name the ÖRS / Raiffeisen specialties: the €5m freies-Kapital floor, the Haftsummenzuschlag and the IPS-Beitrag deduction.

1 · Motivation — why "capital" needs a quality grade

Module 1 established that owners must keep enough Eigenmittel (own funds) in front of the risk that they, not depositors, absorb losses first. But not all "capital" is equally good at absorbing loss. A perpetual common share that pays a discretionary dividend can soak up losses indefinitely while the bank keeps trading. A dated subordinated bond that matures in three years and pays a fixed coupon cannot: it must be repaid, it ranks above equity, and stopping its coupon is a default event. Both sit on the liability side above senior creditors — but only one keeps a living bank alive.

▶ The core idea: loss absorption has two regimes

Regulation grades capital by when it absorbs loss:

  • Going concern (Unternehmensfortführung) — the instrument absorbs loss while the bank continues to operate: it can take a write-down or stop paying out, without triggering insolvency. This is Tier 1 (CET1 + AT1).
  • Gone concern (Liquidation/Abwicklung) — the instrument only absorbs loss after the bank fails, by ranking below depositors and senior creditors in the wind-up. This is Tier 2 (T2).

The whole tier hierarchy is just this distinction made legal. Hold it: it is also the conceptual seed of the Problemfall (a going-concern, can-we-survive-a-bad-year question) vs the Extremfall (a gone-concern, Liquidationsfall question) split you met in M1 and will formalise in M3/M4.

You will spend most of this course computing the numerator-less side of an inequality — the risk. This module is the other side: the Deckungsmasse. The Leitfaden's freies Kapital (free capital) and anrechenbare Eigenmittel (eligible own funds) are built directly from the CRR own-funds stack, line by line. If you cannot read that stack, you cannot read the cover fund — and M13 (the Deckungsmassen module) becomes a wall of position numbers with no meaning.

2 · Concept & logic — the own-funds hierarchy (CRR Part Two)

The CRR (Regulation (EU) 575/2013), Part Two ("Eigenmittel"), defines the tiers. The architecture is a nested sum:

$$ \underbrace{\text{CET1}}_{\text{Art. 26–50}} \;\subseteq\; \underbrace{\text{T1} = \text{CET1}+\text{AT1}}_{\text{Art. 25, 51–61}} \;\subseteq\; \underbrace{\text{Gesamtkapital} = \text{T1}+\text{T2}}_{\text{Art. 4(1)(118), 62–71}} $$
CET1 = Common Equity Tier 1 / hartes Kernkapital (the highest-quality, purely going-concern capital). AT1 = Additional Tier 1 / zusätzliches Kernkapital (going-concern but with debt-like features). T1 = Kernkapital (going-concern total). T2 = Tier 2 / Ergänzungskapital (gone-concern). Gesamtkapital = Eigenmittel insgesamt / anrechenbare Eigenmittel = total own funds. Tiers are cumulative: every euro of CET1 also counts toward T1 and toward total capital.

2.1 CET1 — hartes Kernkapital (CRR Art. 26)

The purest loss absorber. It is permanent, freely available, and the last to be repaid in any scenario. Its main constituents (Art. 26(1)):

  • Kapitalinstrumente — common shares / cooperative shares (Geschäftsanteile for a Raiffeisenbank), meeting the 14 eligibility criteria of Art. 28 (perpetual, most subordinated, fully discretionary distributions, loss absorbing first).
  • Agio — share premium related to those instruments.
  • Einbehaltene Gewinne (retained earnings) — accumulated, audited profit not distributed. Deckungsmasse position 7812000.
  • Sonstige Rücklagen (other reserves) and the Fonds für allgemeine Bankrisiken (§57 BWG fund for general banking risks).
  • Kumuliertes sonstiges Ergebnis (accumulated other comprehensive income, AOCI) — e.g. OCI revaluation effects, before the prudential filters of §2.3 strip parts of it back out.

2.2 AT1 — zusätzliches Kernkapital (CRR Art. 51–52)

Going-concern, but engineered from perpetual subordinated debt rather than equity. To qualify it must be:

  • Perpetual — no maturity date, no incentive to redeem (call only with supervisory permission, Art. 77/78);
  • Coupon-discretionary — the bank can cancel distributions at any time, non-cumulatively, without it being a default (this is what makes it loss-absorbing while alive);
  • fitted with a loss-absorption trigger: a CoCo (contingent convertible) mechanism that, when CET1 falls below a contractual threshold (Art. 54, at least 5,125% CET1), either writes the instrument down or converts it into CET1.

So AT1 is the "middle" tier: cheaper than equity for the issuer, but able to recapitalise a still-trading bank by switching off coupons and, in stress, converting to hard equity.

2.3 T2 — Ergänzungskapital (CRR Art. 62–63)

Gone-concern capital: dated subordinated debt with an original maturity of at least 5 years regulatory, amortised in regulatory recognition over its final five years (Art. 64). It pays a fixed/contractual coupon (cancelling it is a default), so it cannot absorb loss in a living bank — only in liquidation/resolution, where it ranks below senior creditors and depositors but above T1.

The loss-absorption ladder in one line

CET1 (write-down anytime) → AT1 (cancel coupon / convert at trigger) → T2 (subordination in wind-up). Going concern is CET1 + AT1; gone concern adds T2. This ladder is why the minimum ratios are tiered (next section) and why T2 can never be used to plug a CET1 or buffer shortfall — a gone-concern instrument cannot do a going-concern job.

2.4 The three minimum ratios (CRR Art. 92)

Each ratio is "tier ÷ Gesamtrisikobetrag." The denominator is the Gesamtrisikobetrag (total risk exposure amount, TREA — the sum of RWA for credit, market, operational and CVA risk; Deckungsmasse position 7980000). The CRR sets three floors so that the bank holds not just enough capital but enough of the right quality:

$$ \frac{\text{CET1}}{\text{Gesamtrisikobetrag}} \ge 4{,}5\% , \qquad \frac{\text{T1}}{\text{Gesamtrisikobetrag}} \ge 6\% , \qquad \frac{\text{Gesamtkapital}}{\text{Gesamtrisikobetrag}} \ge 8\% $$
All three are regulatory (CRR Art. 92(1)). The 8% is the historic Basel-I anchor (M1). The tiering means: of the 8% total, at least 6% must be going-concern (T1), and of that at least 4,5% must be the hardest form (CET1). Equivalently, AT1 may fill at most 1,5pp of the 6%, and T2 at most 2pp of the 8%.
⚖️ Why three floors, not one?

A single 8% floor would let a bank meet it with 8% of dated subordinated debt — capital that does nothing for a living bank. The 2008 crisis showed exactly this failure: banks were "8% capitalised" but had almost no hard equity to absorb going-concern losses. Basel III's answer was to push the quality up by raising the CET1 sub-floor to 4,5% and the T1 sub-floor to 6%. So the three ratios encode a quality constraint, not just a quantity one — and the buffers (§5) sit entirely in CET1 to reinforce it.

3 · Structure — deductions, and how CET1 is actually computed

Gross book equity overstates loss-absorbing substance. The CRR therefore deducts from CET1 anything that would not be there — or not be worth its book value — exactly when you needed it (in stress). The headline computation is:

$$ \text{CET1} = \big(\text{Kapital} + \text{einbehaltene Gewinne} + \text{Rücklagen} + \text{AOCI}\big)\; -\; \underbrace{\text{Prudential filters}}_{\text{Art. 32–35}} \;-\; \underbrace{\text{Abzugsposten}}_{\text{Art. 36–48}} $$
Prudential filters adjust accounting equity for items the supervisor distrusts; Abzugsposten (deductions) remove assets with no reliable stress value. Both reduce CET1 directly. The position list that follows is — almost item-for-item — the Leitfaden's Extremfall freies-Kapital adjustments (§5).

3.1 Prudential filters (CRR Art. 32–35)

  • Eigenes Kreditrisiko (own-credit filter, Art. 33) — gains/losses on the bank's own liabilities driven by changes in its own credit spread are filtered out. (Otherwise a bank's CET1 would rise as its own creditworthiness deteriorates — perverse.) Art. 33 also removes cash-flow-hedge reserves (Zahlungsstrom-Absicherungsrücklagen) that do not reflect fair-valued assets.
  • Prudent valuation / AVA (Art. 34, applying Art. 105) — fair-valued positions get an Additional Valuation Adjustment: the difference between the accounting fair value and a prudent (conservative, exit-price) value is deducted from CET1. This is the vorsichtige Bewertung haircut.
  • Neubewertungsreserve (revaluation reserve) — its CET1 treatment depends on the item; under IFRS, fair-value/OCI revaluation reserves flow through AOCI and are subject to filters. The Leitfaden strips the Neubewertungsreserve out of the Deckungsmasse in both scenarios (§5) — a deliberately conservative choice, because a revaluation gain is fragile cover.

🟣 Why cross-holdings matter so much in a Raiffeisen sector

The Raiffeisen sector is a web of mutual participations: a Raiffeisenbank holds shares in its Raiffeisenlandesbank, which holds shares in RBI, and so on. Under Art. 36(1)(h)/(i), holdings in other financial-sector entities — including other Raiffeisen entities — are deducted from CET1 precisely to stop the same euro of capital being counted at two banks at once. In an IPS full of internal participations, these deductions are not a footnote — they can be a large slice of the stack, and getting them wrong systematically overstates sector capital. This is the conceptual seed of the IPS consolidation adjustments in M12 (eliminate intra-circle participations) and the double-counting problem in M13.

4 · Worked example — three ratios, the buffer test, and freies Kapital

Worked example — a small Raiffeisenbank's capital position

Inputs (post-deduction, anrechenbare figures): CET1 = €60m, AT1 = €5m, T2 = €15m, Gesamtrisikobetrag (RWA) = €600m. Assume the only buffer in force is the Kapitalerhaltungspuffer (CCB) = 2,5% regulatory; CCyB/SyRB = 0 for simplicity.

Step 1 — the tier totals. \(\text{T1}=\text{CET1}+\text{AT1}=60+5=\)€65m. \(\;\text{Gesamtkapital}=\text{T1}+\text{T2}=65+15=\)€80m.

Step 2 — the three ratios.

$$ \frac{\text{CET1}}{\text{RWA}}=\frac{60}{600}=10{,}0\%,\quad \frac{\text{T1}}{\text{RWA}}=\frac{65}{600}=10{,}83\%,\quad \frac{\text{Gesamtkapital}}{\text{RWA}}=\frac{80}{600}=13{,}33\%. $$

All three clear their Pillar-1 minima (4,5% / 6% / 8%) comfortably.

Step 3 — the buffer is CET1-only. The CCB of 2,5% must be met with CET1 on top of the 4,5% CET1 minimum. So the CET1 requirement incl. buffer is \(4{,}5\%+2{,}5\%=7{,}0\%\) of RWA = €42m. CET1 of €60m exceeds it — buffer intact, no distribution restriction.

Step 4 — "freies Kapital" (CET1 above the requirement). Define the binding requirement as the largest CET1 demand. Three constraints (assume no P2R for this example):

  • CET1 minimum: 4,5% · 600 = €27m;
  • CET1 needed so AT1 doesn't have to fill T1: T1 ≥ 6% · 600 = €36m, AT1 only €5m ⇒ CET1 ≥ €31m;
  • CET1 needed for total: Gesamtkapital ≥ 8% · 600 = €48m, with AT1+T2=€20m ⇒ CET1 ≥ €28m;
  • plus the CCB 2,5% in CET1 on top of 4,5% ⇒ €42m.

The binding figure is €42m (the buffered CET1 requirement). So, before the ÖRS-specific stripping:

$$ \text{CET1 above requirement} = 60 - 42 = \textbf{€18m.} $$

The Leitfaden's freies Kapital for the Problemfall then takes this surplus and subtracts the Neubewertungsreserve, subject to the €5m legal floor ÖRS / legal. If, say, the Neubewertungsreserve were €4m, freies Kapital = €18m − €4m = €14m (well above the €5m floor). (In the Extremfall the build is different — see §5.)

Sensitivity — why T2 cannot rescue a buffer breach. Suppose CET1 fell to €38m (everything else unchanged). All three Pillar-1 ratios still pass (CET1 6,33% ≥ 4,5%; total 12,17% ≥ 8%). But the buffered CET1 requirement is €42m, so CET1 is €4m short of the combined-buffer line — even though there is €15m of spare T2. T2 is gone-concern; it cannot count toward the CET1 buffer. The bank is inside its buffer ⇒ MDA restriction bites (next section).

⚖️ Verify the live rates against the FMA

The 2,5% CCB is fixed in the CRD regulatory, but the CCyB, SyRB and O-SII rates are set by the national authority (in Austria the FMA, on FMSB advice) and change over time. Any rate you quote for those post-dates the 09/2024 Leitfaden — verify against the current FMA / FMSB list before using it in a live RTFA. Treat the buffer rates as time-varying inputs, the buffer mechanics as stable.

5.2 MDA / Ausschüttungssperre — why yellow includes buffers and red excludes them

The buffers are not hard solvency floors; they are a distribution gate. Define the combined buffer requirement (CBR) = CCB + CCyB + max(SyRB, G/O-SII as applicable). If CET1 falls into the CBR (above the hard 4,5%+P2R line but below 4,5%+P2R+CBR), the bank is not insolvent — but it enters the Maximum Distributable Amount (MDA) regime (CRD Art. 141): dividends, AT1 coupons and bonuses are capped by a formula that tightens as the breach deepens. In German: the Ausschüttungssperre (distribution restriction).

Cross-link M4 — this is exactly the traffic-light logic

The Leitfaden's Problemfall traffic light tests utilisation against CRR + P2R + Puffer for the yellow line (>90%) but against CRR + P2R only (buffers excluded) for the red line (>95%). That is the MDA logic encoded: breaching the buffer stack is a serious-but-recoverable warning (yellow — you lose distribution freedom); breaching the minimum below the buffers is the true red line (you are below the regulatory floor). M4 turns this into the full traffic-light arithmetic; here just hold why the buffer belongs in the yellow test and not the red one.

5.3 Leverage ratio — the non-risk-based backstop (CRR Part Seven)

Every ratio above divides by risk-weighted assets — so a bank that games its models can look strong. The Leverage Ratio (CRR Part Seven, Art. 429 ff.) is the deliberately model-free backstop:

$$ \text{Leverage Ratio} = \frac{\text{T1 (Kernkapital)}}{\text{Gesamtrisikopositionsmessgröße (total exposure measure)}} \ge 3\% $$
Numerator = T1 (not total capital). Denominator = unweighted total exposure (on-balance + derivatives + SFTs + off-balance via CCFs) — no risk weights. The 3% minimum is regulatory (CRR Art. 92(1)(d)). This is the guard against übermäßige Verschuldung (excessive leverage): the risk that a thin-but-RWA-flattering balance sheet is in fact dangerously geared. The Leitfaden lists the Leverage-Ratio positions (e.g. LU/LK 5985064 / 5985066).

5.5 From the stack to "freies Kapital" — preview of M13

The Leitfaden builds freies Kapital (free capital) differently in the two scenarios, but always from this own-funds stack:

$$ \textbf{Problemfall:}\quad \text{freies Kapital} = \underbrace{\big(\text{CET1} - \text{CET1-Erfordernis inkl. P2R}\big)}_{\text{CET1 above the requirement}} \;-\; \text{Neubewertungsreserve} $$ $$ \textbf{Extremfall:}\quad \text{freies Kapital} = \text{anrechenbare Eigenmittel} - \text{Neubewertungsreserve} + \text{nachrangiges FK} + \text{Abzugsposten Beteiligungen} $$
Problemfall (going concern, normative) counts only CET1 surplus above the requirement — because that is the going-concern cushion — and strips the fragile Neubewertungsreserve. The €5m legal floor applies.
Extremfall (gone concern, Liquidationsfall) takes all anrechenbare Eigenmittel — including nachrangiges Fremdkapital (subordinated debt, i.e. T2, which does absorb loss in liquidation) — and adds back the Abzugsposten Beteiligungen (the participation deductions), because in a liquidation those participations have a recoverable value that is available to creditors. It still strips the Neubewertungsreserve. Each line traces directly to §3 of this module — that is the point of learning the stack first.

5.6 ÖRS / Raiffeisen specialties

  • €5m freies-Kapital floor ÖRS / legal — a legal minimum free-capital amount each member must show; binds when a tiny bank's computed surplus is below it.
  • Haftsummenzuschlag (cooperative liability supplement) ÖRS — an Austrian Raiffeisen specialty: the additional liability that cooperative members owe (Genossenschaft uncalled liability), recognised as a sector-specific addition (Pos. 410). It reflects the mutual-liability nature of a Genossenschaft that a plain joint-stock bank does not have.
  • B-IPS / IPS-Beitrag deduction ÖRS — the contribution to the (B-)IPS support fund (Pos. 420) is treated as a deduction, recognising that committed IPS contributions are not freely available own funds.

6 · Nuances & traps

⚠ Trap 1 — T2 cannot do a CET1 or buffer job

T2 (Ergänzungskapital) counts toward the 8% total ratio and toward gone-concern cover — but it is useless for the 4,5% CET1 minimum, the 6% T1 minimum, and every buffer (all CET1). A bank can be flush with T2, pass all three Pillar-1 ratios, and still be inside its combined buffer with MDA distribution restrictions biting (see the §4 sensitivity). Surplus T2 never rescues a CET1 shortfall.

⚠ Trap 2 — cross-holdings double-count capital across the IPS

In a sector built on mutual participations, a holding in another financial-sector entity that is not deducted means the same euro of capital is counted twice — once at the holder, once at the holdee. Art. 36(1)(h)/(i) deductions exist precisely to prevent this. At the IPS level it is even sharper: M12's consolidation must eliminate intra-circle participations and intra-group exposures, or aggregate sector capital is systematically overstated. Treat every Raiffeisen-on-Raiffeisen participation as a deduction candidate.

⚠ Trap 3 — the Neubewertungsreserve is stripped in BOTH Deckungsmassen

A revaluation reserve (Neubewertungsreserve) looks like equity, but its value is market-dependent and can vanish in exactly the stress you are testing. The Leitfaden therefore subtracts it from the cover fund in both the Problemfall and the Extremfall. Do not count it as free capital in either scenario — a common over-statement of the Deckungsmasse.

⚠ Trap 4 — buffers stack on top of the 8%, not inside it

The CCB/CCyB/SyRB are additional CET1 above the 4,5% CET1 minimum and the 8% total — never carved out of it. The CET1 needed to satisfy a 2,5% CCB is 4,5% + 2,5% = 7,0% of RWA of CET1, on top of whatever CET1 is also doing duty inside the 6% T1 and 8% total floors. Mixing the buffer into the 8% understates the true CET1 demand.

Recap

  • Own funds are tiered by loss-absorption regime: CET1 + AT1 = going concern (T1); + T2 = gone concern (Gesamtkapital). CET1 (hard equity, retained earnings, reserves, AOCI) → AT1 (perpetual, coupon-discretionary, CoCo trigger) → T2 (dated subordinated, ≥5y).
  • Three regulatory Pillar-1 minima on the Gesamtrisikobetrag: CET1 ≥ 4,5%, T1 ≥ 6%, Total ≥ 8% (CRR Art. 92) — they enforce quality, not just quantity.
  • CET1 is computed after prudential filters (own-credit/cash-flow-hedge Art. 33; AVA Art. 34/105) and deductions (goodwill/intangibles, DTAs, financial-sector holdings — Art. 36). These same items drive the Leitfaden's freies-Kapital build.
  • Buffers (CCB 2,5% reg; CCyB/SyRB/G-O-SII authority-set — verify against FMA) sit on top of the 8%, all in CET1. Breaching the combined buffer triggers the MDA / Ausschüttungssperre — the reason the yellow test includes buffers and the red test excludes them (M4).
  • The Leverage Ratio = T1 / total exposure ≥ 3% (CRR Part Seven) is the non-risk-based backstop against übermäßige Verschuldung.
  • Every concept maps to a §8.1 position (Gesamtrisikobetrag 7980000, CET1 7810002, einbehaltene Gewinne 7812000, CCB 8127010, CCyB 8127030, SyRB 8128000…); ÖRS specialties: €5m floor, Haftsummenzuschlag (410), IPS-Beitrag (420). freies Kapital = CET1-above-requirement − Neubewertungsreserve (Problemfall) / anrechenbare EM − Neubewertungsreserve + nachrangiges FK + Abzugsposten Beteiligungen (Extremfall) — full build in M13.
🇦🇹 Zusammenfassung (Deutsch)
  • Eigenmittel sind nach Verlustabsorption gestuft: hartes Kernkapital (CET1) und zusätzliches Kernkapital (AT1) tragen going concern; Ergänzungskapital (T2, ≥5 Jahre) erst gone concern. Mindestquoten am Gesamtrisikobetrag: CET1 ≥ 4,5%, Kernkapital ≥ 6%, Gesamt ≥ 8% (CRR Art. 92).
  • CET1 wird nach aufsichtlichen Filtern (eigenes Kreditrisiko/Cashflow-Hedge Art. 33; vorsichtige Bewertung/AVA Art. 34/105) und Abzugsposten (Geschäfts- und Firmenwert, latente Steuern, Beteiligungen an Finanzunternehmen — Art. 36) berechnet.
  • Die Puffer (Kapitalerhaltungspuffer 2,5%, antizyklischer Puffer, Systemrisikopuffer) liegen über den 8% und bestehen aus CET1. Ein Unterschreiten löst die Ausschüttungssperre (MDA) aus — deshalb enthält der gelbe Test die Puffer, der rote nicht (M4). Aktuelle Pufferraten bei der FMA verifizieren.
  • Die Leverage Ratio = Kernkapital / Gesamtrisikopositionsmessgröße ≥ 3% (CRR Teil 7) sichert gegen übermäßige Verschuldung.
  • ÖRS-Besonderheiten: 5-Mio.-€-Untergrenze des freien Kapitals, Haftsummenzuschlag (Pos. 410) und IPS-Beitrag (Pos. 420). Die Neubewertungsreserve wird in beiden Deckungsmassen abgezogen.
