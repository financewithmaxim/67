Module 14 — Data, reporting, frequency & Kennziffern (§8, §9)

This is the plumbing module: every number you have computed across Modules 5–13 is fed by a concrete data source — and almost all of those sources are recycled supervisory reporting (COREP / FINREP / VERA) plus one credit-risk supplement (the R1). Understand which position feeds which formula, on what cadence, and you understand how the whole Früherkennung actually runs in production.

By the end of this module you can…

  • Explain the design principle — reuse supervisory formats to minimise manual effort — and why it makes COREP/FINREP data quality a first-order driver of RTFA quality.
  • Read the data-source → risk map: name the input that feeds each risk type and the Module it lands in.
  • Trace the credit-risk data flow (R1 / ÖRS Datenspezifikation), the 10-BAT-each-way timeline, and why the R1 superseded the Excel credit inputs.
  • Define the §9 Kennziffern — Loan/Deposit, CIR, Asset Encumbrance, NPE, and the IRRBB Supervisory Outlier Test — and say which are regulatory and which are ÖRS choices.
  • Trace one quarter of credit risk end-to-end, from R1 submission to the RTFA vs the Deckungsmasse.

1 · Motivation — why the plumbing matters

A risk framework that requires hundreds of Raiffeisenbanken to hand-key inputs every quarter will fail on two fronts: it will be slow, and it will be wrong. The Früherkennung's central operational decision is therefore not a modelling one — it is a data-sourcing one: wherever a figure already exists in a supervisory report the bank is legally obliged to file, the Früherkennung reuses that exact format rather than asking for a fresh number. The catalogue of those formats is aufsichtliches Meldewesen (supervisory reporting): COREP (own funds), FINREP (financial reporting), VERA (the Austrian Vermögens-, Erfolgs- und Risikoausweis reporting suite), supplemented for credit risk by the R1.

▶ The prize and its price

The prize: minimal manual effort, automatic alignment with what the supervisor already sees, and a quarterly cadence that piggybacks on the OeNB-Meldekalender (OeNB reporting calendar) deadlines. The price: the RTFA is only as good as the supervisory data feeding it. A FINREP misclassification or a COREP own-funds error propagates straight into the Deckungsmasse and the traffic light. Data quality of COREP/FINREP directly drives RTFA quality — that is the single most important sentence in this module.

2 · Concept — the reuse principle and the reporting envelope

The Früherkennung sits on a quarterly Rhythmus (rhythm). Each quarter, on the OeNB-Meldekalender deadline, the institution assembles its figures — overwhelmingly from formats it is filing anyway — and reports them to the ÖRS. The reporting envelope is fixed and worth memorising:

Reporting parameterValueNature
CadenceQuarterly (quartalsweise), aligned to OeNB-MeldekalenderÖRS choice
ScopeUnconsolidated (unkonsolidiert) AND, where applicable, consolidated (konsolidiert)regulatory scopes
Reporting addresssrg-office@rbinternational.comÖRS choice
CurrencyAll amounts in EURÖRS choice

Two scopes matter because the RTFA is run at several consolidation levels (Module 12): the same bank shows a different Auslastung (utilisation) solo, as a §30-BWG KI-Gruppe (credit-institution group), and within the IPS. The data has to arrive at both the unconsolidated and the consolidated level to support all of those views.

The R/SQL framing — what is actually new for you

You already pull COREP and R1 positions in SQL every day; this module does not teach you that. The value here is the mapping: each R1 / COREP / FINREP position is a column you can already query, but knowing which position feeds which formula — e.g. that COREP own-funds position 7810002 (CET1) lands in the Deckungsmasse, not the risk side — is what lets you trace a wrong traffic light back to its source column. Learn the dependency graph, not the query.

3 · The credit-risk data flow (R1 / ÖRS Datenspezifikation)

Credit risk is the exception to "just reuse a supervisory report": it needs more granularity than COREP carries (exposures by customer, internal collateral, FX flags, top GvK). So since 30.6.2023 it runs on a dedicated supplement, the R1 format, defined by the ÖRS Datenspezifikation (data specification). The flow is a closed loop with a symmetric deadline:

Step 1 — Submission (10 BAT). Quarter-ultimo data (Quartalsultimo) from the Landesorganisationen / RSG, in the R1 format, within 10 BAT (Bankarbeitstage, banking business days) after quarter-end, reported per Landesgruppen-Mitglied (each KI-Gruppe).

Step 2 — Central processing. The ÖRS runs Qualitätssicherung (quality assurance) and the central credit-risk calculation (EL, UL, FX add-on, HHI surcharge — Module 5).

Step 3 — Results back (10 BAT). Results returned to the Landesorganisationen within 10 BAT, both aggregated and detailed, at a granularity equal to the input granularity.
🟣 The R1 superseded the Excel credit inputs

Per the September-2024 version history, the Excel "Leitfaden Früherkennung" workbook no longer needs the credit-risk inputs filled in — the R1 supersedes them. The reason is double-keying: the credit numbers already arrive in the richer R1, so re-entering them in the Excel template was redundant and a source of error. ÖRS choice Note this is a process decision, not a methodology change; the IRB formula behind it is unchanged.

⚠ Trap — 10 BAT each way, both legs

The 10-BAT deadline applies to both legs: 10 BAT to submit the R1, then 10 BAT for the ÖRS to return results. People remember the submission deadline and forget the return leg. End-to-end, a quarter-ultimo credit number is in the banks' hands roughly 20 BAT after quarter-end — that latency is why the RTFA is a quarterly monitoring tool, not a real-time one.

Three observations a reviewer will test you on. First, one source can feed several risks (FINREP feeds both op risk and the Deckungsmasse). Second, the cadence is mixed: most rows are quarterly, but MMATRIX and the PD/LGD Erhebung are annual (§5 below). Third, the Deckungsmasse rows are pure supervisory reuse — Beleg 12, COREP 80/81 and FINREP — which is exactly why the reuse principle makes COREP/FINREP quality drive the whole RTFA.

5 · Kennziffern (§9) — the monitoring ratios

Alongside the RTFA, §9 defines a set of Kennziffern (key ratios) that are monitored each quarter as early-warning signals. They are indicators, not capital charges. Five matter:

KennzahlWhat it measuresSource positionsNature
Loan/Deposit ratio (Kredit-Einlagen-Quote)Funding self-sufficiency: loans relative to depositsFINREP / VERA balance positionsÖRS choice of monitoring metric
Cost-Income-Ratio (CIR)Operating efficiency: costs ÷ operating incomeFINREP P&L (incl. Betriebserträge)ÖRS choice
Asset Encumbrance (belastete Vermögenswerte)Share of assets pledged / unavailable to unsecured creditorsAU_F / AK_F positions (AE template)regulatory template, monitored as ratio
NPE ratio (notleidende Forderungen)Non-performing exposures ÷ total exposuresFINUS / FINUD positions (FINREP)regulatory definition (EBA NPE)
IRRBB outlier test / Supervisory Outlier Test (SOT)Whether the ΔEVE/ΔNII under prescribed rate shocks breaches the outlier thresholdIRRBB ITS positionsregulatory (EBA/GL/2018/02; SOT RTS)

The SOT deserves emphasis. It comes from EBA/GL/2018/02 (the IRRBB guidelines) and the SOT RTS (regulatory technical standards): a bank is flagged as an "outlier" if the change in economic value of equity (and, under the revised standards, net interest income) under prescribed interest-rate shock scenarios exceeds a regulatory threshold of capital. The §9 update aligned the position numbers with the IRRBB ITS so the SOT draws from the same gap data as the Zinsrisiko computation in M6.

🔵 The SOT is a TEST, not a capital charge

The Supervisory Outlier Test produces a Kennzahl/limit (a pass/fail against a threshold), not a number you add to the Deckungsmasse risk side. It answers "is this bank an interest-rate outlier the supervisor should look at?" — a Pillar-2 supervisory flag — whereas the Zinsrisiko VaR in M6 is the actual capital figure. Confusing the two (adding the SOT outcome to the RTFA) double-counts interest-rate risk and misreads the regulation. regulatory

🟣 Removed: Kennziffern that double as Sanierungsindikatoren

The September-2024 version removed the Kennziffern that double as Sanierungsplan-Sanierungsindikatoren (recovery-plan recovery indicators). The rationale is to avoid duplicate governance: those indicators are already monitored under the recovery-planning framework, so reporting them again in the Früherkennung added effort without information. 6 · Frequency & governance — who consumes the output

The cadence is mostly quarterly but has an annual layer; both feed a fixed governance chain.

ItemFrequencyFeeds
RTFA (Problemfall & Extremfall)QuarterlyTraffic light, Deckungsmasse comparison
MMATRIX migration matricesAnnualMigration risk (M5)
PD / LGD estimation (Erhebung)AnnualCredit parameters (M5, M15)
Materiality review of risk types (Sektorrisiko review)AnnualWhich risks are material enough to model

The annual Sektorrisiko (sector-risk) review is the governance act that re-checks materiality — whether each risk type still warrants its place in the framework. The quarterly RTFA and the annual reviews flow up to three consumers: the SRK (Sektorrisikokomitee, sector-risk committee), the ÖRS Vorstand (management board) and the Aufsichtsrat (supervisory board). The whole data-and-reporting machinery exists to put a defensible quarterly traffic light in front of those three bodies.

7 · Worked example — one quarter of credit risk, end-to-end

Worked example — tracing Q-ultimo credit risk through the loop

Take a single Landesgruppen-Mitglied (one KI-Gruppe) at quarter-end. Follow the credit number from raw position to traffic light.

t = 0 (quarter-end). Quartalsultimo book is struck. The R1 columns the learner already queries are populated: exposures, internal ratings (→ master-scale PD, M5), interne Sicherheiten (internal collateral), FX flags (→ FX exposure add-on), and the top-110 GvK positions (→ HHI surcharge).

t ≤ 10 BAT — submission. The Landesorganisation / RSG submits the R1, per ÖRS Datenspezifikation, to the ÖRS. (The Excel credit inputs are left blank — the R1 supersedes them.)

t: ÖRS processing. Qualitätssicherung, then the central credit-risk calc: per customer, Risikoposition = Exposure − interne Sicherheiten; then EL = LGD·PD (→ excess/shortfall = EL − Vorsorgen, which goes to the Deckungsmasse side), UL via the IRB formula (Art. 153/154), the FX add-on on FX exposures, and the HHI surcharge on the corporate UL (top-110 GvK).

t ≤ +10 BAT — results back. ÖRS returns aggregated + detailed results at input granularity. Net: ~20 BAT end-to-end.

Assembly into the RTFA. Risk side: UL (+FX +HHI). Deckungsmasse side: built from supervisory formats — COREP own funds (CET1 7810002, GESAMTRISIKOBETRAG 7980000), FINREP profit, Beleg 12 stille Reserven, COREP 80/81 — plus the excess/shortfall (EL vs Vorsorgen) from the same R1 run. Utilisation = risk ÷ Deckungsmasse → traffic light.

Position-dependency summary. The risk side flows from the R1 (granular, ÖRS-specific); the Deckungsmasse side flows from COREP/FINREP/Beleg 12 (reused supervisory). The two meet only at the excess/shortfall, which the R1 run produces and the Deckungsmasse absorbs. That is why a single R1 quality issue can move both sides of the RTFA at once.

Timeline.   quarter-end  →  ≤10 BAT  [R1 submission]  →  ÖRS QA + credit calc  →  ≤10 BAT  [results back]  →  RTFA assembled (risk = R1 UL/FX/HHI; Deckungsmasse = COREP/FINREP/Beleg 12 ± excess/shortfall)  →  SRK / Vorstand / Aufsichtsrat.

8 · Nuances & traps

⚠ Trap — both consolidation scopes, every quarter

The data must arrive unconsolidated AND (where applicable) consolidated. A common error is reporting only the solo figures and assuming the ÖRS will consolidate centrally. The RTFA's different Auslastung views (solo / §30-BWG KI-Gruppe / IPS, Module 12) each need their own data level; a missing consolidated submission leaves one view un-computable.

⚠ Trap — reused supervisory formats means inherited data quality

Because the Deckungsmasse and several risk inputs are recycled COREP/FINREP, any defect there — a misclassified NPE in FINUS/FINUD, a wrong own-funds line in COREP — flows untouched into the RTFA. The reuse principle saves effort but transfers the data-quality burden onto the supervisory reporting team. COREP/FINREP data quality directly drives RTFA quality.

⚠ Trap — the SOT is a limit/Kennzahl, not a charge

Do not add the Supervisory Outlier Test result to the risk side of the RTFA. It is a pass/fail outlier flag (EBA/GL/2018/02; SOT RTS), separate from the Zinsrisiko VaR (M6) that is the actual capital figure. Treating the SOT as a capital charge double-counts interest-rate risk.

Recap

  • Design principle: reuse supervisory formats (COREP / FINREP / VERA) to minimise manual effort; supplement only where needed (credit risk → R1). Quarterly, OeNB-Meldekalender deadlines, unconsolidated AND consolidated, EUR, Excel templates, to srg-office@rbinternational.com.
  • Credit-risk flow (since 30.6.2023): R1 per ÖRS Datenspezifikation, submitted in 10 BAT; ÖRS does QA + central calc; results back in 10 BAT (~20 BAT round-trip). The R1 superseded the Excel credit inputs.
  • Data → risk map: IRRBB gaps→Zinsrisiko (M6); FX→Währungsrisiko (M6); Wertpapiere×rating+Markit→Spreadrisiko (M6); 7516000→CVA (M7); Betriebserträge→op risk (M6–8); liquidity gaps→FLVaR (M9); GvK→Großkredite (M11/M5); Verkehrswerte→Beteiligungsrisiko (M10); MMATRIX→migration (M5); PD/LGD Erhebung→parameters (M5/M15); Beleg 12 + COREP 80/81 + FINREP→Deckungsmassen (M13).
  • Kennziffern (§9): Loan/Deposit, CIR, Asset Encumbrance (AU_F/AK_F), NPE (FINUS/FINUD), and the IRRBB SOT (EBA/GL/2018/02; SOT RTS — a test, not a charge). §9 aligned positions to the IRRBB ITS and removed Kennziffern that double as Sanierungsindikatoren.
  • Frequency/governance: quarterly RTFA; annual MMATRIX, PD/LGD estimation, Sektorrisiko materiality review. Consumers: SRK, ÖRS Vorstand, Aufsichtsrat.
🇦🇹 Zusammenfassung (Deutsch)
  • Grundprinzip: zur Minimierung des manuellen Aufwands nutzt die Früherkennung aufsichtliche Meldeformate (COREP / FINREP / VERA) und ergänzt nur dort, wo nötig (Kreditrisiko → R1). Quartalsweise, OeNB-Meldekalender, unkonsolidiert UND konsolidiert, EUR, Excel.
  • Kreditrisiko-Datenfluss (seit 30.6.2023): R1 laut ÖRS-Datenspezifikation, Lieferung in 10 BAT; ÖRS macht Qualitätssicherung + zentrale Berechnung; Ergebnisse zurück in 10 BAT. Der R1 ersetzt die Kreditrisiko-Eingaben im Excel.
  • Daten-Risiko-Zuordnung verbindet jede Quelle (z.B. IRRBB-Gaps → Zinsrisiko, GvK → Großkredite, Beleg 12 + COREP 80/81 + FINREP → Deckungsmassen) mit dem jeweiligen Modul.
  • Kennziffern (§9): Kredit-Einlagen-Quote, CIR, Asset Encumbrance (AU_F/AK_F), NPE-Quote (FINUS/FINUD), IRRBB-Ausreißertest/SOT (EBA/GL/2018/02; SOT-RTS) — der SOT ist ein Test/Limit, kein Kapitalbedarf.
  • Frequenz/Governance: quartalsweise RTFA; jährlich MMATRIX, PD/LGD-Schätzung und Sektorrisiko-Wesentlichkeitsprüfung. Adressaten: SRK, ÖRS-Vorstand, Aufsichtsrat.
