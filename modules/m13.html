Module 13 — Risikodeckungsmassen: the cover side of the RTFA, line by line (§6, §8.1)

Module 12 finished the numerator of the Risikotragfähigkeitskalkül — the aggregated risk. This module builds the denominator: the Risikodeckungsmasse (risk-cover potential). You will see exactly what counts as loss-absorbing substance in the Problemfall versus the Extremfall, why the Neubewertungsreserve (revaluation reserve) is stripped in both, how the credit Expected Loss from Module 5 re-enters as the excess/shortfall, the RLB 60%-Verkehrswert option with its worked numbers, and the precise COREP/FINREP/VERA melder positions your R-script reads.

By the end of this module you can…

  • State the four/five building blocks of the Deckungsmasse and write the Problemfall and Extremfall formulae side by side.
  • Apply the quarterly haircut 85/85/90/100% to the budgeted Jahresüberschuss and say why prudence motivates it.
  • Explain why an IFRS/CRR-Rechnungskreis melder does not add the period surplus after tax (it is already in equity — double-count avoidance).
  • Build Freies Kapital from the own-funds stack of Module 2, and know the €5m legal floor and its exception.
  • Compute stille Reserven, apply the RLB 60%-Verkehrswert option, and sign the excess/shortfall correctly.
  • Map every line to its VERA / COREP (Beleg 80/81) / FINREP-FINUS / Beleg-12 position.

1 · Motivation — what is actually allowed to absorb a loss?

The RTFA inequality \(\text{Risiko}_q \le \text{Deckungsmasse}_q\) is only as honest as its right-hand side. If you let too much in, every bank looks solvent in stress; if too little, you over-capitalise a sector that already carries mutual safety nets. The Leitfaden therefore defines the Deckungsmasse scenario-specifically: the substance you may count in a Problemfall (a once-in-20-years bad year, you keep operating) is not the same substance you may count in a Extremfall (a Liquidationsfall, near-wind-down at 99,9%).

▶ The governing intuition: "what could really pay for the loss, given the scenario?"

In a Problemfall the bank is a going concern: it can use this year's earnings and the capital it holds above the regulatory minimum, but it must keep the minimum intact and pay a Mindestausschüttung (minimum dividend). In an Extremfall it is a gone concern: you may reach into the entire own-funds stack (incl. subordinated debt) because the minimum no longer needs protecting — but you may not count next year's hoped-for profit after tax, and you must strip the Neubewertungsreserve (revaluation reserve) because it is volatile, scenario-correlated paper value, not hard loss-absorbing cash.

2 · Concept — the four/five building blocks

Both scenarios are sums of the same families of components; the scenario decides which version of each enters and with what sign. The blocks are:

  1. Freier Jahres-/Konzernüberschuss (free annual / group surplus) — the earnings buffer.
  2. Freies Kapital (free capital) — own funds usable for loss absorption.
  3. Stille Reserven (hidden reserves) — market value over book value on securities & participations.
  4. Excess/Shortfall — the Expected-Loss-vs-provisions delta (this is where credit-risk EL from Module 5 re-enters).
  5. − Neubewertungsreserve (revaluation reserve) — an explicit subtraction in the Extremfall; in the Problemfall it is simply excluded from Freies Kapital, so it never enters either way.

3 · Each block, derived

3.1 Freier Jahres-/Konzernüberschuss — the earnings buffer

Problemfall, UGB-Rechnungskreis (Austrian commercial-code accounting circle). A going-concern bank can absorb a loss out of the profit it expects to earn this year, but only the part not already promised away:

$$ \text{Freier Überschuss}_{\text{UGB}} = h_Q\cdot \text{budget JÜ} + \text{Gewinnvortrag} - \text{Mindestausschüttung} $$
budget JÜ = budgeted/expected Jahresüberschuss (annual surplus) after tax; Gewinnvortrag = retained-profit carried forward; Mindestausschüttung = minimum dividend the bank is committed to pay (so it cannot absorb losses); \(h_Q\) = the quarterly haircut.

The quarterly haircut \(h_Q\) is applied only to the budgeted surplus and ramps with how far into the year you are:

Reporting quarterQ1Q2Q3Q4
Haircut \(h_Q\) on budget JÜ ÖRS choice85%85%90%100%

Why a ramp? Prudence. Early in the year the budget is least certain — little of it has actually been earned, the macro picture can still turn — so the guide recognises only 85% of it. By Q4 most of the year is realised, so the full 100% counts. The haircut does not touch the Gewinnvortrag (already earned) or the Mindestausschüttung (a fixed deduction).

Problemfall, IFRS / CRR-Rechnungskreis. An IFRS group melder uses the budgeted Konzernüberschuss (group surplus) after tax & minorities, plus the expected risk costs (erwartete Risikokosten):

$$ \text{Freier Überschuss}_{\text{IFRS}} = \text{budget Konzernüberschuss}_{\text{after tax, after minorities}} + \text{expected risk costs} $$
The + expected risk costs add-back exists because under IFRS 9 the Expected Loss is already deducted inside own funds (the loss allowance reduces equity). Adding the expected risk costs back to the surplus prevents charging EL twice — once in the surplus and again as the excess/shortfall. This is the IFRS analogue of Module 5's rule "capital carries UL only; EL lives in the Deckungsmasse."

Extremfall. In a near-liquidation you may not count a hoped-for net profit you will never realise as a going concern. The guide therefore uses the profit/(−)loss from continuing operations, pre-tax + Gewinnvortrag. For an IFRS melder there is no period surplus after tax at all — it is already inside equity, which the Extremfall counts directly via anrechenbare Eigenmittel (avoiding the double count).

3.2 Freies Kapital — own funds available to absorb losses

This is where the own-funds stack of Module 2 (CET1 / AT1 / T2, the buffers, the deduction items) becomes a Deckungsmasse input.

Problemfall. Freies Kapital is the hard CET1 the bank holds above its legal Eigenmittelerfordernis (including the P2R and the combined buffer requirement, read off the utilisation band), minus the Neubewertungsreserve, usable for loss absorption:

$$ \text{Freies Kapital}_{\text{PF, UGB}} = \underbrace{\text{CET1} - \text{Neubewertungsreserve}}_{\text{hard, durable CET1}} - \underbrace{(\text{EM-Erfordernis} + \text{P2R} + \text{Puffer})}_{\text{must stay intact}} $$
Only the CET1 excess over the requirement (incl. P2R & buffers per the utilisation band of Module 4) is free; the minimum itself is not loss-absorbing in a going concern because breaching it is itself the failure event. The Neubewertungsreserve is excluded here — the Problemfall mirror of the Extremfall's explicit subtraction.
  • €5m legal floor regulatory — applies even to small institutions; a bank cannot claim Freies Kapital that drops its CET1 below the absolute €5m minimum. An exception for a very small institution requires a binding sector-Sicherungseinrichtung backstop (the scheme commits to top it up), notified to the ÖRS.
  • IFRS / CRR-Rechnungskreis addition: add the AfS reserve (available-for-sale fair-value reserve, which is not counted in own funds) minus the tax on it. The going-concern view recognises that realisable cushion the prudential own-funds figure leaves out.

Extremfall. A gone concern may use the whole capital stack. The UGB build is:

$$ \text{Freies Kapital}_{\text{EF}} = \text{anrechenbare Eigenmittel} - \text{Neubewertungsreserve} + \text{nachrangiges FK} + \text{Abzugsposten Beteiligungen} $$
anrechenbare Eigenmittel = total eligible own funds; nachrangiges FK = subordinated liabilities (T2-eligible, since in liquidation they too absorb loss); Abzugsposten Beteiligungen = the participation deduction items are added back here because participation risk is captured separately as Beteiligungsrisiko (Module 11) — leaving them deducted would double-penalise.

For an IFRS bank, build Freies Kapital from book equity outward, connecting each line to a Module 2 own-funds concept:

$$ \begin{aligned} \text{Freies Kapital}_{\text{EF, IFRS}} =\;& \text{book equity incl. minorities} \\ &+\ \text{subordinated liabilities} (>15\text{ months}) \\ &-\ \text{goodwill / intangibles} \\ &-\ \text{prudent-valuation AVA (Art. 34 / 105 CRR)} \\ &-\ \text{certain DTAs (Art. 36 / 38 CRR)} \\ &+\ \text{Haftsummenzuschlag} \\ &-\ \text{B-IPS Beitrag} \end{aligned} $$
Each maps to Module 2: subordinated liabilities (>15 months residual) are T2; goodwill/intangibles and certain DTAs are CET1 Abzugsposten (deductions, Art. 36/38); the AVA (Additional Valuation Adjustment, Art. 34 referencing Art. 105 prudent valuation) reduces fair-valued positions; the Haftsummenzuschlag (cooperative members' supplementary-liability add-on) is a Raiffeisen-specific own-funds plus; the B-IPS Beitrag (paid-in IPS contribution) is netted out. Own-credit gains/losses are filtered per Art. 33 CRR (DVA / own-credit-risk fair-value changes removed) so the bank does not book a "gain" from its own deteriorating credit.

3.3 Stille Reserven — market value above book value

$$ \text{Stille Reserve} = \text{Marktwert / Verkehrswert} - \text{Buchwert} $$
on securities (Wertpapiere) and participations (Beteiligungen). A positive difference is a stille Reserve (hidden reserve); a negative one is a stille Last (hidden charge).

IFRS / CRR-Rechnungskreis treatment:

  • HtC (Hold-to-Collect) securities → NO stille Reserven/Lasten. Under IFRS 9 they are measured at amortised cost and held to maturity, so the interim market-value gap is irrelevant to loss-absorption and is excluded both ways.
  • Participations → stille Reserven in the Problemfall; stille Reserven and Lasten in the Extremfall (the gone-concern view recognises hidden charges too).
  • Participations without a market value: apply a 20% haircut on the "presumed" market value (per the OeNB ICAAP-Leitfaden) ÖRS choice; a resulting stille Last is not recognised (prudent asymmetry — you may not invent a hidden charge from a guessed value).
🟣 The RLB 60%-Verkehrswert option — the sector's internal-value rule

A Bundesland (regional Raiffeisen organisation) over the yellow threshold may count the stille Reserven of its participation in the Spitzeninstitut (the apex/Landesbank, the RLB) up to 60% of the Verkehrswert minus the book value — instead of the ordinary, more limited recognition. The condition (only when over yellow) and the 60% cap are an ÖRS choice.

Worked example — the RLB 60% option, both numbers

Inputs: Verkehrswert of the RLB participation = €432m; Buchwert summed across the participating Raiffeisenbanken (RBn) = €104m.

Full stille Reserve = \(432 - 104 = \textbf{€328m}\) (the total hidden reserve, not all recognisable).

Ordinary recognition (previously allowed): \(432 \times 0{,}16 = \textbf{€69m}\) — i.e. only 16% of the Verkehrswert was creditable.

60% option cap: \(0{,}60 \times 432 - 104 = 259{,}2 - 104 = \textbf{€155m}\) maximum.

So the option lifts the recognisable stille Reserve from €69m to up to €155m — still well short of the full €328m. Policy rationale: recognise genuine sector-internal value (the RLB participation really is worth far more than book in normal times) without over-recognising a volatile, scenario-correlated value that may not be realisable in the very stress you are testing. The 60% cap is the compromise between "ignore it" (€69m) and "count it all" (€328m).

3.4 Excess/Shortfall — where credit-risk EL re-enters

$$ \text{Excess/Shortfall} = \underbrace{\text{Wertberichtigungen}}_{\text{provisions held}} - \underbrace{\text{Expected Loss}}_{EL=\sum PD\cdot LGD\cdot EAD} $$
Wertberichtigungen = the provisions actually booked across the whole portfolio: gebildete Vorsorgen incl. Eventual-Rückstellungen (contingent provisions), Portfolio-/Pauschalwertberichtigungen (portfolio / general value adjustments), the §57(1) BWG Unterdeckung (statutory under-provisioning reserve), and the relevant passive Rechnungsabgrenzung (accruals). EL is the Module 5 Expected Loss across the whole portfolio.
Mind the sign — it can add OR subtract

If provisions exceed EL (an excess), the bank has over-provided relative to its modelled expected loss, so the surplus provisions are extra cover → ADD to the Deckungsmasse. If EL exceeds provisions (a shortfall), the bank is under-provided for its expected loss, so the gap must come out of the cover → SUBTRACT. This applies in both scenarios identically. It is the accounting counterpart of Module 5's rule: the IRB capital number carries UL only, while EL is netted against provisions here. Never also subtract EL from the risk side — that would double-count.

4 · Worked example — a Problemfall Deckungsmasse end to end

Worked example — building the cover (UGB Problemfall)

A Raiffeisenbank reports at Q3. Inputs (all €m):

Block 1 — Freier Überschuss. Budgeted JÜ = 4,0. Q3 haircut = 90% ⇒ \(4{,}0 \times 0{,}90 = 3{,}6\). Less Mindestausschüttung 1,0, plus Gewinnvortrag 2,0:

$$ 3{,}6 - 1{,}0 + 2{,}0 = \textbf{€4,6m freier Überschuss}. $$

Block 2 — Freies Kapital = €30,0m (CET1 above the EM requirement incl. P2R & buffers, net of Neubewertungsreserve).

Block 3 — Stille Reserven = €8,0m (Marktwert − Buchwert on securities & participations).

Block 4 — Excess/Shortfall: provisions 6,0 − EL 5,0 = +€1,0m (an excess → adds).

$$ \text{Deckungsmasse}_{\text{PF}} = 4{,}6 + 30{,}0 + 8{,}0 + 1{,}0 = \textbf{€43,6m}. $$

Which lines change for the Extremfall?

  • Block 1: drop the after-tax surplus; use profit from continuing operations pre-tax + Gewinnvortrag (the 90% haircut and Mindestausschüttung mechanics fall away).
  • Block 2: Freies Kapital widens to the full stack — anrechenbare Eigenmittel − Neubewertungsreserve + nachrangiges FK + Abzugsposten Beteiligungen (typically larger than the €30m going-concern excess).
  • Block 3: now includes stille Lasten on participations as well (could lower the figure).
  • New line: − Neubewertungsreserve is explicitly subtracted.
  • Block 4: excess/shortfall is computed identically (here still +€1,0m).

6 · Nuances & traps

⚠ The quarterly haircut hits the BUDGET only

The 85/85/90/100% haircut applies to the budgeted Jahresüberschuss, not to the Gewinnvortrag and not to the Mindestausschüttung. Applying 90% to the whole €4,6m freier Überschuss instead of just the €4,0m budget JÜ is a classic mistake — recompute the worked example to see it: \(0{,}9\times4{,}6 = 4{,}14 \ne 4{,}6\).

⚠ The Neubewertungsreserve is stripped in BOTH scenarios — by different mechanics

In the Extremfall it is an explicit − Neubewertungsreserve line. In the Problemfall it never appears because it is excluded from Freies Kapital at the outset. Either way it is out. Do not "add it back" in the Problemfall thinking it was only removed in the Extremfall.

⚠ Excess/shortfall can be + OR −

It is not always additive. Provisions > EL ⇒ excess (adds); EL > provisions ⇒ shortfall (subtracts). A shortfall reduces the Deckungsmasse in both scenarios. Confusing the sign flips your utilisation the wrong way.

⚠ The 60% option is conditional and capped

The RLB 60%-Verkehrswert recognition is only for a Bundesland over the yellow threshold — not a default rule, and not for the Problemfall of any green institution. And it is a cap (\(0{,}6\cdot VW - BW\)), not the full stille Reserve. In the example: €155m max, not €328m.

⚠ IFRS HtC securities carry no stille Reserven — and never double-count next-year profit

Hold-to-Collect securities under IFRS 9 generate no stille Reserven/Lasten. And the cardinal IFRS double-count trap: the period surplus after tax is already in equity, so the Extremfall counts it through anrechenbare Eigenmittel and does not add it again as a separate "next-year profit". Likewise the IFRS Problemfall adds back expected risk costs precisely because EL is already inside own funds under IFRS 9.

Recap

  • Deckungsmasse blocks: Freier Jahres-/Konzernüberschuss + Freies Kapital + Stille Reserven + Excess/Shortfall; the Extremfall additionally subtracts the Neubewertungsreserve.
  • Freier Überschuss: Problemfall UGB = haircut·budget JÜ + Gewinnvortrag − Mindestausschüttung, haircut 85/85/90/100% (Q1–Q4, prudence); IFRS = budgeted Konzernüberschuss after tax & minorities + expected risk costs (EL already in own funds). Extremfall = continuing-ops pre-tax + Gewinnvortrag; IFRS adds no after-tax surplus.
  • Freies Kapital: Problemfall = CET1 above the EM requirement (incl. P2R & buffers) − Neubewertungsreserve, €5m floor; Extremfall = anrechenbare EM − Neubewertungsreserve + nachrangiges FK + Abzugsposten Beteiligungen (IFRS build via Art. 33/34/36/38/105 CRR + Haftsummenzuschlag − B-IPS).
  • Stille Reserven = Marktwert − Buchwert; IFRS HtC securities → none; participations without market value → 20% haircut, no stille Last; RLB 60% option: €432m VW, €104m BW ⇒ stille Reserve €328m, cap \(0{,}6\cdot432-104=\)€155m (vs €69m ordinary).
  • Excess/Shortfall = Vorsorgen − EL, sign + (excess, adds) or − (shortfall, subtracts), both scenarios — the re-entry point of Module 5's EL.
  • Worked Problemfall: 4,6 + 30,0 + 8,0 + 1,0 = €43,6m.
🇦🇹 Zusammenfassung (Deutsch)
  • Die Risikodeckungsmasse setzt sich aus freiem Jahres-/Konzernüberschuss, freiem Kapital, stillen Reserven und Excess/Shortfall zusammen; im Extremfall wird zusätzlich die Neubewertungsreserve abgezogen.
  • Freier Überschuss (UGB, Problemfall): budgetierter Jahresüberschuss mit Quartalsabschlag 85/85/90/100% (Vorsicht: nur auf den Budgetwert) + Gewinnvortrag − Mindestausschüttung. Im IFRS-/CRR-Rechnungskreis Konzernüberschuss nach Steuern und Minderheiten + erwartete Risikokosten (EL bereits in den Eigenmitteln).
  • Freies Kapital: Problemfall = CET1 über dem Eigenmittelerfordernis (inkl. P2R und Puffer) − Neubewertungsreserve, gesetzliche €5m-Untergrenze; Extremfall = anrechenbare Eigenmittel − Neubewertungsreserve + nachrangiges Fremdkapital + Abzugsposten Beteiligungen.
  • Stille Reserven = Markt-/Verkehrswert − Buchwert; IFRS-HtC-Wertpapiere: keine; Beteiligungen ohne Marktwert: 20%-Abschlag, keine stille Last. RLB-60%-Option: bei Verkehrswert €432m und Buchwert €104m beträgt die stille Reserve €328m, anrechenbar maximal \(0{,}6\cdot432-104=\)€155m (statt €69m).
  • Excess/Shortfall = Wertberichtigungen − Expected Loss; positiv (Excess → erhöht) oder negativ (Shortfall → vermindert) — in beiden Szenarien.
