Module 4 — The RTFA framework: Problemfall (95%), Extremfall (99,9%), and the traffic lights

The Risikotragfähigkeitskalkül is the whole machine in one inequality: aggregated Risiko on one side, Deckungsmasse on the other, read at two confidence levels. This module makes that inequality precise — the probabilities, the VaR logic that lets you compare risks measured at different horizons, and the green/yellow/red rules with their reporting duties.

By the end of this module you can…

  • Explain why 95% means "once in 20 years" and 99,9% means a Liquidationsfall — from the quantile, not by assertion.
  • State exactly what risk and what Deckungsmasse each scenario uses, and why they differ.
  • Use VaR horizontal/vertical consistency to convert a risk measured at one horizon/confidence to another.
  • Apply the green/yellow/red thresholds — and know they differ between Problemfall and Extremfall.
  • Name the reporting consequences (Management Summary, Redepflicht) and who receives them.

1 · Motivation — one inequality, two questions

Every quarter your unit answers, per institution and per view: is there enough loss-absorbing substance in front of the risk? Formally,

$$ \text{Auslastung} \;=\; \frac{\text{Risiko}_q}{\text{Deckungsmasse}_q} \;\overset{?}{\le}\; 100\% $$
Auslastung = utilisation; q = the confidence level of the scenario. The genius — and the trap — is that both numerator and denominator change with the scenario. It is not the same risk measured against more or less cover; it is two different definitions of risk set against two different definitions of cover.
▶ Why two scenarios and not one

A single confidence level cannot serve two masters. The supervisor cares whether you breach the regulatory minimum after a plausibly bad year (a frequent, "manageable" stress — the Problemfall). The bank's own risk management cares whether it could be economically wiped out (a rare, near-fatal stress — the Extremfall). Different questions ⇒ different probabilities ⇒ different cover. The Leitfaden lets each institution choose which is the binding steering measure and which is the side-condition (§4).

2.2 The Solvabilität and Stress side-conditions

Two further lenses sit around the RTFA (§4.3–4.4):

  • Solvabilität — the pure regulatory capital ratio (CET1/T1/T2 vs Eigenmittelerfordernis). Because it may not capture all risks adequately, the Leitfaden treats it as a strict side-condition (Nebenbedingung), not the main measure.
  • Stress-Szenario — a 2–3-year "bad case" narrative whose probability is not statistically measured; it catches things the parametric VaR misses (concentration follow-on effects, funding-cost spirals). See the Leitfaden Stress Testing. Reverse stress testing inverts it: find the loss that exhausts the cover, then build the scenario that produces it.

3 · Derivation — where 95% and 99,9% come from

3.1 The "once in 20 years" reading of 95%

A confidence level \(q\) on a one-year loss distribution is a quantile statement: the loss exceeds the capital only with probability \(1-q\) per year. Treating years as independent Bernoulli trials, the expected waiting time to the first exceedance is geometric:

$$ \mathbb{E}[\text{years to first breach}] \;=\; \frac{1}{1-q}. $$
At q = 95%: \(1/(1-0.95)=20\) years. At q = 99,9%: \(1/(1-0.999)=1000\) years.

So at 100% Problemfall utilisation, statistically a loss larger than the Deckungsmasse is expected once every 20 years — which the Leitfaden judges adequate precisely because the sector safety nets (IPS, Sicherungseinrichtungen) stand behind a single bank. A remedial action once in 20 years is acceptable when you are not alone. This is the explicit ÖRS justification for 95% being "conservative enough", even for larger banks, given that risks are added (correlation +1) and the statistical assumptions are more representative at portfolio scale.

⚖️ Why 99,9% / 1-year for the Extremfall is not arbitrary

The Extremfall mirrors the IRB approach: the CRR's credit risk-weight formula is itself calibrated to a 99,9% / one-year confidence (Module 5). Using the same calibration makes the economic-capital number commensurable with the regulatory one and defensible to the supervisor. The Leitfaden cites the OeNB/FMA Gesamtbankrisikosteuerung guide: the Liquidationsfall is "a VaR-consistent determination of total bank risk over a one-year horizon at 99,9%, because this holding period and probability also underlie the IRB approach for credit risk." So the 99,9% is regulatory-anchored, not an ÖRS invention.

3.2 VaR horizontal & vertical consistency — the conversion engine

The Leitfaden makes a strong claim: a risk measured at one (horizon, confidence) can be transformed into an equivalent at another. This is what lets the bank measure market risk at, say, 10 days/95% and credit risk at 1 year/99,9% and still add them coherently.

Vertical (re-scale the confidence) — under a normal/parametric assumption, a VaR is a quantile multiple of a volatility, so converting confidence is just a ratio of standard-normal quantiles:

$$ \text{VaR}_{q_2} \;=\; \text{VaR}_{q_1}\cdot\frac{\Phi^{-1}(q_2)}{\Phi^{-1}(q_1)}. $$
\(\Phi^{-1}\) = standard-normal inverse CDF. Key quantiles you will reuse constantly: \(\Phi^{-1}(0.95)=1{,}6449\); \(\Phi^{-1}(0.99)=2{,}3263\); \(\Phi^{-1}(0.999)=3{,}0902\).

Horizontal (re-scale the holding period) — under i.i.d. returns, volatility scales with the square root of time (the "square-root-of-time rule"):

$$ \text{VaR}_{T_2} \;=\; \text{VaR}_{T_1}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{T_2}{T_1}}. $$
T = holding period in (trading) days. This is exactly why the market-risk and liquidity modules scale a daily/annual volatility to a 250-day holding period.
This is the thread tying Modules 6–9 together

The CVA scalars 0,71 and 1,33 (Module 7), the trading-book scalars, and the op-risk 5% factor (Module 8) are all just the vertical formula applied to a regulatory charge calibrated at 99% (or 99,9%). Once you have this, those "magic numbers" stop being magic.

4 · Worked example — converting a risk and reading the light

Worked example A — vertical conversion (the CVA scalar, previewed)

A CVA own-funds charge is calibrated at 99% (the regulatory CVA confidence). What is the Problemfall (95%) equivalent under the normal assumption?

$$ \text{factor} = \frac{\Phi^{-1}(0.95)}{\Phi^{-1}(0.99)} = \frac{1{,}6449}{2{,}3263} = 0{,}71. $$

And the Extremfall (99,9%) equivalent: \(\;3{,}0902/2{,}3263 = 1{,}33.\) These are exactly the Leitfaden's CVA factors (§5.2). You have just derived two of the course's headline numbers from one formula.

Worked example B — reading the traffic light

An institution reports, on a Solo basis:

  • Problemfall: Risiko = €86 m, Deckungsmasse (CRR+P2R+Puffer) = €93 m → Auslastung = 92,5%.
  • Extremfall: Risiko = €140 m, Deckungsmasse = €152 m → Auslastung = 92,1%.

Problemfall 92,5% is > 90% → gelb (Beobachtungsstatus). Extremfall 92,1% is > 90% and ≤ 95% → gelb. The institution must produce a Management Summary commenting on the result and describing measures to reduce risk or raise the Deckungsmasse. Note: the Leitfaden states that > 90% on a single-institution basis is not targeted and triggers planning of remedial measures regardless of colour.

⚠ The subtle Problemfall detail

In the Problemfall, the denominator changes between the yellow and red tests: yellow is measured against cover including the Kapitalerhaltungspuffer and supervisory buffers (CRR+P2R+Puffer); red is measured against cover excluding those buffers (CRR+P2R only). The logic: yellow warns when you start eating into buffers; red fires only when you would breach the hard minimum even after buffers are gone. The Extremfall uses a single denominator. Examiners probe this.

5.2 Reporting & Redepflicht (§4.5)

  • Gelb (Beobachtungsstatus): the unit writes a Management Summary commenting on the result and the measures to reduce risk / increase the Deckungsmasse.
  • Rot: a detailed Management Summary with concrete steps and timelines to get back to yellow.
  • Who is covered: all R-IPS member institutions (Solo or KI-/FH-Gruppe view) and the R-IPS itself.
  • Addressees: for an RLB (solo + consolidated) and its Primärbanken, the respective Landesorganisationen; for all RLBs, their subsidiaries, individual Primärbanken, RBI and the R-IPS, the ÖRS.
  • Redepflicht Bankprüfer (§63(3) BWG): when the auditor's duty-to-report is triggered, reports go to bg-lg@raiffeisen-einlagensicherung.at.
🟣 ÖRS choice vs regulatory requirement here

The 95% Problemfall confidence, the 90%/95% traffic-light cut-offs, and the ">90% solo not targeted" rule are ÖRS modelling/appetite choices — expressions of the sector's Risikoappetit. The 99,9%/1-year Extremfall and the requirement to run an ICAAP at all (§39a BWG) are regulatory-anchored. The trade-off the ÖRS makes at 95%: it accepts a ~once-in-20-years remedial trigger in exchange for not over-capitalising the sector, justified by the mutual safety nets.

6 · Nuances & traps

⚠ Trap — "more conservative confidence = bigger number" is not automatic here

Going from 95% to 99,9% raises the risk, but it also changes the Deckungsmasse definition (the Extremfall lets you count the full own-funds stack). So a bank can be greener in the Extremfall than the Problemfall, or vice versa. Never assume the Extremfall is the binding constraint — the Leitfaden explicitly lets each institution decide which scenario steers and which is the side-condition.

⚠ Trap — aggregation is addition (correlation +1)

The RTFA adds the single risks with correlation = +1 (Module 12) — i.e. no diversification benefit across risk types. This is deliberately conservative and is what makes the simple 95% defensible. The only place correlation < 1 enters is within a risk type (e.g. between currencies in FX risk, between rating classes in spread risk, and between Zinsrisiko and Credit-Spread risk inside market risk).

Recap

  • RTFA = Risiko / Deckungsmasse, read at 95% (Problemfall) and 99,9% (Extremfall) — both numerator and denominator change between scenarios.
  • 95% ⇒ a breach once every 20 years (\(1/(1-q)\)); acceptable because of the sector safety nets. 99,9%/1-yr = Liquidationsfall, anchored to the IRB calibration.
  • VaR is vertically (\(\Phi^{-1}(q_2)/\Phi^{-1}(q_1)\)) and horizontally (\(\sqrt{T_2/T_1}\)) consistent — this generates the 0,71 / 1,33 / 5% scalars later.
  • Traffic lights: Problemfall green ≤90 / yellow >90 / red >95 (red drops the Puffer from the denominator); Extremfall green ≤90 / yellow 90–95 / red >95.
  • Yellow → Management Summary; Red → detailed Summary with steps & timing; auditor's Redepflicht per §63(3) BWG.
🇦🇹 Zusammenfassung (Deutsch)
  • Das Risikotragfähigkeitskalkül stellt das aggregierte Risiko der Deckungsmasse gegenüber — im Problemfall (95%) und im Extremfall (99,9%). Beide Größen ändern sich je Szenario.
  • 95% bedeutet statistisch einen Schadensfall größer der Deckungsmasse alle 20 Jahre (\(1/(1-q)\)) — wegen der Sicherungseinrichtungen im Sektor als ausreichend erachtet. Der Extremfall (99,9%/1 Jahr) ist der Liquidationsfall und an den IRB-Ansatz angelehnt.
  • Der VaR ist horizontal und vertikal konsistent — daraus folgen später die Skalierungsfaktoren 0,71 und 1,33.
  • Ampel: Problemfall grün ≤90% / gelb >90% / rot >95% (rot ohne Puffer im Nenner); Extremfall grün ≤90% / gelb 90–95% / rot >95%.
  • Gelb → Management Summary; Rot → ausführliche Summary mit Maßnahmen; Redepflicht des Bankprüfers gem. §63 Abs. 3 BWG.
Source: ÖRS Leitfaden Früherkennung 09/2024, Ch. 4 (incl. §4.1–4.5); OeNB/FMA Leitfaden Gesamtbankrisikosteuerung. Quantile values are standard-normal.
