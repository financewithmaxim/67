Module 11 — Other risks: Länderrisiko, makroökonomisches Risiko, Großkredite, the 5% buffer & FX-capital risk

This module sweeps up the "long tail" of the RTFA: the country-transfer add-on (Länderrisiko), the point-in-time macro overlay on credit risk (makroökonomisches Risiko), the sector-level large-exposure monitor (Risiko aus Großkrediten), and the §5.10 closers — the flat 5% buffer for unquantified qualitative risks and the Problemfall-only Fremdwährungs-Eigenmittelrisiko — anchored against the §3.10 list of qualitative risks each one stands in for.

By the end of this module you can…

  • Explain why Länderrisiko (country risk) is an add-on even though sovereign ratings already feed credit risk — and compute it (UL-only, pre-collateral, defaults excluded).
  • Run the makroökonomisches Risiko mechanics: PD·1,25, LGD·1,05, rerun the IRB formula, take the difference — and explain why it is credit-only and includes EL.
  • Distinguish the Leitfaden's Großkredite concentration monitor (vs R-IPS CET1) from the CRR 25%-of-Tier-1 legal large-exposure limit.
  • Show that the 5% sonstige-Risiken buffer scales the whole quantified risk stack by 1,05, and justify it via the Deckungsmasse.
  • Separate the Fremdwährungs-Eigenmittelrisiko (capital-base effect, Problemfall only) from the FX credit-risk add-on of M5 (exposure effect, both scenarios).
  • Define the §3.10 qualitative risks and say which capital element captures them.

1 · Motivation — why a "rest" category exists

By Module 10 you have quantified the heavy hitters: credit (M5), market (M6), operational (M7), liquidity (M8), CVA (M9), participation (M10). But the RTFA must be complete — every material single risk has to sit in the additive stack, because aggregation is by addition with correlation \(+1\) (Module 4/12). Three groups of risk remain that are real but do not fit the main engines cleanly:

  • Cross-border risk that ratings under-capture — a sovereign can block transfer/convertibility even when individual obligors are sound. → Länderrisiko (§5.3).
  • A whole-cycle downturn that pushes PDs and LGDs together — a point-in-time stress the through-the-cycle credit number does not show. → makroökonomisches Risiko (§5.8).
  • Sector-level concentration in the largest names, monitored above the legal limit. → Risiko aus Großkrediten (§5.9).

And finally the §5.10 closers: a flat buffer for risks too soft to model (strategic, reputational, business-model, systemic, model — the §3.10 list), and the FX effect on the capital requirement itself.

▶ The thread that ties §5.3, §5.8, §5.9, §5.10 together

All four are credit-risk-adjacent overlays or completeness patches. Länderrisiko and makro reuse the M5 credit machinery on different inputs; Großkredite re-reads the same exposures through a concentration lens; the 5% buffer and FW-Eigenmittelrisiko close the gap between "what we modelled" and "what can hurt the Deckungsmasse". None introduces a new statistical engine — which is exactly why they live in one module.

2 · Concept & logic

2.1 Länderrisiko — transfer & convertibility

Länderrisiko (country risk) here means Transfer- und Konvertierungsrisiko (transfer and convertibility risk): the risk that a solvent foreign obligor cannot service a cross-border claim because the sovereign restricts FX or capital movement. The motivating question — "why an add-on at all, given we already rate sovereigns inside credit risk?" — is answered by rating ceilings: an obligor's rating is capped near its sovereign's, but the cap does not fully capture the sovereign-driven transfer/convertibility component. The add-on isolates that residual.

2.2 Makroökonomisches Risiko — a point-in-time overlay

Credit UL in M5 uses through-the-cycle PDs. A genuine cyclical downturn raises PDs and LGDs simultaneously across the book. Makroökonomisches Risiko (macroeconomic risk) measures that: assume a GDP downturn, recompute credit EL+UL with stressed inputs, and take the increment. It is applied only to credit risk, the most material single risk, and — unusually — it captures both EL and UL, because a downturn raises expected loss too. It is a point-in-time macro overlay, conceptually distinct from M5's FX PD-elasticity (which reacts to the exchange rate, not GDP).

2.3 Risiko aus Großkrediten — a monitor, not the legal limit

Risiko aus Großkrediten (large-exposure risk) is a sector-level concentration monitor, assessed per Gruppe verbundener Kunden (GvK — group of connected clients, per the OeNB customer structure). It sets the total of the largest exposures (customers, FI, Sovereigns) in relation to R-IPS CET1 (Kernkapital — core capital). Crucially it is not the CRR Part Four legal large-exposure limit (25% of Tier 1 to one client/group). The legal limit binds at the single-institution level; the Leitfaden monitor watches the aggregated sector book on top of it.

2.4 §5.10 closers

Two devices close the framework: a 5% buffer (Puffer sonstige Risiken) standing in for the qualitative §3.10 risks, and the Fremdwährungs-Eigenmittelrisiko (FX own-funds risk) — the effect of an FX move on the capital requirement base, applied in the Problemfall only.

3 · Derivation & mechanics

3.1 Länderrisiko (§5.3)

The add-on is computed like credit risk — the same IRB UL engine of M5 — but on three specific settings:

$$ \text{Länderrisiko} \;=\; \sum_{c}\; UL_{\text{IRB}}\!\big(PD_{\text{sov}(c)},\,LGD,\,EAD^{\text{vor Sicherheiten}}_{c}\big),\qquad \text{rating } 5{,}x \text{ excluded.} $$
Computed on internal data BEFORE collateral (vor Sicherheiten) — transfer risk is not mitigated by local collateral you cannot repatriate. PD is taken by sovereign rating (\(PD_{\text{sov}}\)) of the obligor's country, not the obligor's own PD. Only the unexpected loss (UL) is taken — no EL term. Defaulted exposures (Ratingstufe 5,x) are excluded — a name already in default carries no incremental transfer risk.
🟣 Sitzlandprinzip vs Haftungszuordnung

Which country does an exposure belong to? Two assignment rules: Sitzlandprinzip (domicile principle — a political/legal-seat assignment) versus Haftungszuordnung (an economic assignment to where the repayment risk actually sits, e.g. the location of the cash flows or guarantor). The Leitfaden prefers the economic Haftungszuordnung — it tracks the real source of transfer risk rather than the registered address. ÖRS choice

🟦 When you may skip it

If the country rating already embeds transfer/convertibility risk, the add-on may be skipped for direct sovereign investments — applying it there would double-count the very risk the sovereign rating already prices. This is the consistency check that keeps the add-on a genuine residual.

3.2 Makroökonomisches Risiko (§5.8)

Take the existing credit risk (EL+UL). Stress the two credit drivers, rerun the M5 IRB formula, and difference:

$$ PD' = PD\cdot 1{,}25,\qquad LGD' = LGD\cdot 1{,}05 $$ $$ \text{Makro-Risiko} \;=\; \underbrace{\big[EL(PD',LGD') + UL(PD',LGD')\big]}_{\text{new credit risk}} \;-\; \underbrace{\big[EL(PD,LGD) + UL(PD,LGD)\big]}_{\text{original credit risk}} $$
The calibration comes from the Leitfaden Stresstesting: a \(\approx 2\%\) GDP decline maps to PDs up \(\approx 25\%\) and LGDs up \(\approx 5\%\). ÖRS modelling Re-run means: feed \(PD',LGD'\) through the same conditional-PD / \(K\) machinery of M5 (cross-link Module 5), recompute both the EL term \(LGD'\!\cdot PD'\) and the UL term, and subtract the un-stressed total. The result covers both EL and UL.
Why both EL and UL here — and only credit

Elsewhere the RTFA capital number is pure UL (EL lives in the Deckungsmasse). Makro risk breaks that pattern on purpose: a downturn raises expected loss too, and the point is to capture the full deterioration of the credit book, so the difference is taken on EL+UL together. It is applied to credit risk only because credit is the most material risk — stressing the smaller risks would add complexity without materiality.

3.3 Risiko aus Großkrediten (§5.9)

Assess at the GvK level. For the three relevant client structures — customers, FI, Sovereigns — take the largest exposures, sum them, and relate to R-IPS CET1:

$$ \text{Großkredit-Kennzahl} \;=\; \frac{\sum_{\text{largest GvK}}\text{Exposure}}{\text{R-IPS CET1 (Kernkapital)}},\qquad \text{R-IPS members excluded.} $$
The numerator sums the largest exposures across customers / FI / Sovereigns at GvK level; R-IPS-member companies, banks and issues are excluded (intra-circle exposures are not external concentration — cf. the consolidation logic of Module 13). The denominator is R-IPS CET1. This is a monitoring view, set in relation to capital, not a binding cap.
🟦 Monitor vs legal limit — keep them apart

regulatory CRR Part Four sets a hard large-exposure limit: exposure to one client or GvK may not exceed 25% of Tier 1 capital. That is a legal ceiling on a single name/group.   The Leitfaden's §5.9 measure is a sector-level concentration monitor on top of that limit: it aggregates the largest exposures and watches the whole stack against R-IPS CET1. Concentration is also handled in two other places — the HHI granularity add-on inside credit risk (Module 5) and via internal limits. §5.9 is the macro-prudential lens; the others are the per-loan and governance lenses.

3.4 Sonstige Risiken — the 5% buffer (§5.10)

Both scenarios add 5% of the quantified risks as an approximation for the qualitative §3.10 risks. Because it is applied to the sum of all quantified risks, it scales the whole stack:

$$ \text{Puffer} = 0{,}05\cdot \sum_k R_k,\qquad \text{Gesamtrisiko} = \Big(\sum_k R_k\Big)\cdot 1{,}05 $$
\(R_k\) = each quantified single risk (credit incl. its add-ons, market, op, CVA, participation, country, macro, Großkredite, and — Problemfall — FW-EM). The buffer is a flat \(+5\%\) on their sum, i.e. a \(\times 1{,}05\) on the entire quantified risk total. ÖRS choice
🟣 Why a flat % — and what it trades off

Justification: the qualitative risks (strategic, reputational, business-model, systemic, model) are hard to quantify, and — decisively — future operating income is NOT counted in the Extremfall Deckungsmasse. Normally a healthy earnings stream is the first cushion against these soft risks; since the Extremfall (a Liquidationsfall) cannot rely on it, a flat capital buffer is a reasonable proxy for that missing cushion. The trade-off: a flat percentage cannot distinguish a bank with high strategic/business-model risk from one with low — it charges both 5%. It buys comparability and simplicity across hundreds of banks at the cost of risk sensitivity. ÖRS choice

3.5 Fremdwährungs-Eigenmittelrisiko (§5.10, Problemfall only)

This is the FX effect on the capital requirement, not on the exposure. An FX appreciation raises the euro Saldo of FX loans → raises the RWA base (Bemessungsgrundlage) → raises the Eigenmittel (own-funds) requirement → reduces free Deckungsmasse. Mechanics:

$$ \Delta\text{Bemessungsgrundlage} = \text{Risikoposition}_{\text{FX}}\cdot v_{\text{fx}},\qquad \text{FW-Eigenmittelrisiko} = 0{,}08\cdot \Delta\text{Bemessungsgrundlage} $$
\(\text{Risikoposition}_{\text{FX}}\) = the FX portion of the risk positions in Corporates, Retail, LRG, CIU; \(v_{\text{fx}}\) = the scenario-scaled 3-year FX volatility. If there is no full currency separation in the data, use the FX proxy 15% (Problemfall) / 30% (Extremfall) in place of \(v_{\text{fx}}\). The \(0{,}08 = 8\%\) is the own-funds rate applied to the uplift of the RWA base. regulatory (8% own-funds factor) on an ÖRS-modelled base.
🟦 Why the Extremfall does NOT apply it

In the Extremfall (Liquidationsfall) the FW-Eigenmittelrisiko is not applied: in a liquidation you no longer run the bank as a going concern, so the RWA-base / capital-requirement effect is irrelevant to the cover — what matters there is the loss on the assets themselves, which is already in credit risk. The 15%/30% proxy still defines the scaling conceptually, but the 8%-on-RWA-base charge is a Problemfall-only construct.

4 · Worked example

Worked example — three §5.8 / §5.10 calculations (illustrative; assumptions stated)

(a) Makroökonomisches Risiko. A portfolio's current credit risk is \(EL+UL = \)€50m. Apply the calibrated downturn: \(PD' = PD\cdot 1{,}25\), \(LGD' = LGD\cdot 1{,}05\). Rerunning the M5 IRB formula with the stressed inputs lifts the EL term roughly proportionally and the UL term more than proportionally (the conditional-PD is convex in PD); for this book the rerun gives a new credit risk of \(\approx\)€64m.

$$ \text{Makro-Risiko} \approx 64 - 50 = \textbf{€14m.} $$

Assumptions: the €64m is the full rerun (not a back-of-envelope ×1,25); the exact uplift depends on the PD/LGD mix and rating distribution — a high-PD book moves more because the conditional-PD response steepens. The €14m is added to the stack as a single number covering both EL and UL.

(b) Fremdwährungs-Eigenmittelrisiko (Problemfall). Risikoposition of FX loans (Corporates/Retail/LRG/CIU) = €40m, and the data has no full currency separation, so use the 15% Problemfall proxy:

$$ \Delta\text{Bemessungsgrundlage} = 40\text{m}\cdot 0{,}15 = \textbf{€6m},\qquad \text{FW-EM-Risiko} = 0{,}08\cdot 6\text{m} = \textbf{€0,48m.} $$

So €0,48m of Deckungsmasse is consumed by the higher own-funds requirement. In the Extremfall this charge is €0 (not applied).

(c) 5% buffer. Suppose every quantified single risk sums to €100m (credit + market + op + CVA + participation + country + macro + Großkredite + FW-EM). The buffer is:

$$ \text{Puffer} = 0{,}05\cdot 100\text{m} = \textbf{€5m},\qquad \text{Gesamtrisiko} = 100\text{m}\cdot 1{,}05 = \textbf{€105m.} $$

Note the buffer is computed on the total after all the other §5 items — including the macro and FW-EM numbers from (a) and (b) — so it genuinely scales the whole stack by 1,05.

6 · Nuances & traps

⚠ Makro risk is credit-only and includes EL

Everywhere else the capital figure is pure UL and EL lives in the Deckungsmasse. Makroökonomisches Risiko is the exception: it is the difference in EL+UL, and it is applied to credit risk only. If you (a) apply the PD·1,25 / LGD·1,05 stress to market or op risk, or (b) strip the EL out of the difference, you have mis-specified it. The whole point is the full credit-book deterioration in a downturn.

⚠ FW-Eigenmittelrisiko ≠ the FX credit-risk add-on (M5)

Two different FX effects, easily conflated:

  • FX credit-risk add-on (M5): FX appreciation raises the exposure/loss of FX loans → bigger UL. Applied in both scenarios.
  • FW-Eigenmittelrisiko (M11, §5.10): the same appreciation raises the RWA base / capital requirement → less free Deckungsmasse. Applied in the Problemfall only (irrelevant in a liquidation).

One hits the asset/loss side, the other the capital-requirement side. Charging both is correct; charging the FW-EM in the Extremfall is not.

⚠ Großkredite monitor ≠ the CRR 25% legal limit

The §5.9 measure relates the aggregated largest exposures to R-IPS CET1 as a sector-level concentration monitor. It is not the CRR Part Four 25%-of-Tier-1 hard limit on a single client/GvK. Citing 25% as the §5.9 threshold, or treating §5.9 as the binding legal cap, is wrong — they are two layers (governance limit vs macro monitor), alongside the HHI granularity add-on inside credit risk.

⚠ Country risk is UL-only and excludes defaults

Länderrisiko takes only UL (no EL term), is computed before collateral, by sovereign-rating PD, and excludes Ratingstufe 5,x (already-defaulted exposures carry no incremental transfer risk). Don't add an EL term, don't net collateral, and remember it may be skipped for direct sovereign investments where the country rating already embeds transfer/convertibility risk.

⚠ The 5% buffer scales the WHOLE stack

It is 5% of the sum of all quantified risks, i.e. a \(\times 1{,}05\) on the total — not a separate fixed line item bolted on independently. Compute it last, on everything else (including macro and FW-EM), or you understate it.

Recap

  • Länderrisiko (§5.3): transfer/convertibility add-on because rating ceilings under-capture sovereign-driven risk; computed like credit risk on internal data before collateral, by sovereign-rating PD, UL-only, defaults (5,x) excluded; may skip for direct sovereign investments if the country rating already embeds it; economic Haftungszuordnung preferred over the political Sitzlandprinzip.
  • Makroökonomisches Risiko (§5.8): credit-only point-in-time overlay; \(PD'=PD\cdot1{,}25\), \(LGD'=LGD\cdot1{,}05\) (from a ~2% GDP decline), rerun the IRB formula, take the difference — covering both EL and UL. Distinct from M5's FX PD-elasticity.
  • Risiko aus Großkrediten (§5.9): GvK-level monitor of the largest customer/FI/Sovereign exposures vs R-IPS CET1, R-IPS members excluded — a sector concentration monitor on top of the CRR 25%-of-Tier-1 legal limit; concentration also via HHI (M5) and limits.
  • 5% buffer (§5.10): both scenarios add 5% of the sum of quantified risks (×1,05) for strategic/reputational/business-model/systemic/model risk; justified because future operating income is excluded from the Extremfall Deckungsmasse; flat % can't distinguish high- from low-strategic-risk banks.
  • Fremdwährungs-Eigenmittelrisiko (§5.10, Problemfall only): FX appreciation → higher Saldo → higher RWA base → higher own-funds requirement → less Deckungsmasse; scale the Corporates/Retail/LRG/CIU FX Risikoposition by the FX vola (or 15%/30% proxy), take 8%. Not applied in the Extremfall. ≠ the FX credit add-on of M5.
🇦🇹 Zusammenfassung (Deutsch)
  • Länderrisiko (§5.3): Aufschlag für Transfer- und Konvertierungsrisiko, da Rating-Obergrenzen das souveräne Risiko nicht voll erfassen; gerechnet wie Kreditrisiko auf internen Daten vor Sicherheiten, mit Staaten-PD, nur unerwarteter Verlust, Ausfälle (5,x) ausgeschlossen; wirtschaftliche Haftungszuordnung vor politischem Sitzlandprinzip.
  • Makroökonomisches Risiko (§5.8): nur auf Kreditrisiko; \(PD'=PD\cdot1{,}25\), \(LGD'=LGD\cdot1{,}05\) (≈2% BIP-Rückgang), IRB-Formel neu rechnen, Differenz — deckt EL und UL.
  • Risiko aus Großkrediten (§5.9): auf GvK-Ebene; größte Forderungen (Kunden, FI, Staaten) im Verhältnis zum R-IPS-CET1 (Kernkapital); R-IPS-Mitglieder ausgenommen. Sektoraler Konzentrationsmonitor zusätzlich zum gesetzlichen 25%-Limit (CRR Teil 4).
  • 5%-Puffer (§5.10): beide Szenarien addieren 5% der quantifizierten Risiken (×1,05) für strategisches/Reputations-/Geschäftsmodell-/systemisches/Modellrisiko; begründet, weil künftige Betriebserträge nicht in der Extremfall-Deckungsmasse enthalten sind.
  • Fremdwährungs-Eigenmittelrisiko (§5.10, nur Problemfall): FW-Aufwertung → höherer Saldo → höhere Bemessungsgrundlage → höheres Eigenmittelerfordernis; FX-Risikoposition skalieren (bzw. 15%/30%-Näherung), 8% davon. Im Extremfall nicht angewandt. Nicht zu verwechseln mit dem FX-Kreditaufschlag aus M5.
