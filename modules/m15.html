Module 15 — Default definition (CRR Art. 178) & LGD estimation (§10.2–10.3)

Every PD, every backtest, every IRB capital number you compute downstream rests on one binary flag: is this exposure in default or not? This module pins down the flag with the precision the supervisor demands — the two Art. 178 triggers, the two-part Erheblichkeitsschwelle (materiality threshold) with its exceptions, the cure (Gesundung) machinery — and then derives the LGD values that, together with the PDs from M5, drive both EL and the IRB unexpected loss.

By the end of this module you can…

  • State the two CRR Art. 178 default triggers and map them to the ÖRS rating codes 5,0 / 5,1 / 5,2.
  • Apply the two-component Erheblichkeitsschwelle correctly — and name every default reason that bypasses it.
  • Run the cure / re-aging two-stage process and say who can be cured (facility vs whole customer) and who cannot cure by "paying again".
  • Reproduce the data-pool LGD estimates and the calibrated default-class LGDs, and connect them to the EL and IRB UL of M5.
  • Contrast the regulatory F-IRB LGD ladder with the ÖRS pooled estimates and explain the FX-on-LGD treatment.

1 · Motivation — why a precise default definition is load-bearing

A rating system is only as good as the event it predicts. The Ausfalldefinition (default definition) is the dependent variable of the entire credit machinery: PDs are calibrated against observed default frequencies, Trennschärfe (discriminatory power — how well the model separates future defaulters from non-defaulters) is measured against the default flag, and backtesting (Validierung) compares predicted PD to realised default rate. If two banks in the R-IPS flag default differently — one counting a €120 retail overdue, another waiting for €600 — their PDs are not comparable, their pooled data-pool is corrupted, and the supervisor cannot trust any of it. The single anchor that prevents this is CRR Art. 178 (Regulation (EU) 575/2013), operationalised identically across all 400-plus Raiffeisenbanken.

▶ One flag, many consumers

The default flag feeds: (1) PD calibration; (2) LGD estimation (living vs defaulted customers are split on exactly this flag — §3 below); (3) EL = PD·LGD·EAD in the Deckungsmasse as excess/shortfall; (4) the IRB UL in M5; (5) NPE/forbearance reporting. A wrong flag propagates into all five. Precision here is not pedantry — it is the consistency that makes a pooled, multi-bank model defensible to the Aufsichtsrat (supervisory board).

2 · Concept — the two Art. 178 triggers and the 5,x mapping

CRR Art. 178(1) defines default by two alternative triggers — either suffices:

  • (a) Unlikeliness to Pay (UtP) — Unwahrscheinlichkeit der vollständigen Rückzahlung: the institution considers it unlikely that the obligor pays its credit obligations in full without recourse by the institution to actions such as realising collateral. This is the qualitative, forward-looking trigger (Art. 178(1)(a)).
  • (b) >90 days past due — the obligor is more than 90 days past due on a material credit obligation (Art. 178(1)(b)). This is the quantitative, mechanical trigger — gated by the materiality threshold of §3.

The ÖRS guide operationalises both triggers into three default rating codes on the master scale ÖRS mapping:

CodeMeaningArt. 178 trigger
5,0>90 days overdue (90-Tage-Überfälligkeit)(b) past-due
5,1all other default reasons (sonstige Ausfallgründe)(a) UtP
5,2insolvency (Insolvenz) or write-off (Abschreibung)(a) UtP, loss-bearing

The split matters because the three codes carry different LGDs (§3.2): 5,0 inherits the class LGD, but 5,1 → 80% and 5,2 → 100% are calibrated to reflect provisioning, not empirical recovery.

3 · Derivation — the materiality threshold, cure, and LGD

3.1 The Erheblichkeitsschwelle (materiality threshold) — BOTH parts required

The >90-days trigger only bites on a material obligation. Per Leitlinie (EU) 2020/978 and CRR Art. 178(2)(d), materiality has two components, and BOTH must be satisfied simultaneously — it is a conjunction, not a choice:

$$ \text{material} \iff \underbrace{\big(\text{overdue} \ge T_{\text{abs}}\big)}_{\text{absolute}} \;\wedge\; \underbrace{\left(\frac{\text{overdue}}{\text{total on-balance exposure}} \ge 1\%\right)}_{\text{relative}} $$
where \(T_{\text{abs}}=\) €100 for retail (Mengengeschäft) / €500 for non-retail regulatory; the relative component is 1% of total on-balance exposures excluding equity positions (Beteiligungen) regulatory. The 90-day clock only starts once the obligation is material; \(\wedge\) is logical AND.

Worked threshold examples from the guide:

  • Two accounts each written off €300 → cumulative €600 > €500 (non-retail absolute) → material → default. Amounts on the same obligor aggregate.
  • A €200 partial write-off in 2017 plus a €400 write-off in 2019 → 2019 cumulative €600 > €500 → default in 2019. Write-offs accumulate over time on the obligor.
⚖ Exceptions — default reasons with NO materiality threshold

Two reasons bypass the Erheblichkeitsschwelle entirely — ≥ €1 suffices:

  • Insolvenz (insolvency / bankruptcy filing) — any balance, however small, triggers default (→ 5,2).
  • Fälligstellung (acceleration / calling the loan due) with an expected economic loss (erwarteter wirtschaftlicher Verlust).

These are UtP events (Art. 178(1)(a)), not past-due events, so the >90-day materiality gate simply does not apply.

3.2 Ausfallende / Cure (Gesundung) — the re-aging two-stage process

Default is not permanent. To cure (gesunden), the guide requires a sustainable improvement over at least 12 months (the general cure period / Mindestbeobachtungszeitraum) and that the customer is again fit for Normalbetreuung (standard servicing, not workout). For past-due defaults the mechanics are the "re-aging" two-stage process:

The re-aging two checks
  • A Wohlverhaltensperiode / probation of ≥ 90 days (RZ 71, EBA/GL/2016/07) must elapse between two checks.
  • At the 2nd check the overdue counter must be ≤ 30 days.
  • If a new default criterion arises within the probation period, the whole process restarts.

Scope of cure differs by segment:

  • Non-retail: you cure the whole customer — all accounts/facilities must satisfy the criteria together (the default attaches at obligor level).
  • Retail (Mengengeschäft): you may cure a single facility (default can attach at facility level).
⚠ Loss-bearing default reasons cannot cure by "paying again"

Where an economic loss has already crystallised — Direktabschreibung, EWB-Auflösung, Forderungsverzicht, Forderungsverkauf — the default cannot be ended simply because the customer resumes payments. The loss is realised; resumed payment does not reverse it. After any valid cure, a new (non-default) rating must be freshly assigned — you do not restore the pre-default grade.

3.3 LGD estimation — the data-pool approach (§10.3)

LGD is the share of exposure lost given default. The guide estimates it with a Daten-Pool-Ansatz (data-pool approach) on RLB (Raiffeisen-Landesbank) data, which separates living (lebende) customers from defaulted (ausgefallene) customers — the split made on exactly the default flag of §2. The reference point is the time of default; the estimate is an economic LGD:

$$ \text{LGD}_{\text{economic}} \;=\; 1 - \frac{\displaystyle\sum_t \frac{\text{Recovery}_t - \text{WorkoutCost}_t}{(1+r)^{t-t_{\text{def}}}}}{\text{EAD}_{t_{\text{def}}}} $$
where all workout cash flows and cost components are discounted back to the time of default \(t_{\text{def}}\); \(r\) = discount rate; the numerator is the present value of net recoveries. "Economic" means costs of the workout are included, not just nominal recovery.

The pooled living-portfolio results (with a conservatism add-on / Konservativitätsaufschlag, applied to ratings 0,5–5,0 and NR for those classes) ÖRS estimates:

Retail unselbständig (employed)55,8%data-pool, living

Every LGD here enters the M5 machinery twice: in the expected loss \(EL = LGD\cdot PD\cdot EAD\) (which sits in the Deckungsmasse as excess/shortfall) and in the IRB unexpected loss \(K=[LGD\cdot PD_{\text{cond}}-LGD\cdot PD]\cdot(\text{maturity adj.})\). LGD scales both terms linearly — so the 45,5/54,4/55,8% pooled choices and the 80/100% default-class calibrations move capital directly.

4 · Worked example

Worked example — materiality of a retail past-due, both must hold

A retail (Mengengeschäft) customer has total on-balance exposure €40.000 and is overdue €350 for 95 days. Test the two materiality components (retail \(T_{\text{abs}}=\)€100):

  • Absolute: €350 > €100 ✓
  • Relative: €350 / €40.000 = 0,875% < 1% ✗

The relative component fails, and both are required (logical AND). Even though the customer is past 90 days, the obligation is not material → NOT a default.

Now raise the overdue to €500 (exposure unchanged at €40.000):

  • Absolute: €500 > €100 ✓
  • Relative: €500 / €40.000 = 1,25% > 1% ✓

Both hold → material → with >90 days past due → default, code 5,0.

Contrast — insolvency, no threshold. A customer files for Insolvenz on a balance of just €50. Insolvency carries no materiality threshold (≥ €1 suffices) → default regardless, code 5,2. The €50 would never satisfy the €100 absolute test, yet the past-due gate is irrelevant — this is a UtP trigger.

5 · In practice — regulatory LGD ladder vs ÖRS pooled estimates

The CRR F-IRB supervisory LGD ladder spans a wide range; the guide overrides it with pooled internal estimates for the classes where it has data, and keeps the regulatory values where it does not:

So the ÖRS Corporate estimate (45,5%) sits essentially on top of the 45% regulatory unsecured anchor regulatory, while the two retail segments (54,4% / 55,8% ÖRS estimates) sit materially above it — the pooled data say retail recoveries are weaker than the flat 45% would suggest.

🟣 The FX-on-LGD effect is handled implicitly

Recall from M5 that an FX loan's euro exposure rises when the foreign currency appreciates, and the uplift is treated as unsecured (collateral value is fixed in domestic terms). The Leitfaden does not add a separate FX adjustment to LGD: the effect is absorbed two ways — (1) the long observation window of the data-pool already spans several FX swings, so realised FX-driven losses are in the pooled estimate; and (2) the unsecured-exposure uplift in the credit-risk add-on mechanically raises the blended LGD on the FX portion. This is an ÖRS proportionality choice — simpler than a parametric FX-LGD model, defensible because of the window length.

6 · Nuances & traps

⚠ BOTH thresholds — not either/or

The single most common error: treating materiality as "absolute OR relative". It is absolute AND relative. A €350 retail overdue on €40.000 clears the €100 absolute test but fails the 1% relative test (0,875%) — so it is not a default. Miss the AND and you over-flag small balances on large exposures.

⚠ Insolvency & Fälligstellung have NO threshold

These are UtP triggers (Art. 178(1)(a)), not past-due triggers, so the Erheblichkeitsschwelle does not gate them — €1 suffices. Applying the €100/€500 absolute test to an insolvency filing is a category error: a €50 insolvency is still a default (5,2).

⚠ Cure: ≥12 months general, but ≥90 days via re-aging — and scope differs

The general sustainability period is ≥12 months, but the past-due re-aging probation (Wohlverhaltensperiode) is ≥90 days between two checks with the counter ≤30 days at the 2nd check; any new default criterion restarts it. Non-retail cures the whole customer (all accounts); retail can cure a single facility. And loss-bearing reasons (Direktabschreibung, EWB-Auflösung, Forderungsverzicht, Forderungsverkauf) cannot cure via "pays again". After cure, assign a new rating.

⚠ 5,1 = 80% / 5,2 = 100% are provisioning-calibrated, not empirical

These default-class LGDs are set so that provisioning is reflected calculatorily — they are not measured recovery rates. Do not "validate" them against realised recoveries as if they were estimates; they are a calibration choice ÖRS choice ensuring EL on defaulted exposures aligns with booked provisions.

Recap

  • CRR Art. 178 default = (a) Unlikeliness to Pay (UtP) or (b) >90 days past due on a material obligation — either suffices.
  • ÖRS mapping: 5,0 = >90 days · 5,1 = other default reasons · 5,2 = insolvency / write-off.
  • Erheblichkeitsschwelle (Leitlinie (EU) 2020/978): absolute €100 retail / €500 non-retail AND relative 1% of on-balance exposures — both required. Insolvenz & Fälligstellung+Verlust have no threshold (≥€1).
  • Cure: ≥12 months sustainable improvement; re-aging two-stage with ≥90-day probation, counter ≤30 days at 2nd check, restart on new criterion. Non-retail = whole customer; retail = single facility. Loss-bearing reasons can't cure by paying again; assign a new rating after cure.
  • LGD (data-pool, living portfolio): Corporate 45,5% · Retail selbst. 54,4% · Retail unselbst. 55,8%; defaults 80% (5,1) / 100% (5,2); other classes 45% F-IRB, covered bonds 12,5%. These feed both EL and the IRB UL of M5.
