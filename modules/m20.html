Module 20 — CRR III: operational risk (SMA), CVA, FRTB market risk & SA-CCR

CRR III imports the residual Basel III/IV "finalisation" packages that the 09/2024 Leitfaden could only gesture at: a single Standardisierter Messansatz (SMA, standardised measurement approach) for operational risk that euthanises BIA, TSA and AMA; a rebuilt Kreditbewertungsanpassung (CVA, credit valuation adjustment) charge wired into FRTB sensitivities; the fundamental review of the trading book (FRTB) for market risk; and SA-CCR for counterparty exposure. This module derives each charge from first principles, works a full Business-Indicator-to-capital computation, shows how all three standardised charges feed the output floor numerator, and tells you exactly which crutch numbers in the Leitfaden — the 0,71/1,33 CVA scalars and the 5%/15% op-risk factors — are now scaffolding to be torn down.

By the end of this module you can…

  • Compute the Geschäftsindikator (Business Indicator, BI) from its three components and map it through the marginal-coefficient buckets to the Business Indicator Component (BIC).
  • Derive the Internal Loss Multiplier (ILM) from the loss component and explain why the EU exercises the discretion to set ILM = 1 — and why the €1bn BI loss-data threshold still matters even then.
  • Decompose the revised CVA framework into BA-CVA (reduced/full) and SA-CVA, give the SCVA per-counterparty mechanics, and explain the SA-CVA permission gating and the retained-but-now-reported exemptions.
  • Lay out the FRTB standardised approach (SBM with max-over-three-correlation-scenarios + DRC + RRAO) and the internal-models machinery (expected shortfall, NMRF, PLA test, backtesting), including the reporting-only-vs-own-funds timing.
  • Reconstruct SA-CCR exposure as \(\alpha\,(RC+PFE)\), distinguish margined from unmargined replacement cost, and say what it replaces and why alpha is 1,4.
  • Explain how SMA, SA-CVA and FRTB-SA all feed the 72,5% output floor numerator, and state which Leitfaden shortcut figures CRR III supersedes.

1 · Motivation — why this exists

The 09/2024 Leitfaden Früherkennung was written to a CRR II rulebook. To turn a few balance-sheet and derivative lines into a plausible capital number for early-warning purposes, it leaned on deliberately crude scalars: a flat 5%/15% applied to gross-income-type bases for operational risk (echoing the old Basisindikatoransatz, BIA, and Standardansatz, TSA), and a 0,71 reduced-factor / 1,33 standardised-factor pair to stand in for the CVA charge on derivatives. Those numbers were never meant to be defensible to the Aufsichtsrat as final regulatory capital — they were forecasting placeholders.

CRR III (Regulation (EU) 2024/1623, generally applying from 1 January 2025) replaces all four placeholders with real, derivable methodologies. The junior risk controller's job shifts from "plug a scalar" to "reconstruct and defend the methodology". This module gives you the four pillars — SMA, CVA, FRTB, SA-CCR — at viva depth, shows how three of them feed the output floor (Modul 21), and at the end maps each old Leitfaden crutch to its CRR III successor.

▶ The unifying idea

Basel III "finalisation" (what the EU calls CRR III, and the press calls Basel IV) does one thing everywhere: it removes modelling freedom that proved unreliable and replaces it with prescribed, sensitivity-aware standardised formulas, while keeping a narrow internal-model lane that must prove itself (P&L attribution, backtesting) and that requires prior supervisory permission. Every charge below is an instance of that pattern.

2 · Concept & logic

2.1 Operational risk: from gross income to a Business Indicator

Operational risk capital used to come from three approaches: BIA (a flat coefficient on gross income), TSA (business-line coefficients) and AMA (internal models). The Basel Committee found AMA non-comparable and the income proxies weak. CRR III collapses all three into the SMA: a single, non-model standardised charge built from a Geschäftsindikator (Business Indicator, BI) — a refined, more loss-predictive size proxy than gross income — scaled by regulatory marginal coefficients, optionally adjusted by an institution's own loss history.

2.2 CVA: pricing the counterparty's spread, not just its default

CVA risk is the risk of mark-to-market loss from changes in counterparty credit spreads (and, in the full versions, the associated market risk factors) on derivatives and securities-financing transactions. Modul 11 covered counterparty default; CVA is the spread-migration cousin. CRR III replaces the CRR II standardised/advanced split with three tiers: a reduced and a full Basic Approach (BA-CVA), and a Standardised Approach (SA-CVA) that reuses FRTB sensitivities — and that, unlike BA-CVA, requires prior supervisory permission.

2.3 FRTB: redrawing the trading/banking-book boundary

FRTB rebuilds market-risk capital. It hardens the Handelsbuch/Bankbuch (trading-book/banking-book) boundary against arbitrage, and offers two routes: a sensitivities-based Standardised Approach (SBM + DRC + RRAO) and an Internal Models Approach (IMA) built on expected shortfall rather than VaR, gated by desk-level tests and supervisory approval.

2.4 SA-CCR: a risk-sensitive replacement for CEM

SA-CCR replaces the Current Exposure Method (CEM, Marktbewertungsmethode) for computing the exposure-at-default (EAD) of derivatives. It splits EAD into a current replacement cost (RC) and a forward-looking potential future exposure (PFE), with netting, collateral and margining handled coherently — and an alpha of 1,4 on top.

3 · Derivation

3.1 The Business Indicator and the BIC

The BI is the sum of three components, each defined to be sign-robust (absolute values, capped ratios) so that hedging or accounting noise cannot suppress the size signal:

$$ \text{BI} = \text{ILDC} + \text{SC} + \text{FC} $$
where \(\text{ILDC}\) is the interest, lease & dividend component; \(\text{SC}\) the services component; \(\text{FC}\) the financial component. Each underlying line is a three-year average.

The interest, lease and dividend component caps the net interest margin so that high-margin (often higher-op-risk) banks are not under-counted:

$$ \text{ILDC} = \min\!\big(\,|\text{II}-\text{IE}|,\ 2{,}25\%\cdot\text{IEA}\,\big) + \text{DI} $$
where \(\text{II}\) = interest income, \(\text{IE}\) = interest expense, \(\text{IEA}\) = interest-earning assets, \(\text{DI}\) = dividend income; the cap coefficient is 2,25%.

The services component uses maxima rather than nets so that outsourcing/insourcing does not erase the indicator. It is the larger of fee & commission income and fee & commission expense, plus the larger of other operating income and other operating expense:

$$ \text{SC} = \max(\text{FCI},\text{FCE}) + \max(\text{OOI},\text{OOE}) $$
where \(\text{FCI}/\text{FCE}\) = fee & commission income/expense, \(\text{OOI}/\text{OOE}\) = other operating income/expense.

The financial component captures trading- and banking-book P&L volatility via absolute values:

$$ \text{FC} = |\text{Net P\&L}_{\text{trading book}}| + |\text{Net P\&L}_{\text{banking book}}| $$
where each term is the absolute three-year-average net profit or loss on the respective book.

The BI is then mapped to the Business Indicator Component (BIC) through a piecewise-linear, marginal-coefficient schedule defined in the CRR III article on the BIC — exactly the mechanics of a progressive tax table. With bucket boundaries at €1bn and €30bn and marginal coefficients 12% / 15% / 18%:

$$ \text{BIC} = 0{,}12\cdot\min(\text{BI},1) + 0{,}15\cdot\big(\min(\text{BI},30)-1\big)^{+} + 0{,}18\cdot\big(\text{BI}-30\big)^{+} $$
where BI is in € billion and \((x)^+=\max(x,0)\). The marginal coefficients and the bucket boundaries are regulatory value set by the CRR III BIC article (transposing Basel d424); cite the article rather than treating them as self-evident, and verify each figure.

3.2 The loss component, the Internal Loss Multiplier and the €1bn threshold

For larger banks Basel layers an institution-specific adjustment. The loss component (LC) is 15× the average annual operational-loss total over a 10-year window. The Internal Loss Multiplier (ILM) compresses the ratio of loss experience to BIC through a logarithm so it cannot explode:

$$ \text{ILM} = \ln\!\Big(e - 1 + \big(\tfrac{\text{LC}}{\text{BIC}}\big)^{0{,}8}\Big) $$
where \(e\approx2{,}718\); the exponent 0,8 and the constant are regulatory value. If \(\text{LC}=\text{BIC}\) then \(\text{ILM}=1\); a worse-than-average loss history pushes ILM above 1, a better-than-average history below 1.

The operational-risk own-funds requirement is then \( \text{ORC} = \text{BIC}\cdot\text{ILM} \), with op-risk RWA = \(12{,}5\cdot\text{ORC}\). The EU discretion is decisive: CRR III lets the EU set regulatory value ILM = 1 for all institutions, so that ORC = BIC and the loss data does not drive the capital number.

⚠ ILM = 1 does not abolish the loss-data obligation

Even where ILM is set to 1, the Basel framework attaches a mandatory loss-data-collection obligation to institutions whose BI crosses the €1bn bucket boundary. Setting ILM = 1 removes loss data from the capital formula; it does not, by itself, remove the duty to collect the data once you are in bucket 2. Conflating "ILM = 1" with "no loss dataset needed" is a viva trap: the BI bucket and the loss-collection trigger are separate switches.

▶ ÖRS modelling decision — relying on the ILM = 1 election

For the Raiffeisen sector the EU's ILM = 1 election is the operative reality: a small Primärbank's ORC is simply its BIC, and below the BI loss-data threshold no 10-year dataset is needed at all. ÖRS modelling decision to rely on this proportionality. The trade-off: you gain enormous simplification and avoid the data-quality minefield of loss collection — but you forfeit any capital relief a clean loss history (LC < BIC ⇒ ILM < 1) might have earned. A bank with genuinely benign op-risk pays the same BIC-driven charge as a sloppy peer of equal size.

3.3 BA-CVA, the SCVA mechanics and the FRTB link

BA-CVA is the default CVA approach: an institution uses it unless it has obtained supervisory permission for SA-CVA. BA-CVA (reduced) aggregates standardised per-counterparty CVA capital with a single-factor correlation \(\rho\) between systematic and idiosyncratic components:

$$ K_{\text{reduced}} = \sqrt{\Big(\rho\textstyle\sum_c \text{SCVA}_c\Big)^2 + (1-\rho^2)\textstyle\sum_c \text{SCVA}_c^{2}} $$
where \(\text{SCVA}_c\) is the standalone CVA capital for counterparty \(c\), and \(\rho=\)50% regulatory value.

The per-counterparty \(\text{SCVA}_c\) is not a hand-wave — it is a supervisory risk weight times a maturity-adjusted, discounted exposure summed over netting sets:

$$ \text{SCVA}_c = \frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot RW_c \cdot \sum_{NS} M_{NS}\cdot \text{EAD}_{NS}\cdot DF_{NS} $$
where \(RW_c\) is the counterparty's supervisory CVA risk weight (by sector/rating); \(M_{NS}\) is the effective maturity of the netting set; \(\text{EAD}_{NS}\) is the SA-CCR exposure; \(DF_{NS}=\dfrac{1-e^{-0{,}05\,M_{NS}}}{0{,}05\,M_{NS}}\) is the supervisory maturity discount factor (it pulls back the full-maturity exposure to a present-value-equivalent); and the leading \(\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\) with \(\alpha=\)1,4 removes the SA-CCR alpha so CVA is not double-scaled by the default-risk add-on. For cleared trades \(DF=1\). These coefficients are regulatory value — verify each.

BA-CVA (full) adds recognition of eligible CVA hedges; SA-CVA is the advanced tier, computing delta and vega capital from FRTB-style sensitivities of CVA to underlying risk factors — the structural bridge to the market-risk framework in §3.4. SA-CVA's reliance on those sensitivities is precisely why it is permission-gated: the supervisor must approve the institution's ability to compute and govern them, just as FRTB-IMA gates desk-level model use.

▶ Exemptions retained — but now you must calculate and report them

The longstanding EU CVA exemptions (transactions with non-financial counterparties below the clearing threshold, certain intragroup trades, and pension-scheme arrangements) are retained under CRR III; the exemption scope was not materially narrowed. What changed is the transparency overlay: institutions must now calculate, report and disclose the CVA own-funds requirement that would apply to the exempted transactions. Characterise it as "retained but now subject to calculation and reporting" — not as "narrowed". Securities-financing transactions and client-cleared trades through a qualifying CCP (QCCP) follow their own exemption rules. regulatory value for the exemption perimeter — verify the article and the clearing-threshold conditions.

3.4 FRTB standardised and internal models

The SBM computes delta, vega and curvature capital within each risk class (GIRR, CSR, equity, commodity, FX). Crucially, the prescribed correlations are stressed under three scenarios — high, medium and low — and the SBM capital is the maximum across the three scenarios, not their sum. To that you add the two non-sensitivity charges:

$$ K_{\text{SBM}} = \max_{s\in\{\text{high},\text{med},\text{low}\}} K_s, \qquad K_{\text{SA}} = K_{\text{SBM}} + K_{\text{DRC}} + K_{\text{RRAO}} $$
where each \(K_s\) aggregates delta + vega + curvature under correlation scenario \(s\); DRC = default risk charge (jump-to-default), RRAO = residual risk add-on (a notional-times-coefficient backstop for exotic/gap risks). Risk weights and correlations are regulatory value.

The max-over-scenarios is the defence point: a senior manager who assumes the three scenarios are additive will overstate the charge by up to a factor of three. Curvature captures the second-order (gamma) risk that delta misses for optionality; vega captures volatility sensitivity. The IMA discards VaR for expected shortfall at 97,5% with liquidity-horizon scaling, isolates non-modellable risk factors (NMRF) into a stressed add-on, and gates model use desk-by-desk through the P&L attribution (PLA) test and backtesting. Fail, and the desk falls back to the SA.

3.5 SA-CCR — and why margining changes the replacement cost

$$ \text{EAD} = \alpha\cdot(\text{RC}+\text{PFE}), \qquad \alpha = 1{,}4 $$
where RC = replacement cost (current, collateral-aware), PFE = potential future exposure = multiplier × Σ asset-class add-ons over hedging sets. \(\alpha=\)1,4 regulatory value, carried over from the IMM to scale for model/correlation risk.

RC is computed differently for unmargined and margined netting sets — a distinction the CEM blurred and a frequent reconstruction error:

$$ \text{RC}_{\text{unmargined}} = \max(V-C,\,0), \qquad \text{RC}_{\text{margined}} = \max\!\big(V-C,\ \text{TH}+\text{MTA}-\text{NICA},\ 0\big) $$
where \(V\) = net MtM of the netting set, \(C\) = net collateral (haircut-adjusted), TH = margin threshold, MTA = minimum transfer amount, NICA = net independent collateral amount. The margined RC recognises that even a fully-margined set can re-accumulate exposure up to TH + MTA before a margin call bites.

The PFE add-on per trade is a supervisory factor \(SF\) (by asset class) times an adjusted notional times a maturity factor \(MF\). For unmargined sets \(MF\) reflects a one-year-capped horizon; for margined sets \(MF\) is driven by the margin period of risk (MPOR) — typically a small number of business days — so margining sharply shrinks the add-on:

$$ MF_{\text{unmargined}} = \sqrt{\tfrac{\min(M,\,1\text{yr})}{1\text{yr}}}, \qquad MF_{\text{margined}} = \tfrac{3}{2}\sqrt{\tfrac{\text{MPOR}}{1\text{yr}}} $$
where \(M\) = remaining maturity; MPOR = margin period of risk (floored by supervisory minima, e.g. 10 business days for daily-margined OTC sets, higher for harder-to-value or large netting sets). Supervisory factors and MPOR floors are regulatory value — verify per asset class.

The PFE multiplier sits in \((0,1]\) and rewards over-collateralisation/negative MtM by shrinking the aggregate add-on:

$$ \text{multiplier} = \min\!\Big(1,\ 0{,}05 + 0{,}95\cdot \exp\!\big(\tfrac{V-C}{2\cdot 0{,}95\cdot \text{AddOn}^{\text{agg}}}\big)\Big) $$
where \(V\) = net MtM, \(C\) = net collateral; floor 5% regulatory value.

4 · Worked example

▶ BI → operational-risk capital for a mid-sized Landesbank

Take a three-year-average institution with: net interest |II−IE| = €0,9bn but capped at 2,25%·IEA where IEA = €30bn ⇒ cap = €0,675bn; dividend income €0,05bn. Services: max(FCI,FCE) = €0,55bn, max(OOI,OOE) = €0,30bn. Financial: |trading-book P&L| = €0,12bn, |banking-book P&L| = €0,08bn.

Step 1 — components. ILDC = min(0,90, 0,675) + 0,05 = 0,675 + 0,05 = €0,725bn. SC = 0,55 + 0,30 = €0,85bn. FC = 0,12 + 0,08 = €0,20bn.

Step 2 — BI. BI = 0,725 + 0,85 + 0,20 = €1,775bn.

Step 3 — BIC (marginal buckets). First €1bn at 12% = 0,120; the slice from €1bn to €1,775bn (= €0,775bn) at 15% = 0,11625; nothing above €30bn. BIC = 0,120 + 0,11625 = €0,23625bn ≈ €236,25m.

Step 4 — ILM. Under the EU election ILM = 1, so ORC = BIC = €236,25m. (Contrast: if loss data gave LC = €0,40bn, then LC/BIC = 0,40/0,23625 = 1,693; raised to 0,8 = 1,693^0,8 ≈ 1,531; ILM = ln(2,718 − 1 + 1,531) = ln(3,249) ≈ 1,178, lifting ORC to ≈ €278,3m — a 17,8% surcharge for a worse-than-average loss record. Note this bank's BI sits in bucket 2, so it is in loss-data-collection territory regardless of the ILM election.)

Step 5 — RWA. Op-risk RWA = 12,5 × 236,25m = €2,953bn. At an 8% minimum that is the €236,25m own-funds requirement, consistent by construction.

5 · In practice (the sector)

For a typical Raiffeisen Primärbank with BI well under €1bn, op-risk capital is simply 12% of BI — a number you can recompute in three lines of R from the FINREP P&L. SA-CCR matters only where the bank runs a derivatives book (often centralised at the Zentralinstitut); CVA and FRTB are concentrated at the sector's market-active entities, while the small banks see them only through their participation/consolidation. The sector's role in Sektorrisiko-Controlling is to recompute these at the consolidated level and reconcile the placeholder forecasts in the Leitfaden against the now-real charges.

▶ All three standardised charges feed the output floor

The single most important capital driver for IRB banks is the output floor (Modul 21): RWA may not fall below 72,5% of the RWA that the standardised approaches would produce. SMA, SA-CVA and FRTB-SA are all standardised charges, so they all sit in the numerator of the floored (standardised) RWA. Mechanically: \(\text{RWA}_{\text{binding}}=\max\!\big(\sum \text{RWA}_{\text{own approaches}},\ 72{,}5\%\cdot\sum \text{RWA}_{\text{standardised}}\big)\). Because the floor is phased in — from 50% at the 2025 start rising to 72,5% by 2030 — an IRB bank's op-risk and market-risk SA charges grow in capital relevance year by year even if its own modelled numbers do not move. regulatory value for the floor level and the phase-in schedule — verify both.

▶ Reporting-only vs own-funds timing for FRTB

FRTB has been live as a reporting requirement (the SA used for COREP market-risk reporting) ahead of its binding own-funds date. CRR III empowers the Commission to set/defer the FRTB own-funds application date by delegated act; the Commission delegated act deferred the FRTB market-risk own-funds application to 1 January 2026, with documented pressure for a further deferral to 2027 — resolve to the actually-adopted date before relying on it. Do not capitalise FRTB at the binding multiplier before that date — but do report it. regulatory value for the timing — verify the adopted delegated act.

6 · Nuances & traps

⚠ The Leitfaden scalars are now wrong, not conservative

The Leitfaden's 0,71/1,33 CVA scalars belonged to the CRR II standardised CVA formula; under CRR III, BA-CVA-reduced computes the charge from per-counterparty SCVA with a 50% correlation, so the old scalars do not map to anything. Likewise the 5%/15% op-risk factors were gross-income proxies (BIA/TSA flavour); SMA replaces the base (BI, not gross income) and the coefficients (12/15/18% marginal). Re-deriving, not rescaling, is the only defensible path.

⚠ Marginal, not flat — and the overstatement grows toward the boundary

BIC coefficients are marginal. A bank with BI = €2bn does not pay 15% on €2bn; it pays 12% on the first €1bn and 15% only on the second, giving €270m rather than €300m — an overstatement of about 11% at BI = €2bn. The flat-coefficient error is worst at the bucket boundary: just above €1bn, applying 15% instead of 12% to (almost) the whole BI overstates the charge by roughly a quarter (15/12 − 1 = 25%). State the overstatement as boundary-dependent, not a fixed "quarter".

⚠ ILM = 1 is an EU election, not a global truth

The Basel text computes ILM from loss data; the EU sets it to 1. If you consolidate or benchmark against a non-EU group, their ORC may carry ILM ≠ 1. Always state which jurisdiction's election you are applying — and remember the loss-collection trigger at the €1bn BI boundary survives the election.

⚠ Don't confuse default and CVA — and don't double-count alpha

SA-CCR feeds EAD into both the counterparty-default RWA (Modul 11) and the CVA charge. They are additive, not alternatives. SA-CCR's alpha (1,4) applies to the EAD used for default risk; within SCVA the leading \(1/\alpha\) removes that same 1,4 so the CVA exposure is not scaled twice.

⚠ FRTB-SBM scenarios are a max, not a sum

The high/medium/low correlation scenarios are aggregated by taking the maximum capital across the three, not by adding them. Summing them inflates the SBM charge threefold and is the most common reconstruction error a defender makes under questioning.

▶ Why it matters for the viva

Senior management will ask "did our capital go up under CRR III?" The honest answer is decompositional: op-risk usually rises modestly for fee-heavy banks (services component bites), SA-CCR can raise derivative EAD versus CEM (and falls for well-margined sets via the MPOR maturity factor), and FRTB raises market-risk capital — but the output floor (Modul 21), phasing toward 72,5%, is what ultimately binds the IRB banks, with SMA/SA-CVA/FRTB-SA all in its numerator. Know which lever moved which charge.

Recap

  • CRR III replaces BIA/TSA/AMA with a single SMA: op-risk capital = BIC × ILM, where BIC maps the Business Indicator through 12%/15%/18% marginal buckets at €1bn and €30bn (all CRR-III/EBA values to verify).
  • The Business Indicator sums ILDC = min(|II−IE|, 2,25%·IEA) + DI; a services component SC = max(FCI,FCE) + max(OOI,OOE); and a financial component FC = |trading-book P&L| + |banking-book P&L| — maxima and absolutes stop hedging from masking size.
  • The ILM compresses 15× average annual losses (LC) versus BIC through a log; the EU sets ILM = 1, so ORC = BIC — but the €1bn BI boundary still triggers a loss-data-collection obligation independent of the election.
  • CVA has three tiers: BA-CVA reduced (ρ = 50% aggregation of per-counterparty SCVA), BA-CVA full (adds eligible hedges), and SA-CVA (FRTB sensitivities, permission-gated). SCVA = (1/α)·RW·Σ M·EAD·DF. Exemptions are retained but now must be calculated and reported.
  • FRTB SBM aggregates delta/vega/curvature and takes the maximum across the high/medium/low correlation scenarios (not the sum), plus DRC + RRAO; the IMA uses expected shortfall at 97,5% with NMRF, gated desk-by-desk by the PLA test and backtesting. FRTB is reporting-live ahead of its binding own-funds date.
  • SA-CCR replaces CEM: EAD = 1,4 × (RC + PFE). RC differs for margined (max(V−C, TH+MTA−NICA, 0)) versus unmargined (max(V−C, 0)) sets, and the maturity factor is MPOR-driven when margined. SMA, SA-CVA and FRTB-SA all feed the 72,5% output floor numerator.
